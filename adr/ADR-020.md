## ADR-020 — Administrative boundary: separate deployable, database role and three audit streams
**Status:** ACCEPTED · 2026-09-30

**Decision.** The administrative surface is two separately deployed processes on administrative
hosts — the `admin` console (Next.js, holds no database credential) and the `admin-api` (a separate
build artefact of the `api` repository) — behind their own ingress and their own identity domain.
The admin API connects to Postgres as a dedicated `admin_app` role that reaches customer data only
through masked views and audited `SECURITY DEFINER` functions in an `admin_api` schema; it holds no
table DML on customer data, no `BYPASSRLS`, and never receives a data encryption key. Every
administrative route passes one default-deny policy evaluation point. Every action is exactly one
transactional write into the foundational audit trail carrying an `audit_event_id`; a relay projects
that write into the chained tamper-evident administrative log (its own store plus a write-once copy)
and into the subject-visible transparency stream (a positive projection with a closed vocabulary).
No stream is dual-written. An unmasked value, an elevated session, an export artefact or a
break-glass session is released only after its chain record is confirmed; otherwise it is refused,
never compensated. Controls are gated by capability, not by calendar phase (§9).

**Ticket.** `T-ADR-ADMIN-06`, PenniLogic/docs#40, parent epic E26 (docs#29). Consumed by `T-ADM-01`
to `T-ADM-06`, `T-ADM-13`, `T-SUP-01`, `T-SEC-03`, `T-PLT-05`, `T-CON-07`, `T-CON-09`, `T-TRU-02`
to `T-TRU-04` and `T-QA-13`. It builds on ADR-002 (append-only records), ADR-005 (default deny;
detail-level access to another person's data is logged and visible to them), ADR-006 (per-user DEKs),
ADR-009 (Kotlin/Ktor core, Postgres) and ADR-014 (`admin` is a separate service on a separate auth
domain). It coordinates by reference with ADR-018 (encryption scope, RLS predicates, key custody),
ADR-019 (customer identity provider, passkeys, recovery), ADR-021 (shared-data erasure) and ADR-022
(runtime platform, AI egress). It restates none of their internals.

### 1. Problem

The brief asks for an administrator who can "control the full application". Built literally, inside
the customer application, that makes every staff account — and every phished staff credential — a
total-compromise vector over other people's salary, debt and spending data, because the customer
process, database role, session cookie domain and deploy pipeline would all be shared. Monetary
columns are deliberately plaintext integers (ADR-001, ADR-002 constraints run in the database), so
any credential that reaches the tables reads every balance. The controls that would contain this are
described inconsistently across the research sources (§2), and three audit streams are named without
a stated relationship, store topology or write semantics. Without this record `T-SEC-03` cannot freeze
the audit event schema and `T-ADM-01` cannot start.

### 2. The inconsistency this record resolves

Four statements disagree about what must exist before the administrative surface is used:

| # | Source | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | `architecture/02-security-architecture.md` §11 "Security Requirements Checklist" | Lists ADMIN-01 (separate subdomain + auth domain), ADMIN-02 (redacted-by-default), ADMIN-03 (JIT elevation with ticket gating) and BACKEND-04 (hash-chained audit log streamed to write-once storage) under **MVP (Pre-Launch)**, and ADMIN-10 (four-eyes approval for bulk exports), ADMIN-11 (break-glass procedures documented and tested), ADMIN-12 (anomaly detection) and REBAC-11 (access transparency log visible to data subjects) under **Post-MVP / Series A** |
| (b) | `compliance/01-regulatory-landscape.md` §0 obligation matrix | O-22 "Administrative access lifecycle, approver eligibility and recertification" and O-23 "Support intake, ticket verification for unmasking" are gated **Before any unmask capability**; O-29 "No standing human production-data access and governed break-glass reads" is gated **Before real data** |
| (c) | `architecture/02-security-architecture.md` §2.2 | Principle 2 requires that "a second admin must approve" every JIT elevation and Principle 6 forbids approving one's own elevation — while §11 (a) ships elevation at MVP without its approver and defers four-eyes to Series A |
| (d) | `product/01-product-spec.md` §5 and `product/02-risk-register.md` R5 | The admin console is Phase 3 "Reach", "not parallel workstreams" — while (a) lists ADMIN-01 to ADMIN-03 as pre-launch MVP |

**Resolution.** Phases are delivery sequencing; controls are conditions of capabilities. This record
replaces the calendar phasing in (a) for everything administrative with the capability gates in §9:
the four-eyes control, the break-glass governance and the administrator lifecycle are conditions of
the capabilities they protect, not later hardening, and those capabilities do not exist until their
conditions do. Statement (b) already uses that framing and is confirmed. Statement (c) is resolved in
favour of §2.2: elevation without a distinct approver never exists. Statement (d) is compatible: the
console may be built in any phase, but whenever it is reachable with real data, Gate A (§9) applies.
The research documents are not edited; their execution-gate banners already defer to this record.

### 3. Deployment topology

#### 3.1 Chosen: separate deployables on administrative hosts

```
operator browser ──mTLS + allowlist──▶ admin console (Next.js, `admin` repo, host: admin zone)
                                             │  administrative contract only (T-CON-07)
                                             ▼
                                       admin-api (Ktor, `api` repo, own artefact, host: admin zone)
                                             │  role admin_app → schema admin_api only
                                             ▼
                              primary Postgres ──── audit.event (trail = outbox) ◀── customer api
                                             │                       │
                                             │                audit relay (platform zone,
                                             │                role audit_relay / chain_writer)
                                             │                       ├─▶ audit_chain DB (own store)
                                             │                       │        └─▶ write-once object copy
                                             │                       └─▶ transparency.entry (+ notify outbox)
                                             │
              redaction service (customer zone, holds KMS unwrap) ◀── internal unmask call by audit_event_id
```

1. **Two administrative processes, separate from every customer process.** The console is the
   `admin` repository's Next.js service (ADR-014; scaffolded deny-only by `T-SCA-ADM-01`,
   PenniLogic/admin#1, whose import boundary already refuses customer web code and whose request
   boundary denies everything until an administrative identity provider is configured). The admin
   API is an `admin-api` module of the `api` repository built into its **own artefact** with its own
   entrypoint and deployment manifest (`T-ADM-01`). The customer API artefact contains no
   administrative route and the administrative artefact contains no customer-facing route; both are
   proven by route-table absence tests (§13).
2. **Separate hosts and network segment.** Administrative processes run on hosts that run no
   customer workload, in an administrative network segment. Ingress is a separate load balancer and
   hostname that accepts only mutually authenticated connections (client certificate) from the
   allowlisted operator network; the edge also rejects the framework-answered request classes the
   admin scaffold documents (`TRACE`, `TRACK`, `CONNECT`, asterisk- and absolute-form targets,
   `Upgrade`). Egress from the admin zone is limited to: the admin API (from the console), the
   primary database `admin_api` schema and the chain database read role (from the admin API), the
   administrative identity provider, the ticket-verification interface, and the internal unmask
   endpoint (§6). There is no route from the admin zone to the customer-facing API, the AI service
   or the customer web application, and none from the customer zone to the admin zone.
3. **No shared process, session or cookie domain.** The administrative origin is a hostname no
   customer surface uses. Administrative session cookies carry the `__Host-` prefix (Secure,
   host-only, no `Domain` attribute) and `SameSite=Strict`, so no customer origin can set, read or
   ride them even if both surfaces sit under one registrable domain; the customer application may
   never set a cookie whose scope covers the administrative host. A separate registrable domain is
   permitted and preferred when available but is not a condition of this decision, so no domain
   purchase is decided here.
4. **Separate workload identities and secrets.** Each administrative process has its own platform
   identity. Administrative secrets are never mounted into customer workloads and vice versa. The
   administrative identities hold no KMS permission over customer key-encryption keys (§6.2).
5. **Runtime-neutral.** Which platform provides these hosts is ADR-022's runtime question. Any
   runtime chosen there must satisfy: no co-scheduling of administrative and customer workloads on
   one host; separately addressable network segments with default-deny rules between them; distinct
   workload identities; a separate deployment stage for the administrative artefacts; the chain
   store and its write-once copy in an identity boundary separate from both zones (§8.4); and data
   residency for logs as required by the counsel-reviewed jurisdiction profile (`T-CMP-04`; the
   research baseline is India-region storage for Indian users' logs).

#### 3.2 Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Administrative routes inside the customer API and web app**, guarded by a role claim | One remote-code-execution, SSRF, template or dependency flaw in the customer surface reaches administrative capability; the customer database role (or a superuser added "for admin") is shared, so a BOLA bug becomes a cross-tenant read; the session cookie domain and deploy pipeline are shared; the surface cannot be network-restricted without restricting customers; and the audit writer lives in the blast radius it audits. It is the total-compromise topology §1 describes |
| **Same artefact, separate host** (an `ADMIN_MODE` flag) | Administrative routes ship dormant in every customer instance; a misconfigured customer instance becomes an administrative one; route-table absence cannot be proven |
| **Console with direct database access** (a read-only replica credential in the Next.js server) | Monetary columns are plaintext integers by design, so "read-only" is a full financial read that bypasses redaction, the policy point and the audit write; forbidden outright (§4.1) |
| **Administrative API in the `admin` repository** (Next.js route handlers holding database credentials) | The console would hold production credentials; redaction and the `SECURITY DEFINER` functions belong beside the domain model in `api` |
| **Shared identity realm with an `is_admin` claim** | Customer recovery paths become administrative account-takeover paths; token replay across audiences; one phished customer-grade credential is an administrator (§7) |
| **Remote policy-decision service** in the request path | Adds an availability dependency in the deny path; unreachable means fail closed means outage. Policies are a versioned bundle evaluated in-process (§5) |

### 4. Database role model

#### 4.1 Roles and grants

| Role | Kind | Holder | Grants |
|---|---|---|---|
| `admin_app` | LOGIN, `NOSUPERUSER NOCREATEDB NOCREATEROLE NOINHERIT NOBYPASSRLS` | the admin API process only | `CONNECT` on the primary database; `USAGE` on schema `admin_api`; `SELECT` on `admin_api` views; `EXECUTE` on `admin_api` functions. **Nothing else**: no privilege on any customer schema, on `audit`, on `transparency`, on the wrapped-key tables ADR-018 names, or on any sequence |
| `admin_api_owner` | NOLOGIN | nobody; owns `admin_api` objects | Column-level `SELECT` on the non-financial columns the masked views expose; `INSERT`/`UPDATE` only on the exact columns administrative effects change (for example an account status column, quota-override rows, plan rows); `INSERT` on `audit.event` in the `admin.*` namespace; `EXECUTE` on `audit.confirmation_status(uuid)` and on the administrative access-context setter (§4.3); **no** `EXECUTE` on the customer-subject context setter; no `DELETE` anywhere; no privilege on monetary, ciphertext or key columns. It owns no customer table and is `NOBYPASSRLS`; every customer table it reads has `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` |
| `pennilogic_app` | LOGIN | the customer API (name from the security research; ADR-018 fixes its RLS context) | Unchanged by this record except: `INSERT` on `audit.event`, `SELECT` on `transparency.entry` under RLS, and **no** `SELECT` on `audit.event` |
| `audit_owner` | NOLOGIN | nobody; owns `audit.*` and `transparency.*` | Tables are `INSERT`-only for every login role; a trigger raises on `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE` as defence in depth; retention is by partition archival after the write-once copy is verified (§8.7), never by row deletion |
| `redaction_service` | LOGIN | the redaction service (customer zone; the only identity with KMS unwrap permission, §6.2) | `EXECUTE` on `audit.unmask_grant(uuid)` and on the ADR-018 §4.4 trusted context setter; `SELECT` on the ciphertext and monetary columns of user-scoped tables **only** under the row-level policy keyed to a recorded, chain-confirmed unmask grant; `INSERT` on `audit.event` in the `redaction.*` namespace |
| `audit_relay` | LOGIN | the relay process (platform zone) | `SELECT` on `audit.event`; `INSERT` on `audit.event` in the `audit.*` namespace (chain confirmations); `INSERT` on `transparency.entry` and the notification outbox; `INSERT`/`UPDATE` on its own cursor table only |
| `chain_writer` | LOGIN in the separate `audit_chain` database | the relay process | `INSERT` on `chain.record`; nothing else |
| `chain_reader` | LOGIN in `audit_chain` | the admin API, for the policy-gated audit-review capability only (`T-ADM-11`) | `SELECT` on `chain.record` and `chain.checkpoint`; nothing else |
| `chain_verifier` | LOGIN in `audit_chain` and in the primary cluster | the verification job (infrastructure zone) | `SELECT` on `chain.*` and on `audit.event` for two-way reconciliation; `INSERT` on `chain.checkpoint`; read and checkpoint-write on the write-once copy; sole use of the checkpoint signing key |

Rules: no login role used by any process is a superuser, a table owner or `BYPASSRLS`; the console
holds no database credential at all; **no production database access from the console** — the only
path from an operator to a row is console → admin API → `admin_api` view or function. `audit.event`
is written only through an insert function that **binds each action namespace to exactly one calling
role** — `admin.*` to `admin_api_owner`, `redaction.*` to `redaction_service`, `audit.*` to
`audit_relay`, `broker.*` to the `T-PLT-05` broker identity, customer namespaces to `pennilogic_app`
— so no process can write another process's events; in particular the administrative process cannot
forge a chain confirmation or a delivery record. Direct human access to the primary database, backups,
object stores and keys is governed by `T-PLT-05` (empty standing access, brokered, second-approved,
time-boxed sessions that write into the same streams with the same `audit_event_id`). Migrations,
including partition detachment, run under a separate migration role, serialized, one at a time, under
the same governance.

#### 4.2 What "masked by default" means at the database

Masked views omit monetary and ciphertext columns rather than transforming them: an `admin_api` view
cannot select a balance, an amount, a merchant ciphertext or an account reference. The administrative
contract (`T-CON-07`) therefore has no field in which an unmasked financial value could travel by
default; unmasking is a distinct operation (§6). Aggregate reads for analytics (`T-ADM-10`) exist only
as pre-defined `admin_api` functions that return cohort statistics with a minimum cohort size (§16) and
never a per-subject row; they accept no ad-hoc filter, and each function is reviewed for differencing
risk before it is added.

#### 4.3 Rule for cross-tenant administrative reads

Every administrative read is cross-tenant. It is permitted only in these forms:

1. **Masked single-subject lookup**: one subject identified by an account identifier or by the
   subject's own support request; fields limited to the operator's role by policy; through an
   `admin_api` view; never a search or listing over financial fields. The console structurally has
   no route that lists an individual's transactions or balances (`T-ADM-04`).
2. **Unmasked read** of named fields of one subject, only through the approved unmask path (§6), with
   an eligible access basis, a recorded purpose, a distinct approver, a time-box and chain-before-
   plaintext.
3. **Aggregate read** through a pre-defined cohort function with the published minimum cohort size.
4. **Bulk export** never through the console; only a formally requested, dual-approved export job
   (`T-ADM-05`) whose output lands in a governed location and is itself recorded and subject-visible.

Cross-tenant reads happen inside a `SECURITY DEFINER` function owned by `admin_api_owner` that first
inserts the foundational event (§8.1) and only then performs the read, so a read without its record is
impossible. The row-level policy admitting the function's rows is keyed to that recorded access via
the trusted session context ADR-018 §4.4 defines; no `BYPASSRLS`, no owner bypass, no superuser
anywhere in the path, and `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` on every table read — the administrative roles
are subject to ADR-018 §4.4 in full. ADR-018 owns the predicate and setter form; this record owns that
the admin path uses them. Administrative functions **never set the customer-subject context** — an
administrative role that could assume `app.current_user_id` would be impersonation, not
administration — they set a distinct administrative access context whose only accepted value is the
`audit_event_id` of the event the same transaction has just inserted, and the policies ADR-018 §4.4
defines admit administrative rows only under that context; the customer setter is not executable by
any administrative role (§4.1). `SECURITY DEFINER` does not bypass row-level security here — the
research pitfall applies to a definer that owns the table or holds `BYPASSRLS`, and `admin_api_owner`
is neither: it defines no customer table and is not the table owner whose privileges row-level
security would exempt. Every definer function sets an explicit `search_path`, has `EXECUTE` revoked
from `PUBLIC` and granted only to `admin_app`, takes typed parameters and builds no SQL from input; a
lint over the catalogue enforces this (§13).

#### 4.4 What every administrative read or effect must record

In the foundational event (and therefore in the chain): `audit_event_id`; operator principal id and
administrative session id; action (closed vocabulary, §10); subject id (null for system-level
actions); resource type and id; data classes or column groups read or changed (never the values);
whether the read was masked or unmasked; access-basis reference and its provenance class; purpose
code; approver principal id when one is required; policy bundle version and decision; time-box
bounds; the operator's network origin and client-certificate identity; outcome and reason code; row or
record count. Never a monetary value, a merchant string, raw message content, a credential or key
material — enforced by a schema test with planted values (`T-SEC-03`).

### 5. Policy evaluation point

1. **One point, in-process, default deny.** The admin API has a single middleware through which every
   route — including health, static and framework routes — passes before any handler. The decision is
   computed from a policy bundle versioned in the `api` repository and deployed with the artefact; a
   policy change is a reviewed deploy, not a runtime edit. A route with no explicit allow rule is
   denied; a route added without a decision fails a coverage check and the build; an evaluation error
   or timeout denies (fail secure).
2. **Decision inputs**: operator principal, role and current elevation; hardware-factor freshness;
   session age and idle time; action and resource; subject; access-basis reference with provenance
   class and its verification result; purpose code; approver identity and assertion; policy bundle
   version; chain health (§8.4); the operator's rolling distinct-subject counter. Every decision,
   allow or deny, is a foundational event.
3. **Explicit allow rules** grant the smallest scope that a named capability needs. Deterministic
   caps are policy, not analytics: an operator who exceeds the published distinct-subject cap within
   its rolling window is refused, not flagged, however many sessions they open (§16).
4. **Elevation** is time-boxed to the published maximum, requires a distinct approver who satisfies
   separation of duties with both the requester and the access-basis originator, expires mid-session,
   and issues no elevated session until its chain record is confirmed. Self-approval is impossible by
   construction (the approver field cannot equal the requester or the originator). An approval is a
   **hardware-factor assertion by the approver over the request's `audit_event_id`**, stored in the
   approval event; the process that releases the protected thing verifies that signature against the
   approver's credential as registered in the administrative identity domain (§7) — a store the admin
   API cannot write — so a compromised admin API cannot manufacture a second pair of eyes.
5. **Bulk export** is a formally requested job with two such approvals, executed by an export
   identity that is neither the admin API nor the relay, verifies both assertions, obtains plaintext
   only through the redaction service under the export's own chain-confirmed grant, subject by
   subject (it holds no key permission, §6.2), writes its output only to a governed location, and
   records one event per included subject (§8.2).
6. **Break-glass** is an administrative principal whose credential is vaulted and used only with dual
   presence. Its allow rules cover **system control only** — kill switches over customer-facing
   features and AI providers, session revocation, account suspension — and **never** unmask, export
   or lifecycle grants; no allow rule, break-glass or otherwise, targets the audit trail, the relay,
   the chain, the verifier or the policy bundle itself. Every use is time-boxed to the published
   maximum, fully recorded with `break_glass = true`, alerts on start, rotates the credential
   automatically after the session and requires a recorded post-use review. Emergency human reads of
   production data are `T-PLT-05` broker sessions, not console break-glass. If approval
   infrastructure is unavailable the answer for an unmask is to wait: statutory clocks run in days,
   and ticket-system unavailability denies (`T-ADM-03`). If the audit path itself is down, the console
   offers nothing (§8.4 item 4); platform incident response runs through the deploy and rollback path
   (`T-PLT-01`) under `T-PLT-05` governance, never through the console.
7. **Administrative effects** (suspension, refund, quota override, plan or provider configuration,
   lifecycle changes) execute as `admin_api` functions that write the effect and its event in one
   transaction; the console's confirmation primitive records intended before/after values through the
   API before the change applies (`T-ADM-04`). Before/after values enter the event only for
   non-financial configuration (status, plan and feature rows); a money-bearing effect such as a
   refund or quota override records the reference to the billing or ledger record it created — which
   is itself append-only and server-authoritative — never the amount (§4.4). The admin API never
   calls the customer-facing API and never uses `pennilogic_app`.

### 6. Unmask path and key custody

#### 6.1 Only the approved path produces an unredacted value

Unredacted values never appear by default and are never a property of a role. An unmask is a distinct
operation (`T-CON-07`) that requires, all at once: an **eligible access basis** — a user-originated
support request verified open for that subject by the ticket-verification interface (`T-SUP-01`), a
system-originated security incident record, or a statutory request record — with immutable
originator, source channel and provenance class, where an **operator-originated record is never
eligible** regardless of reassignment or reopening; a **purpose code** from the published vocabulary;
a **distinct approver** eligible under the administrator lifecycle (`T-ADM-06`) who is neither the
requester nor the originator and whose approval is a hardware-factor assertion over the request
(§5 item 4); a **field-level scope** (subject, named fields, time range); a **time-box** with single
delivery; a **fresh hardware-factor assertion** by the requester; and **chain confirmation** before
release (§8.4).
The affected subject is notified through the notification outbox under its payload allowlist, and the
unmask appears in their transparency stream without operator identity (§8.6).

#### 6.2 Administrators never receive raw data encryption keys — enforcement

1. **Database grants**: `admin_app` and `admin_api_owner` hold no privilege on the wrapped-DEK
   tables ADR-018 names, and no `admin_api` view or function selects a ciphertext or key column.
2. **Key-management policy**: the administrative workload identities hold no decrypt or unwrap
   permission on any customer key-encryption key. Only the redaction service's identity in the
   customer zone does (custody per ADR-018 §4.4). This is verified by an IAM policy test in every
   environment.
3. **The redaction service is the only read path.** After the authorizing event is recorded and its
   chain record confirmed, the admin API calls the internal unmask endpoint of the redaction service
   over mTLS with the `audit_event_id` alone. The service calls `audit.unmask_grant(audit_event_id)`,
   which returns the grant only if the request event has outcome `allow`, an approval event from a
   distinct principal whose hardware-factor assertion verifies, a relay-written
   `audit.chain_confirmed` event, an unexpired time-box and no prior `redaction.delivered` event; it
   then unwraps the subject's DEK in its own process, decrypts **only the named fields of the named
   record**, returns their plaintext, and writes a `redaction.delivered` event with the same
   correlation. The admin API receives field values, never a key, never a whole row, never more than
   the approved scope, never twice for one grant.
4. **Code boundary**: the admin console's import boundary refuses every PenniLogic package
   (`T-SCA-ADM-01`); the `admin-api` module has a static assertion that it imports no key-unwrap
   client and a runtime assertion that no administrative path unwraps a key (`T-ADM-03`).
5. **Break-glass cannot unmask** (§5 item 6), so no emergency mode widens key access.

### 7. Administrative identity domain

The administrative identity domain is a **separate realm, tenant or instance** of whatever identity
provider ADR-019 selects for customers — or a different provider — with these properties, none of
which ADR-019 may relax: its own issuer and audience, relying-party identifier, user store, session
store, credential store and recovery process; no federation with the customer realm; enrolment only
of organisational identities through the joiner flow (`T-ADM-06`), which requires a **device-bound,
phishing-resistant FIDO2 credential with user verification** before the first privileged action;
synced or cloud-backed passkeys are not accepted for administrative identities; **no customer
credential of any kind** (passkey, OTP, password, recovery code) is accepted, and the customer realm
accepts no administrative credential — the admin API validates only the administrative issuer and
audience, the customer API rejects them. An administrative principal is a distinct identifier
namespace with no link to a customer id, bound to exactly one natural person who holds at most one
active administrative principal (enforced at enrolment, `T-ADM-06`), so one person cannot be both
requester and approver under two administrative names; an operator who is also a customer holds two
unrelated principals, and the administrative domain never reads or writes the customer identity
store.
Administrative sessions have the published idle and absolute limits, are revocable mid-session, and
every unmask, elevation, export and lifecycle action requires a fresh hardware-factor assertion.
Mover and leaver flows revoke sessions, refresh families, database role membership, console access
and break-glass eligibility with evidence; standing permissions lapse automatically when not
recertified within the published window; an approval by an ineligible or offboarded principal is
refused (`T-ADM-06`).

### 8. The three audit streams

#### 8.1 Purpose, reader, store

| Stream | Purpose | Written by | Read by | Store | Write |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundational application audit trail** (`audit.event`, `T-SEC-03`) | System of record for the existence, order and content of every security-, consent-, sharing- and administration-relevant action; the transactional outbox for the other two streams | The action's own transaction, by whichever role performs it (`pennilogic_app`, `admin_api_owner` functions, broker sessions) | Security and compliance reviewers through governed queries; the relay; **never** a subject-facing endpoint directly (§8.6) | Primary Postgres cluster, dedicated `audit` schema, `INSERT`-only, partitioned by time | **Single authoritative write**, atomic with the action it describes; an action without its event rolls back and vice versa |
| **Chained tamper-evident administrative log** (`chain.record`, `T-ADM-02`) | Unfalsifiable record of every administrative, break-glass and broker action for insider-risk investigation, dispute and regulator evidence | The relay, as `chain_writer`, appending a hash-chained record per administrative event | Security reviewers, the DPO, the audit-review console screen (`T-ADM-11`) in masked form; the verification job with independent credentials; administrators can read, never write or delete | **Own store**: the `audit_chain` database in an identity boundary separate from both zones, continuously streamed to a **write-once object copy** under a platform-enforced retention lock | **Projection** of the trail: at-least-once, idempotent by `audit_event_id`, strictly ordered, single appender |
| **Subject-visible transparency stream** (`transparency.entry`, `T-CON-09`) | Shows a data subject what happened to their data and account: what was accessed or changed, by which class of actor, under which purpose, when — and, for household members, by whom (ADR-005) | The relay, as `audit_relay`, by a **positive projection** into a closed schema | The subject only, under RLS, through the transparency payload contract on Android, web and admin rights-request surfaces (`T-TRU-02`, `T-TRU-03`, `T-TRU-04`, `T-ADM-13`) | Primary cluster, `transparency` schema, `INSERT`-only, RLS `subject_id = current subject`; **shares the cluster** with the trail because it is derived and rebuildable from it, and holds nothing the trail does not | **Projection** of the trail, same relay, with the subject notification enqueued in the same transaction as the entry |

The chain covers every event whose actor class is `operator`, `break_glass_operator` or
`production_broker`, and every `admin.*` action performed by the system on an administrator's behalf
(export execution, recertification lapse, policy bundle deploy). Customer-actor events stay in the
trail and, where subject-visible, the transparency stream.

#### 8.2 Events that appear in more than one stream

| Event class | Trail | Chain | Transparency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unmasked read of a subject's fields (console unmask, broker session read) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ as `unmasked_read`, actor class `operator`, no operator identity |
| Account-affecting administrative action on a subject (suspend, reinstate, refund, quota override, rights-request fulfilment, erasure execution) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ as `account_action` |
| Export job containing a subject's data | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ as `export`, one entry per included subject |
| Masked single-subject lookup | ✓ | ✓ | — (not detail-level; ADR-005's visibility rule attaches to detail access) |
| Policy decision (allow or deny), elevation request/approval/expiry, lifecycle, break-glass session, configuration change with no subject | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Household member's detail-level read of shared data | ✓ | — | ✓ as `shared_detail_read`, actor class `household_member` **with** the member's display identity (ADR-005) |
| Subject's own authentication, consent and sharing-grant actions | ✓ | — | ✓ as `self`, per the published vocabulary |

The transparency projection is the **only** subject-visible read path; the "events about themselves"
query in `T-SEC-03` is served from `transparency.entry`, never from `audit.event`, because the trail
carries operator identity and internal reason codes.

#### 8.3 Correlation identifier

The identifier that correlates one administrative action across all three streams is
**`audit_event_id`**: a UUID version 7 (RFC 9562, time-ordered) minted once, server-side, by the
foundational audit module inside the action's transaction. It is the primary key of `audit.event`,
carried verbatim as `audit_event_id` in `chain.record`, in `transparency.entry`, in every response
of the administrative contract (`T-CON-07`), in the transparency payload (`T-CON-09`), in the broker
session schema (`T-PLT-05`) and in the unmask call (§6.2). A database write into any stream without it
is rejected. Approvals reference the request they approve through `related_event_id` (trail and chain
only). Because the identifier is opaque and the chain has no subject-facing read path, a subject can
quote it in a grievance and the DPO can locate the administrative record without the record ever being
exposed to the subject.

#### 8.4 Write semantics and failure behaviour

1. **No dual write anywhere.** The only write performed inside an action's transaction is the
   foundational event. Neither the chain nor the transparency store is written by the acting
   process. A two-store commit would need two-phase commit or produce phantom or missing records on
   partial failure; both are rejected.
2. **Outbox and relay.** `audit.event` is the outbox. The relay tails it with a gap-safe cursor (it
   must be proven not to skip an event whose transaction commits after a higher sequence value),
   appends the chain record, streams it to the write-once copy, writes the `audit.chain_confirmed`
   event, writes the transparency entry and enqueues the subject notification, and only then advances
   its cursor. Delivery is at-least-once; every projection is idempotent by `audit_event_id`;
   projection failure or lag **never loses an event** because the event is durable in the trail.
3. **Chain-before-release.** The one place a second store's state matters is release of something
   sensitive. An unmasked value, an elevated session token, an export artefact and a break-glass
   session are released only after the relay confirms the chain record of their authorizing event.
   Confirmation is itself a trail event — `audit.chain_confirmed`, with `related_event_id` pointing
   at the authorizing event — that only `audit_relay` can write (§4.1); `audit.*` events are not
   chain-scoped, so confirmation does not recurse. The acting process observes it through a status
   function and never through chain credentials of its own. If confirmation does not
   arrive within the published bound, the release is **refused**: a `*.refused` event is written with
   the same correlation, nothing is compensated because nothing was released, and the operator is told
   to retry later. A masked lookup or configuration change is not held on projection, because its
   event is already durable and it releases no protected value.
4. **Health precondition.** While projection lag exceeds its bound or the last chain verification is
   not green within its interval, the policy point denies unmask, elevation, export and break-glass
   requests outright (fail closed) and continues to allow masked reads and system control.
5. **Authority.** The trail is authoritative for whether an action happened and what it recorded. The
   chain and its write-once copy are authoritative in an **integrity dispute** about an
   administrative action: where trail and chain disagree, the write-once copy is presumed correct for
   the investigation and the disagreement is itself a security incident. The transparency stream is
   never authoritative and is rebuildable from the trail by re-running the projection.

#### 8.5 Chain verification

Each `chain.record` carries `seq`, `audit_event_id`, the canonical event bytes (RFC 8785 JSON
canonicalization of the chain-scoped fields, which exclude values by construction), `prev_hash` and
`record_hash = SHA-256(prev_hash ‖ canonical_bytes)`; the first record's `prev_hash` is 32 zero bytes.
A single appender keeps the chain linear. At the published checkpoint interval the verification job
signs the current head (`seq`, `record_hash`, time) with a key only its identity holds and writes the
checkpoint to the write-once copy and to `chain.checkpoint`. Verification recomputes every hash from
the last verified checkpoint, compares the chain database with the write-once copy record by record,
reconciles chain records against trail events by `audit_event_id` in both directions (a chain record
without a trail event is a forged projection; a chain-scoped trail event without a chain record after
the lag bound is a lost or suppressed projection), and publishes the result, chain length and last
verified position; any mismatch alerts within the published interval.
Administrators cannot delete from the chain: `chain_writer` has `INSERT` only, no login role has
`UPDATE`/`DELETE`, the write-once copy is under a retention lock that the platform enforces against
every principal including the account owner, and the verifier's credentials are held by neither the
admin process nor the relay. A legitimate discontinuity (a restore) is recorded as an explicit
`chain.discontinuity` record referencing the approved change record and is accepted only when a signed
checkpoint precedes it; anything else is tampering.

#### 8.6 Transparency isolation

The transparency stream is built by a **positive projection**: an explicit mapping from named
foundational fields into the closed transparency schema (`T-CON-09`). It is never produced by filtering
the chain or the trail with a deny-list, so a new event field cannot leak by default. It carries
`audit_event_id`, `occurred_at`, `actor_class`, `access_kind`, `data_classes`, `purpose_code` and, for
`household_member` only, `actor_ref`. It never carries an operator, break-glass or broker identity, a
ticket or incident reference, an internal reason code, a network address, a policy version, a row
count, a value or key material; `operator`, `break_glass_operator` and `production_broker` all
project to `operator`. No subject-facing service has any read path to `chain.record`, so the chained
log cannot be reader-filtered into the transparency stream by accident, and a schema test with planted
operator identity, ticket reference and reason code fails the build. The entry is **always written**;
the one thing that may be deferred is its visibility: a `statutory_request` basis may carry a
counsel-recorded notification hold from the jurisdiction profile (`T-CMP-04`), in which case the
entry and its notification become visible when the hold expires, without further action. The hold is
itself a chained `admin.*` event with an expiry and can neither be open-ended nor originate from the
requesting operator.

#### 8.7 Retention and readers

Retention values are read from the counsel-reviewed jurisdiction profile (`T-CMP-04`), not from this
record. The rule this record fixes: the chain and its write-once copy are retained for **the greater
of** the profile's security-log floor and its financial-action floor (research baselines 180 days and
seven years); the write-once lock equals that period; the trail's live partitions keep at least the
security-log floor and are archived to the write-once copy — and the archive verified — before any
partition is detached; transparency entries live as long as the subject's account plus the profile's
floor. Erasure (ADR-006, ADR-021) never touches these streams: they hold identifiers and vocabulary
codes, not personal content; post-retention pseudonymisation of subject identifiers, if the profile
requires it, is a `T-CMP-01` job on archived partitions, never on the chain.

Until Gate B is met, the four privileged capabilities are **structurally absent** — no route, no
allow rule, no vaulted credential — not present behind a disabled flag. A Gate B ticket therefore
never ships a capability without its control, which is what dissolves the MVP-versus-later dispute in
§2: four-eyes and break-glass governance are not "Series A hardening" of an existing capability; they
are what makes the capability exist at all.

### 11. Security and privacy consequences

**Of the chosen option.** A compromised customer surface reaches no administrative capability, and a
compromised administrative account reaches masked metadata of one subject at a time, with every step
recorded in a store it cannot alter; consistent falsification of an administrative action requires
compromising the primary cluster, the separate chain database and a retention-locked object store
under a third identity, plus the verifier's signing key. Subjects learn of every detail-level access
without learning who the operator was. Cost: two extra deployables, one extra database, a write-once
store and a relay to operate; unmask and elevation carry a bounded wait for chain confirmation; when
projection is unhealthy, support work slows down by design; there is no emergency path to unmask, so
some incidents wait for approvers. Residual risks: a colluding requester, originator and approver (three
distinct eligible principals, all recorded and subject-visible); a compromised relay, which can stall
(detected by the health bound, which then denies every sensitive capability) or forge chain records
(detected by two-way reconciliation against the trail) but cannot alter the trail or the write-once
copy; a platform-level compromise of the retention lock (outside this record's control; detected by
verification divergence, not prevented); metadata in the trail (subject ids, action codes) remains
readable to governed reviewers and is covered by `T-PLT-05` rather than by encryption; and fixed
cohort functions still carry differencing risk, reviewed per function.

**Of the rejected options.** In-application administration makes one customer-side flaw a full
cross-tenant financial read and every staff credential a total compromise. Direct console database
access reads plaintext balances past every control. A shared identity realm turns customer recovery
into administrative takeover. Dual writes lose or invent chain records on partial failure and force
the acting process to hold chain credentials. Deny-list filtering leaks new fields to subjects by
default. A remote policy service converts its outage into either an outage or an open door.

### 12. Observability

Signals that prove the decision is honoured, published by the tickets named: policy decisions per
route and outcome with a deny-spike alert (`T-ADM-01`); scheduled role and grant inventory —
superuser, `BYPASSRLS`, table-owner and standing human grants for any process or human role must be
zero, plus a diff of `admin_app` privileges against §4.1 (`T-PLT-05`); edge rejections by cause
(certificate, allowlist); cross-domain authentication attempts (should be zero); outbox lag in seconds
and oldest unprojected event age with an alert at the published bound; chain length, last verified

### 13. Tests implementation tickets must add

1. Route-table absence tests: the customer artefact exposes no administrative route, the
   administrative artefact exposes no customer-facing route, and the console has no per-subject
   financial listing route (`T-ADM-01`, `T-ADM-04`).
2. Direct database tests as `admin_app`: `SELECT` on any customer table, on `audit.event`, on the
   wrapped-key tables and on monetary or ciphertext columns fails; `UPDATE`/`DELETE`/`TRUNCATE` on any
   audit or chain table fails for every login role; `chain_reader` cannot insert; no process role is
   superuser, owner or `BYPASSRLS`; a user-scoped table without `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` fails the
   inventory; a namespace-forgery test proves `admin_api_owner` cannot insert an `audit.*` or
   `redaction.*` event; a catalogue lint proves every definer function has a fixed `search_path` and
   no `PUBLIC` execute (`T-ADM-01`, `T-PLT-05`, `T-SEC-03`).
3. Policy coverage test with a planted uncovered route; fail-secure test where evaluation throws and
   the request is denied; rolling distinct-subject cap test across several sessions; impersonation
   test proving no administrative role can execute the customer-subject context setter or read a
   customer row under a customer context (`T-ADM-01`, `T-ADM-05`).
4. Cross-domain credential tests in both directions; administrative cookie test asserting the
   `__Host-` prefix and no `Domain` attribute; token audience and issuer rejection tests (`T-ADM-01`,
   `T-ADM-04`).
5. Transactional coupling: an action whose event insert fails rolls back, and an event cannot be
   inserted outside an action's transaction; a write without `audit_event_id` is rejected (`T-SEC-03`).
6. Relay tests: out-of-order commit is not skipped; duplicate delivery yields one chain record and one
   transparency entry; ordering per chain is strict (`T-SEC-03`, `T-ADM-02`).
7. Chain tests: tampering with any stored record breaks verification and alerts within the interval;
   deletion from the write-once copy fails for every principal including the administrative and relay
   identities; restore discontinuity without a preceding signed checkpoint is reported as tampering
   (`T-ADM-02`).
8. Release-gate tests: with the relay stalled, an unmask, elevation, export and break-glass request is
   refused within the bound, a `*.refused` event exists, and no plaintext, token or artefact was
   released; with verification not green, the policy point denies those four and allows masked reads
   (`T-ADM-03`, `T-ADM-05`).
9. Unmask tests: closed, invalid, operator-originated, self-approved, forged-provenance and
   unreachable access bases deny; requester cannot be approver or originator; an approval event
   whose hardware-factor assertion does not verify, or was signed over a different `audit_event_id`,
   is refused by the redaction service even when the admin API presents it; a second delivery for one
   grant is refused; the redaction service returns only the named fields of the named record; static
   and runtime assertion that no administrative path unwraps a key; IAM test that administrative
   identities cannot decrypt (`T-ADM-03`, `T-SUP-01`).
10. Break-glass tests: the policy bundle grants break-glass no unmask, export or lifecycle rule and no
    rule over the audit trail, relay, chain, verifier or policy bundle; use alerts, is time-boxed,
    rotates the credential and records the review (`T-ADM-05`, `T-ADM-06`).
11. Correlation test: one action yields the same `audit_event_id` in the trail, the chain and — where
    §8.2 requires it — the transparency entry (`T-SEC-03`).
12. Transparency tests: planted operator identity, ticket reference, reason code, network origin and
    value fail the schema; a new trail field does not appear in the projection; a subject reads only
    their own entries; no subject-facing endpoint reads `audit.event` or `chain.record`; an entry
    under a notification hold exists in the store, is invisible until the hold's expiry and visible
    after it, and an open-ended or operator-originated hold is refused (`T-CON-09`, `T-SEC-03`,
    `T-TRU-*`).
13. Aggregate cohort test: a cohort function refuses a result below the minimum cohort size
    (`T-ADM-10`).
14. Lifecycle tests: hardware-factor precondition, approver offboarded mid-flow, recertification lapse
    removal, offboarding evidence (`T-ADM-06`).

### 14. Rollout and rollback

Adopted on merge. `T-SEC-03` freezes the event schema against §10 before any authentication endpoint
emits an event; `T-ADM-01` starts from §3 to §5; `T-ADM-02` from §8.4 and §8.5; `T-PLT-05` from §4.1
and §8.3; the contract tickets from §10. Reversing any part of this record requires a superseding ADR
that states the migration cost: moving administrative routes into the customer process would require
re-issuing every database grant, re-homing the policy bundle and re-proving every absence test; changing
the write semantics would invalidate the chain's continuity and force a signed discontinuity;
changing the correlation identifier would break the link between every existing transparency entry
and its administrative record.
