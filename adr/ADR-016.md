## ADR-016 — Categories as an append-only reporting dimension: exact allocations, as-of views and redirect merges
**Status:** ACCEPTED · 2026-09-30

**Decision.** Eight rules bind the ledger schema, budgets, reporting, ingestion, AI categorisation and
every client that shows where money went:

1. **A category is never a ledger account.** The ledger's income and expense sides are exactly two
   system accounts per (owner, currency) — `system_role = 'INCOME'` (type `INCOME`) and
   `system_role = 'EXPENSE'` (type `EXPENSE`), created lazily under ADR-017 §1.6's pattern and added
   additively under ADR-017 §12 — beside ADR-017's reserved `FX_FEE`. Users create accounts of type
   `ASSET` and `LIABILITY` only. A category is a row of an owner-scoped **reporting dimension** that
   classifies the **entries posted to `INCOME` and `EXPENSE` accounts** (the *categorisable entries*);
   it holds no balance, carries no currency and is never the account of an entry (§1).
2. **Categorisation is append-only.** A transaction's categorisation is an **assignment**: one row of
   `category_assignments` plus its `category_allocations`, inserted as a whole, never updated or
   deleted. The categorisation in force is the **head**, the assignment with the greatest `seq`; a
   change is a new assignment with `seq = head + 1`. Every assignment covers every categorisable
   entry of its transaction, and for each entry its allocation lines **sum exactly** to that entry's
   `amount_minor`, in the entry's sign and currency, enforced by a deferred constraint trigger. A
   remainder is distributed by ADR-015 §2's server-only `allocate` with the line `position` as the
   stable ordering (§2, §3).
3. **Two views, one default.** `CURRENT` (the default) reports every posted transaction under its
   head resolved through the active redirects now. `AS_RECORDED` at an instant `as_of` reports the
   transactions posted by `as_of` under the head and the redirects as they stood at `as_of`. The view
   is a request parameter echoed in every response, never a stored profile setting (§5).
4. **Merge is a redirect, never a rewrite.** Merging category `S` into `T` inserts one row of
   `category_redirects`; no assignment, allocation or entry changes. The target must be terminal (no
   active redirect of its own), a chain resolves in at most **8** hops, and a cycle is impossible by
   construction and refused by the database; a redirect is undone by a write-once retraction, not a
   deletion. A merge never widens a sharing grant on the source category (§6).
5. **System and user categories are rows of one owner-scoped table; "uncategorised" is not a row.**
   System categories are materialised per owner from a versioned reference key list; user categories
   carry an encrypted name; both may have at most one level of parent, fixed at creation. An
   uncategorised amount is an allocation line whose `category_id` is `NULL` — it has no identifier,
   cannot be granted and is never shared. Deleting a category is refused; the offered paths are merge
   and archive (§7).
6. **Principal is a liability movement; interest and fees are expenses.** An instalment's principal is
   an entry on the debt's `LIABILITY` account and is therefore structurally outside the dimension; the
   interest and fee entries are `EXPENSE` entries categorised under the reserved system keys
   `debt.interest` and `debt.fees`. When a source does not state the split, the payment posts as a
   transfer to the liability and the interest enters only when a statement, capture or identified
   reconciliation cause records it (ADR-017 §3.2, §6.2) — never from a projection. A transfer between
   the owner's own accounts has no categorisable entry and cannot be categorised (§8).
7. **`transactions.category_id` is a derived projection, not the record of truth.** ADR-018 §4.3
   compares it, so it exists: for a posted transaction it is the single resolved category of the head
   when the head names exactly one non-null category and the categorisable entries explain the whole
   movement of the transaction, and `NULL` otherwise (split, uncategorised, unexplained, not posted);
   the application writes it through and a deferred trigger refuses any value that disagrees with
   the derivation (§9).
8. **Sources have a fixed precedence.** `USER` > `RULE` > `IMPORT` > `AI` > `SYSTEM`; a new assignment
   supersedes the head only when its source ranks at least as high, so a user's answer is never
   overwritten by a rule, an import, a model or an ingestion default. A reversal's categorisation
   mirrors its target's head with negated amounts, and a reversed transaction and its reversal are
   frozen; the re-post carries the categorisation forward (§4).

**Why.** The research model wrote `Expense:Groceries +1200` as if a category were an account
(`architecture/01-domain-model.md` §2.1), and `PenniLogic/api#7` still says "expense category
debited". Read that way, every category question becomes a ledger question with the wrong answers:
a category merge is a re-post of every historical entry (ADR-017 §2 makes every entry immutable and
every correction a dated reversal, so "Groceries into Food" would post thousands of reversal pairs
under new `booked_at` instants and bury real corrections); a split across categories needs a second
entry per category and a re-post to change it, although `entry_count` is sealed at posting (ADR-017
Z7); an account is bound to one currency (ADR-017 Z3) while a category total is a per-currency
breakdown over whatever currencies the owner spends in (ADR-017 §5.1, §6.5); and a sharing grant on a
category (ADR-005: per member × category × detail level) would become a grant on an account with a
balance. Read the other way — mutable rows that are re-labelled and re-pointed in place — the model
loses the property every other record protects: a figure reported, exported or shared yesterday can
no longer be reproduced today, a grantee's aggregate can be contradicted by the same server a day
later, and a compromised process can re-categorise history without leaving a row (ADR-017 §2.5(d)).
An **append-only dimension over immutable entries** keeps the ledger's guarantees and gives reporting
the freedom the ledger cannot: categorisation changes are new dated facts, both the current and the
as-recorded picture are derivable from the same rows (the ledger is already bitemporal, ADR-017
§3.1), a merge is a dated redirect that the same derivation follows, and a split is a set of lines
whose exact sum the database enforces by the mechanism ADR-017 §1.2 already uses. The dimension is
therefore decided after the money and ledger records, as the parallel boundary of `PenniLogic/docs#36`
requires, and it changes nothing they fixed.

### 1. A category is not an account

**1.1 The decision, stated once.** `categories` rows are never referenced by `entries.account_id`,
never carry a `currency` or a balance, and never appear in ADR-017 §3.1's derivations. Every entry is
posted to an account of ADR-017 §1.6's six types; the income and expense types are represented, per
owner and currency, by exactly one system account each:

| `system_role` | `type` | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `INCOME` | `INCOME` | Counter-account of every income entry of the owner in that currency. |
| `EXPENSE` | `EXPENSE` | Counter-account of every spending entry of the owner in that currency. |

Both are created lazily by the server on the owner's first posting in that currency and are made one
per (owner, role, currency) by ADR-017 §1.6's partial unique index; ADR-017 §12 admits a new
`system_role` whose `type` is one of the six as additive evolution, so ADR-017 is extended, not
contradicted (§15). The accounts API (`PenniLogic/api#6`) accepts `type ∈ {ASSET, LIABILITY}` from a
client and refuses every other type as `validation_rejected` with reason
`account_type_not_user_creatable`; `EQUITY`, `CLEARING`, `INCOME` and `EXPENSE` accounts exist only
as system accounts.

**1.2 What is categorisable.** A *categorisable entry* is an entry whose account has
`type IN ('INCOME', 'EXPENSE')` — the owner's `INCOME` and `EXPENSE` system accounts and ADR-017's
`FX_FEE`. Entries on `ASSET`, `LIABILITY`, `EQUITY` and `CLEARING` accounts are never categorisable,
so a transfer between the owner's accounts, a credit-card bill payment, an ATM withdrawal into a cash
account, a loan disbursement, an opening balance, a reconciliation adjustment and the principal part
of an instalment are outside the dimension by construction, not by a filter that a query can forget
(`PenniLogic/api#8`, `PenniLogic/api#44`; §8). The **categorisable amount** of a transaction is the
`amount_minor` of each of its categorisable entries; in the ordinary one-sided transaction there is
exactly one, and its magnitude is "the transaction amount" the acceptance criteria of
`PenniLogic/docs#36` and `PenniLogic/api#9` name. A category total is a sum of allocation lines, and
therefore a per-currency sum over entries, exactly as ADR-017 §6.5 anticipated.

**1.3 Sign.** Allocation lines carry the entry's sign (ADR-017 §1.1: debit positive). Spending in an
`EXPENSE`-nature category is the positive sum of its lines; a refund posted as a credit to the
`EXPENSE` account (ADR-017 §2.6) is a negative line that reduces it. Income in an `INCOME`-nature
category is the negated sum of its lines. Reports present these signed sums as ADR-017 §5.2's
`MoneyByCurrency`, one bucket per currency, never a mixed total.

**1.4 Rejected: the category as an account, with reasons.** (a) *Merge cost*: ADR-017 §2 forbids
editing an entry, so moving a category's history means one reversal and one re-post per transaction,
each with the correction's `booked_at`, which makes the as-recorded axis useless for real corrections
and multiplies the ledger by the number of merges. (b) *Splits*: a split is a second entry, and
changing it is a re-post, although `entry_count` is sealed (ADR-017 Z7) and the researched product
(`PenniLogic/android#21`, `PenniLogic/web#9`) treats a split as an edit of a label, not of money.
(c) *Currency*: an account has one currency (ADR-017 Z3); a category spans the owner's currencies.
(d) *Sharing*: ADR-018 §4.3 admits transactions by `category_id`; an account-level grant would
expose a balance. (e) *Privilege*: an account-per-category model needs the runtime role to create
and archive accounts on every category edit, and the `accounts` table is a ledger table (ADR-018
§1.3). **Rejected: mutable category rows that rewrite history** — re-pointing `transactions.category_id`
in place and renaming or deleting rows: yesterday's report, export and shared aggregate cannot be
reproduced; a grantee's view changes without a fact that explains it; idempotent replays
(ADR-015 §4.4) return receipts that name categories that no longer exist; and an attacker with the
standing `UPDATE` grant rewrites categorisation history undetectably (ADR-017 §2.5(d)).

### 2. The dimension: tables, constraints and privileges

**2.1 `system_category_keys`** — the reference key list (ADR-018 §1.1 *reference* class: no owner, no
row-level security, `SELECT` granted to `pennilogic_app` and nothing else to any runtime role, rows
written by migrations as the table owner from the versioned seed file `category-seed.v1.json`
published by `PenniLogic/contracts`; `PenniLogic/api#9` owns the seed content).

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `key` | `TEXT` | Primary key; pattern `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{1,30}(\.[a-z][a-z0-9_]{1,30})?$`; a two-segment key's first segment is its `parent_key`. |
| `parent_key` | `TEXT`, nullable | References `system_category_keys (key)`; the parent has `parent_key IS NULL` (one level). |
| `nature` | `TEXT` | `EXPENSE` or `INCOME`; a child's `nature` equals its parent's. |
| `icon`, `colour` | `TEXT` | Values of the seed file's fixed `icons[]` and `colours[]` registries. |
| `introduced_in`, `retired_in` | `INTEGER`, nullable `INTEGER` | Seed versions; a retired key is not materialised for new owners and existing rows stand. |

Reserved keys this record requires: `debt` (`EXPENSE`), `debt.interest`, `debt.fees`, `fees`
(`EXPENSE`) and `fees.foreign_exchange` (the default of an `FX_FEE` entry; E30 write path,
`PenniLogic/api#46`). Every other key, its labels per locale and its icon and colour are seed content
(`PenniLogic/api#9`, reviewed for the Indian market), not this record's.

**2.2 `categories`** — one owner-scoped table under `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` with ADR-018 §4.2's
`owner_rows` and **no grant policy** (a grantee never reads a category row; §9.3).

| Column | Class | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | constrained | Primary key (UUIDv7); `UNIQUE (id, owner_id)` so composite keys are legal. |
| `owner_id` | constrained | `NOT NULL`; the row-level-security predicate. |
| `kind` | constrained | `SYSTEM` or `USER`. |
| `system_key` | constrained | References `system_category_keys (key)`; `NOT NULL` iff `kind = 'SYSTEM'`; partial `UNIQUE (owner_id, system_key)` — one materialised row per owner and key. |
| `parent_id` | constrained | Nullable; composite foreign key `(parent_id, owner_id) → categories (id, owner_id)`; the parent has `parent_id IS NULL` and no active redirect at the child's creation (`category_depth_one`; a redirected parent is `category_redirected`); **immutable**; for a `SYSTEM` row it is the owner's row for `system_category_keys.parent_key`. |
| `nature` | constrained | `EXPENSE` or `INCOME`; immutable; equals the parent's when a parent exists (`category_nature_inherited`); for a `SYSTEM` row equals the key's. |
| `name` | encrypted | `NULL` for a `SYSTEM` row (the client renders the seed label for `system_key` in the user's locale) unless the owner sets a personal label; required for a `USER` row; mutable descriptive metadata. |
| `icon`, `colour` | constrained | Registry values; mutable descriptive metadata; a `SYSTEM` row defaults to the key's. |
| `is_archived` | constrained | Mutable descriptive metadata; an archived category accepts no new user, rule, import or AI allocation (§7.4). |
| `created_at` | constrained | Server clock. |

System rows are materialised lazily — on the owner's first read of the category list or first posting
of a categorisable entry the server inserts one `SYSTEM` row per non-retired key, idempotently under
the partial unique index; a key introduced by a later seed version is materialised the same way at
the next such event. Name uniqueness within an owner and parent is an application check over the
owner's decrypted rows (ADR-018 §1.1: a handful of rows per user in the application), refused as
`category_name_duplicate`; the database cannot compare ciphertext and no blind index is created for
names. A `USER` category may be created under a `SYSTEM` or `USER` root; creating a child under a
child is refused as `category_depth_exceeded`.

**2.3 `category_assignments`** — one row per categorisation act; owner-scoped, `owner_rows`, no grant
policy.

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `UUID` | Primary key (UUIDv7); `UNIQUE (id, owner_id)`. |
| `owner_id` | `UUID` | `NOT NULL`. |
| `transaction_id` | `UUID` | Composite foreign key `(transaction_id, owner_id) → transactions (id, owner_id)`. |
| `seq` | `INTEGER` | `>= 1`; `UNIQUE (transaction_id, seq)`; the **head** of a transaction is its greatest `seq`; a new assignment carries `head + 1`, and a concurrent writer that loses the unique constraint is retried once by the server against the new head, so two devices categorising offline both leave their fact in history and the last committed one is the head (`architecture/01-domain-model.md` §7: last-writer-wins on user-editable metadata). |
| `source` | `TEXT` | `USER`, `RULE`, `IMPORT`, `AI` or `SYSTEM` (§4.2). |
| `reason` | `TEXT` | Closed set of §4.2, compatible with `source`. |
| `provenance_ref` | `UUID`, nullable | Required with the referent §4.2 states per `reason` (a rule, an import evidence reference, an inference record, or the assignment carried or mirrored); optional for `USER_CONFIRMATION` (the clarification item); forbidden for `USER_EDIT` and `POSTING_DEFAULT`. Opaque; never a model output string. |
| `assigned_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | Server clock; `assigned_at_plausible` (`BEFORE INSERT`, within `PT5M` of the database clock, ADR-017 §1.5's `booked_at_plausible` shape) validates and never assigns; for `seq = 1` equal to the transaction's `booked_at` (`initial_assignment_at_posting`). It is the as-recorded axis of §5. |

**2.4 `category_allocations`** — the lines of an assignment; owner-scoped, `owner_rows`, no grant
policy. A line has **no currency column**: it inherits its entry's currency through `entry_id`, as
`split_shares` inherits its expense's (ADR-017 §6.3).

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `UUID` | Primary key (UUIDv7). |
| `assignment_id` | `UUID` | Composite foreign key `(assignment_id, owner_id) → category_assignments (id, owner_id)`. |
| `owner_id` | `UUID` | `NOT NULL`. |
| `entry_id` | `UUID` | References `entries (id)`; the entry belongs to the assignment's transaction (`allocation_entry_in_transaction`) and to a categorisable account (`allocation_entry_categorisable`). |
| `category_id` | `UUID`, nullable | Composite foreign key `(category_id, owner_id) → categories (id, owner_id)`; `NULL` is the **uncategorised line**; at most one `NULL` line per (`assignment_id`, `entry_id`). |
| `amount_minor` | `BIGINT` | `<> 0`; `>= -9223372036854775807` (ADR-017 Z5); same sign as the entry's `amount_minor`. |
| `position` | `SMALLINT` | `0`-based, contiguous and unique within (`assignment_id`, `entry_id`); the stable ordering of ADR-015 §2's `allocate`. |

**2.5 `category_redirects`** — the merge facts; owner-scoped, `owner_rows`, no grant policy.

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `UUID` | Primary key (UUIDv7). |
| `owner_id` | `UUID` | `NOT NULL`. |
| `source_category_id`, `target_category_id` | `UUID` | Composite foreign keys to `categories (id, owner_id)`; `source <> target` (`redirect_self`). |
| `created_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | Server clock, validated by `assigned_at_plausible` (§2.6). |
| `retracted_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)`, nullable | Write-once `NULL`-to-value (`redirect_retracted_once`), validated by `assigned_at_plausible`; a retracted redirect is inactive from that instant and is never reactivated — a new merge is a new row. |

A redirect is **active** at instant `r` when `created_at <= r` and (`retracted_at IS NULL` or
`retracted_at > r`). Partial `UNIQUE (source_category_id) WHERE retracted_at IS NULL` gives every
category at most one active redirect (`redirect_single_active`). The insertion rules are §6.2.

**2.6 The constraints, stated precisely.** For `t` a transaction, `E_c(t)` its categorisable entries,
`A` an assignment of `t`, `L(A, e)` the lines of `A` for entry `e`:

```text
C1  ∀ A, ∀ e ∈ E_c(t):  SUM(L(A, e).amount_minor) = e.amount_minor  ∧  COUNT(L(A, e)) >= 1
      ∧ ∀ l ∈ L(A, e): sign(l.amount_minor) = sign(e.amount_minor)                (category_allocation_sum)
C2  ∀ A: every e ∈ E_c(t) has L(A, e) ≠ ∅, and no line names an entry outside E_c(t)
                                                                                    (category_allocation_coverage)
C3  ∀ l: l.category_id IS NULL ∨ categories(l.category_id).nature = nature_of(account(l.entry))
      where nature_of(EXPENSE account) = EXPENSE, nature_of(INCOME account) = INCOME  (allocation_category_nature)
C4  ∀ l with l.category_id NOT NULL, at insert, unless A.reason copies an earlier assignment's lines
      (REVERSAL_MIRROR, REPOST_CARRY, DUPLICATE_CARRY, REFUND_LINK, RULE_ROLLBACK):
      the category has no active redirect and is_archived = false      (allocation_target_assignable)
C5  ∀ (A, e): positions of L(A, e) are 0..n-1; at most one line with category_id IS NULL
                                                                                    (allocation_shape)
C6  ∀ posted t with E_c(t) ≠ ∅: an assignment with seq = 1 exists at COMMIT, its assigned_at =
      t.booked_at; ∀ A with seq > 1: A.seq = 1 + MAX(seq of earlier assignments of t)  (category_head_exists,
                                                                                     initial_assignment_at_posting)
C7  ∀ A with seq > 1: rank(A.source) >= rank(head.source) ∨ (A.reason = RULE_ROLLBACK ∧
      head.source = RULE ∧ head.provenance_ref = A.provenance_ref)                  (category_assignment_precedence)
C8  ∀ A of a transaction t: t.kind <> 'REVERSAL' ∨ A.seq = 1 (a reversal's mirror is its only assignment);
      ∀ A with seq > 1: t is not REVERSED (ADR-017 §2.1 effective status)          (category_assignment_frozen)
C9  ∀ posted t: transactions.category_id IS NOT DISTINCT FROM projection(t) at COMMIT (§9)
      ∀ non-posted t (entry_count = 0): transactions.category_id IS NULL             (category_projection_equivalence)
C10 ∀ redirect d at insert: target terminal, chain depth into source + 1 <= 8, natures equal,
      a source that is the group of any category targets a root (§6.2)               (redirect_target_terminal,
                                                                                     redirect_depth, redirect_nature,
                                                                                     redirect_parent_to_root)
C11 ∀ category c: c.parent_id IS NULL ∨ (parent(c).parent_id IS NULL ∧ parent(c) had no active
      redirect when c was created); c.nature = parent(c).nature                      (category_depth_one,
                                                                                     category_nature_inherited)
```

C1, C2, C5, C6, C7, C8 and C9 are **deferred constraint triggers**, `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`,
`SECURITY INVOKER`, one trigger family per table, by the mechanism and for the reasons of ADR-017
§1.2–§1.3 (a `CHECK` cannot see the other lines of an assignment, and the assignment is necessarily
incomplete between its first and last line). C3, C4, C10 and C11 are `BEFORE INSERT` validation
triggers or declarative constraints evaluated per row, because each needs only the row and committed
state; C10 additionally runs after the row locks of §6.2 and is re-evaluated at `COMMIT`. ADR-017
§1.2's **visibility precondition**
applies unchanged: every trigger aggregates only the rows the invoking role's policies admit, and
`owner_rows` is `FOR ALL`, so the triggers see every row of the owner's transaction and fail closed
when they do not (`CategoryAllocationSumTriggerTest`). Each rejection raises `ERRCODE 23514` with the
constraint identifier as the message and an opaque identifier as the detail — never an amount.

**2.7 Privileges.** `pennilogic_app` holds `SELECT` and `INSERT` on the four owner tables; column-level
`UPDATE` on `categories.name`, `.icon`, `.colour`, `.is_archived` and on `category_redirects.retracted_at`
(write-once, trigger-guarded); `UPDATE` on `transactions.category_id` as ADR-018 §4.1 already grants,
now bound by C9; no other `UPDATE` and **no `DELETE`** on any of them; `SELECT` only on
`system_category_keys`. "Delete category" in any client is therefore a merge (§6) or an archive (§7.4),
and the API refuses a delete request as `category_delete_unsupported`, naming both paths
(`PenniLogic/api#9`'s outcome). A category change that is not one of these grants is a privilege error,
not a policy breach (`CategoryAppendOnlyPrivilegeTest`).

### 3. Split allocations and the remainder rule

**3.1 One storage shape.** A single-category transaction is an assignment with one line per
categorisable entry; a split is an assignment with several lines for an entry. There is no second
"single-category" storage shape: the wire shorthand `category_id` on a transaction is the projection
of §9, and `PenniLogic/api#9`'s "category-split schema distinct from the single-category assignment
case" is realised as that shorthand plus `allocations[]`, never as two storage paths (§10).

**3.2 The request forms.** For each categorisable entry the caller supplies **either** exact lines
`{category_id | null, amount: Money}` **or** weighted lines `{category_id | null, weight: integer}` with
`1 <= weight <= 1000000`; at most **32** lines per entry; mixing forms within one entry, more lines, a
weight outside the range or an empty line set is `allocation_shape_invalid` (the bounds keep the
server's proportional arithmetic in exact arbitrary-precision integers and deny a degenerate split).
Exact lines must already sum to the
entry's amount in the entry's sign: a shortfall or excess is refused as `allocation_sum_mismatch`
with `direction ∈ {shortfall, excess}` and the field, **and no amount** — T-CON-12's
`validation_rejected` names the field without a monetary value
(`product/client-state-taxonomy.md` §9), which amends `PenniLogic/api#9`'s "naming the exact shortfall
amount" (§10); the client already holds both figures and may show its running total as a display
aid, but never posts a computed share. A line whose sign differs from the entry's is
`allocation_sign_mismatch`; a zero line is refused by C1's `<> 0`; a line on an entry that is not
categorisable is `allocation_entry_not_categorisable`; a line whose category has the other nature
(an `EXPENSE` entry under an `INCOME` category, or the reverse) is `category_nature_mismatch`.

**3.3 The remainder rule, stated once.** Weighted lines are turned into exact lines **on the server
only** by ADR-015 §2's `allocate(shares, ordering)` with `shares` = the weights in `position` order
and `ordering` = ascending `position`: the entry's absolute amount is divided in proportion to the
weights, each share is floored, the remaining minor units are given one each to the lines with the
largest fractional remainder, **ties broken by the lowest `position`**, and the entry's sign is
reapplied. The result sums exactly to the entry's amount for every currency exponent and is
deterministic for the same weights and order; `allocate` is absent from the client artifact
(ADR-015 §2), so no client can compute or post a share, and every share a client shows is the
server's. Worked examples:

```text
₹100.00 (10000 paise), weights [1, 1, 1]  →  3334, 3333, 3333   (equal remainders; position 0 wins the unit)
₹100.00, weights [1, 2]                    →  3333, 6667         (remainder .67 at position 1 beats .33)
¥100 (exponent 0), weights [1, 1, 1]       →  34, 33, 33
refund −₹500.00 (−50000), weights [3, 1]   →  −37500, −12500     (exact; sign reapplied)
₹1,200.00 (120000), exact 80000 + 39900    →  allocation_sum_mismatch{direction: shortfall}
```

`MoneyAllocationTest` (ADR-015 §7, `PenniLogic/api#33`) already proves the function; this record's
`CategorySplitAllocationTest` proves its use with the position ordering, the uncategorised line as a
weighted participant, and the refusals (§13).

**3.4 Uncategorised remainder.** A line with `category_id IS NULL` is a legitimate participant in an
exact or weighted split ("₹800 groceries, the rest not yet decided"); C5 admits one per entry so the
sum invariant holds for partially categorised transactions too. Reports count it in the
uncategorised bucket (§5.4).

### 4. The assignment lifecycle: sources, precedence, corrections

**4.1 The initial assignment is part of posting.** Posting a transaction that has a categorisable entry
(`PenniLogic/api#7`; a candidate moving to `posted` under ADR-018 §7.3; every `REVERSAL`, `REPOST`,
`EXCHANGE_LEG` with a fee entry) inserts its `seq = 1` assignment **in the same database transaction**,
with `assigned_at = booked_at` (C6). For an ordinary posting its content is, in order of precedence: the owner's matching
`merchant_rules` row (`source = RULE`, `reason = RULE_APPLIED`; ADR-018 §1.2, `PenniLogic/api#19`);
otherwise the merchant dictionary's `default_category_hint` — a system key — resolved to the owner's
materialised row and then through active redirects (`SYSTEM`, `POSTING_DEFAULT`); otherwise one
uncategorised line per categorisable entry (`SYSTEM`, `POSTING_DEFAULT`). A `REVERSAL` and a `REPOST`
receive §4.4's mirror and carry, a refund linked at posting §8.3's `REFUND_LINK`, and an `EXCHANGE_LEG`
fee entry the reserved key `fees.foreign_exchange` (§8.4). A candidate row (ADR-018's
`candidate` and `suppressed_by_link` statuses, `entry_count = 0`) has no entries and therefore no
assignment; a category suggested for it before posting is the clarification item's state (T-CON-02,
`PenniLogic/contracts#6`), applied at posting as the initial assignment with the suggestion's source,
and `transactions.category_id` stays `NULL` until posting (C9). An assignment request on a candidate
is refused as `transaction_not_posted`.

**4.2 Sources, ranks and reasons.** `rank(USER) = 5 > rank(RULE) = 4 > rank(IMPORT) = 3 > rank(AI) = 2 >
rank(SYSTEM) = 1`. `reason` is the closed set below; a (`source`, `reason`) pair outside the table is
refused by `CHECK`.

| `reason` | Allowed `source` | Meaning; `provenance_ref` |
|---|---|---|
| `USER_EDIT` | `USER` | The owner categorised in a client; none. |
| `USER_CONFIRMATION` | `USER` | The owner accepted a suggestion in the clarification inbox; the clarification item (optional). |
| `RULE_APPLIED` | `RULE` | The owner's learned merchant rule matched; `merchant_rules.id`. |
| `IMPORT_COLUMN` | `IMPORT` | The category column of a user-supplied import (T-CON-10, `PenniLogic/api#10`, `PenniLogic/api#62`) mapped to the owner's categories in the dry-run preview; the import evidence reference. |
| `AI_APPLIED` | `AI` | A model result applied by `T-AIP-01`'s policy above its confidence threshold; the inference record. |
| `POSTING_DEFAULT` | `SYSTEM` | §4.1's default at posting; none. |
| `REVERSAL_MIRROR` | `SYSTEM` | §4.4; the mirrored assignment. |
| `REPOST_CARRY` | the carried assignment's `source` | §4.4; the carried assignment. |
| `DUPLICATE_CARRY` | the carried assignment's `source` | §4.5; the carried assignment. |
| `REFUND_LINK` | `SYSTEM` | §8.3; the linked purchase's head assignment. |
| `RULE_ROLLBACK` | the restored assignment's `source`, or `SYSTEM` when none existed | §4.6; `merchant_rules.id` of the rolled-back rule. |

**4.3 Supersession (C7).** An assignment with `seq > 1` commits only when `rank(source) >= rank(head.source)`,
or when it is a `RULE_ROLLBACK` naming the rule that produced the head. Equal rank supersedes: a later
user act replaces an earlier one, a later model run replaces an earlier one, a `REFUND_LINK` replaces a
`POSTING_DEFAULT`. The refusal is `categorisation_precedence`; the client shows the head and offers
the user's own act, which always succeeds. Consequently a user-confirmed category survives every later
enrichment, rule, import and model result (`PenniLogic/api#18`'s test, `PenniLogic/ai-service#5`'s
override), and disabling the AI path needs no migration: nothing is rewritten, new transactions
receive rule or system defaults, and existing `AI` heads stand until a higher or equal source replaces
them (`CategoryPrecedenceTest`).

**4.4 Corrections mirror and carry.** ADR-017 §2 corrects a fact by a `REVERSAL` `R` and, when the fact
is restated, one or more `REPOST`s `P`. The correction helper (`PenniLogic/api#7`) posts, in the same
database transaction: for `R`, a `REVERSAL_MIRROR` assignment whose lines are the target's head lines
with **negated amounts, the same categories and positions** — verbatim, even if a category has since
been archived or redirected (C4's exemption), so the two heads cancel exactly in every view at every
instant after the reversal and resolve identically at read time; for each `P`, a `REPOST_CARRY`
assignment whose lines carry the replaced transaction's head **categories in position order** and
whose amounts are `allocate(shares = the carried absolute amounts, ordering = position)` over `P`'s
categorisable entry, unless the correction request supplies an explicit assignment, which is posted
instead with the request's `USER` source. A carry applies when the receiving transaction has exactly
one categorisable entry, the ordinary case; a re-post with several categorisable entries receives the
posting default of §4.1 unless the request supplies an assignment. A `REVERSAL` has exactly one assignment, and after the
reversal neither the reversed transaction nor `R` accepts a new one (`transaction_reversed`,
`transaction_is_reversal`; C8): the fact lives on in `P`, which is re-categorised like any other
transaction. A transaction with `entry_count = 0` — a candidate reversed as `reversal_target_not_posted`
would be refused by ADR-017 anyway — is never mirrored. Category totals therefore never filter on
`status`: like ADR-017 §3.1's sums, a reversed transaction's lines remain in every sum and are cancelled
by its reversal's (`CategoryReversalMirrorTest`).

**4.5 Duplicate links.** When ADR-018 §7.3 merges two candidates, the survivor keeps its head; if the
non-surviving row was posted, ADR-017 §2.3's `DUPLICATE_LINK` reversal mirrors it (§4.4). Where the
non-surviving row's head outranks the survivor's — the user categorised the duplicate, the survivor
carries a default — the merge also posts a `DUPLICATE_CARRY` on the survivor with the carried lines
re-allocated over the survivor's single categorisable entry by `allocate` (§4.4's carry rule), so "a user-confirmed value always
wins" (ADR-018 §7.3) holds for categorisation; where both heads are `USER` and differ, the survivor's
stands and the conflict is presented in the clarification inbox (`PenniLogic/android#12`,
`PenniLogic/web#25`). An unmerge re-posts the suppressed candidate under ADR-018 §7.3 and ADR-017 §2.2,
and its `REPOST_CARRY` restores the categorisation it had.

**4.6 Rule rollback restores exactly.** A merchant rule (`PenniLogic/api#19`) that was created from a
clarification may be rolled back (`PenniLogic/android#12`, `PenniLogic/web#25`, T-CON-02). Because
every `RULE_APPLIED` assignment names the rule in `provenance_ref`, the affected set is `SELECT` over
the owner's assignments, the rollback surface shows its count before confirmation, and the rollback
posts, for each affected transaction whose head is still that rule's assignment, one `RULE_ROLLBACK`
assignment with the lines of the assignment that preceded it (`seq − 1`) and that assignment's source
— or one uncategorised line per entry with `source = SYSTEM` when the rule's assignment was `seq = 1`.
Transactions whose head has since moved on (a user edit above the rule) are untouched. History keeps
the rule's assignments; the prior categorisation is restored **exactly**, which is what the tickets'
rollback tests assert (`RuleRollbackRestorationTest`).

**4.7 Idempotency, ownership and concurrency.** Every categorisation, category, merge and retraction
request is a state-changing operation under ADR-015 §4 with its own key; a replay returns the receipt
of the same assignment or redirect, never a second one. A request naming a transaction the principal
does not own receives exactly ADR-017 §2.3's `transaction_not_found` answer, as the composite keys
produce; a category it does not own receives `category_not_found`, and nothing is disclosed either
way. Two devices that categorised the same transaction offline
converge as §2.3 states — both assignments in history, the last committed the head — and a client
that receives the change feed replaces its local head rather than merging lines, because an
assignment converges **as a whole set** (T-SYN-01, `PenniLogic/contracts#4`: allocation-bearing records
never converge per line).

### 5. Views: current and as-recorded

**5.1 Definitions, stated once.** For a transaction `t`, an instant `r`, a category `c`, a period `P`
(ADR-015 §3.3 half-open UTC interval in the profile zone) and a currency `ccy`:

```text
head(t, r)        = the assignment of t with the greatest seq among those with assigned_at <= r
active(d, r)      = d.created_at <= r ∧ (d.retracted_at IS NULL ∨ d.retracted_at > r)
resolve(c, r)     = c when no redirect active at r has source c; otherwise resolve(target, r)   -- ≤ 8 hops (§6.3)
group(c, r)       = resolve(parent(resolve(c, r)), r) when resolve(c, r) has a parent, else resolve(c, r)
line_category(l, r) = NULL when l.category_id IS NULL, else resolve(l.category_id, r)
in_view(t, r)     = t is posted ∧ (r = ∞ ∨ t.booked_at <= r)          -- ADR-017 §3.1's as-recorded axis
total(c, P, ccy, r) = Σ l.amount_minor  over lines l of head(t, r), for every t with in_view(t, r),
                      effective_date(t) ∈ P (ADR-017 §1.5), t.currency = ccy, line_category(l, r) = c
uncategorised(P, ccy, r) = the same sum over lines with l.category_id IS NULL
```

`CURRENT` is `r = ∞`: every posted transaction, the head now, the redirects active now.
`AS_RECORDED` at `as_of` is `r = as_of`: the transactions posted by then, their head then, the
redirects active then. Both are derived at read time from the same rows; nothing is stored per view,
and every sum is exact integer arithmetic over `BIGINT` lines (`SUM` is `NUMERIC` in SQL, as ADR-017
§3.1 relies on), overflow-checked before it becomes a `Money`.
The property `AS_RECORDED at now ≡ CURRENT` holds by construction and is asserted
(`CategoryAsOfViewTest`). A `group` total is the sum of the totals of the categories that resolve
into it, so the one-level hierarchy of §7.2 rolls up without a second derivation.

**5.2 The default is `CURRENT`.** Budgets, the clarification inbox, the AI's reading of the user's
categories, the projection column of §9 and every sharing predicate use the current categorisation:
when a user corrects a category, every surface that answers "where does my money go" must agree, and
ADR-018 §4.3 admits transactions by their effective — current — category. `AS_RECORDED` is offered on
**reports** (category totals, the category detail list and exports; `PenniLogic/api#72`,
`PenniLogic/api#73`, `PenniLogic/contracts#12`, `PenniLogic/contracts#21`, `PenniLogic/contracts#14`),
not on budgets or sharing.

**5.3 The switch.** A report request carries `view = CURRENT | AS_RECORDED` (default `CURRENT`) and, for
`AS_RECORDED`, `as_of` as an ADR-015 §3.1 `Instant`. When `as_of` is omitted it defaults to the
period's `end` instant; a value after the request instant is clamped to the request instant; a value
before the owner's first posting yields an empty report; `as_of` supplied with `CURRENT` is
`validation_rejected` (`as_of_not_applicable`). The response carries the `CategorisationView` it used
— `{mode, as_of}` with the **effective** instant — beside ADR-017 §5.2's `Derivation`, and clients render
an "as recorded on <instant>" marker for the whole surface while the view is active. The choice is a
request parameter: the server stores no view preference, a client may remember the user's last choice
as a display preference, and switching back is the same request with `view = CURRENT`. Nothing
stored changes in either direction.

**5.4 Buckets.** A report row is keyed by the resolved category (`category_id`, its `system_key` when
system, its `group`) with `MoneyByCurrency` totals; the uncategorised bucket is keyed by
`category_id: null` and is never absent when non-zero. Redirected categories never appear as rows in
`CURRENT`; in `AS_RECORDED` they appear exactly as they stood at `as_of`. Labels (`name`, `icon`,
`colour`) are descriptive metadata rendered from the row as it is now in both views — a rename is not
a categorisation fact and changes no total.

**5.5 Read models.** `period_aggregates` (ADR-018 §1.2; `PenniLogic/api#72`) is a `CURRENT`-view read
model keyed by the **resolved** category, recomputed from `total` after any assignment or redirect
change of the owner, under ADR-017 §3.5's five conditions; its `entry_count` counts allocation lines;
ADR-017 §8's `ledger.balance.reconciliation_mismatch{read_model = period_aggregates}` polices it.
`AS_RECORDED` is never cached. Budget rollover and any other figure derived from a category total is
computed from `total` at read time in `CURRENT`, never stored (ADR-010; §10.2).

### 6. Merge is a redirect

**6.1 What a merge is.** Merging `S` into `T` inserts one `category_redirects` row `(S → T, created_at)`.
No assignment, allocation, entry or category row changes; `S` keeps its rows, its name and its history.
From `created_at` on, `resolve(S, r) = resolve(T, r)`: every line that names `S` counts under `T` in
`CURRENT` and in any `AS_RECORDED` view at or after `created_at`, and under `S` in views before it.
New allocations may not name `S` (C4: `category_redirected`); the posting default and the rule engine
resolve their targets first (§4.1). "Transactions move, ledger amounts untouched" (`PenniLogic/api#9`)
is therefore literal: the checksum over every `entries` row and over every `category_allocations` row
is identical before and after a merge (`CategoryNotAnAccountTest`, `CategoryRedirectCycleTest`).

**6.2 Insertion rules (C10).** Inside one database transaction the server locks the `categories` rows of
`S` and `T` with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` in ascending `id` order (so two concurrent merges touching the
same categories serialise without deadlock and the validation that follows sees committed state), then
validates:

1. `S <> T` (`redirect_self`) and both belong to the owner (`category_not_found` otherwise — the
   not-found answer, as ADR-017 §2.3 gives for a foreign target).
2. **`T` is terminal**: no active redirect has source `T` (`redirect_target_not_terminal`). A user who
   wants to merge into a merged-away category is offered its resolution.
3. **`S` has no active redirect** (`redirect_already_active`; `redirect_single_active` is the declarative
   backstop).
4. **Depth**: let `depth_in(S)` be the length of the longest chain of active redirects ending at `S`
   (a recursive query following `target = current`); `depth_in(S) + 1 <= 8` (`redirect_depth_exceeded`).
   The recourse is retracting a redirect in that lineage; eight successive merges of one lineage is
   category churn the client should surface, not a ledger need, and the count is observed (§12).
5. `nature(S) = nature(T)` (`redirect_nature_mismatch`): spending is never merged into income.
6. If `S` is the `group` of any category — some category `x` has a parent that resolves to `S` — `T`
   is a root (`redirect_parent_to_root`), so `group` of §5.1 never nests two deep; a child, or a root
   that heads no group, may target any category of the same nature. Together with C11's rule that a
   child is never created under a redirected parent, this keeps every roll-up one level deep at every
   instant.

The deferred trigger of the same name re-evaluates 2–6 at `COMMIT` as a backstop. Because `T` must be
terminal, **chains form only when a former target is itself merged later** (`A → B`, then `B → C`).

**6.3 Depth 8 and cycle impossibility, proven.** Take any chain `c₀ → c₁ → … → cₖ` of redirects all
active at some instant `r`, and its last-inserted edge `cᵢ → cᵢ₊₁`. Retraction is write-once and
permanent, so every other edge of the chain was active at that insert (inserted earlier, never
retracted before `r`). Rule 2 then forces `cᵢ₊₁` to be terminal at that moment, so `i + 1 = k`, and
because the active chain `c₀ → … → cᵢ` already counted towards `depth_in(cᵢ)`, rule 4 bounds
`k = i + 1 <= depth_in(cᵢ) + 1 <= 8`. A cycle would be such a chain with `cₖ = c₀`; its last-inserted
edge `u → v` would have found `v` with an active outgoing edge (the next edge of the cycle) and been
refused by rule 2. Hence at every instant, in every view, `resolve` terminates within 8 hops and never
revisits a category; the resolver in the API and in every client reads `redirect_max_depth` from §14
and treats a longer walk as a defect (`category_redirect_unresolvable`, a `request_failed`-class
condition that the signals of §12 must show as zero; `PenniLogic/contracts#4` asks what the server
returns when a chain cannot be resolved, and this is the answer). `CategoryRedirectCycleTest` attempts
the two-node cycle, the three-node cycle, the concurrent pair, the ninth hop and raw SQL as the runtime
role, and asserts every one is refused (§13).

**6.4 Retraction (unmerge).** Setting `retracted_at` on the active redirect `S → T` ends it from that
instant; lines that name `S` count under `S` again in `CURRENT` and in `AS_RECORDED` views after the
retraction, and still under `T` in views between `created_at` and `retracted_at`. Removing an edge can
create neither a cycle nor a longer chain, so retraction has no depth rule. A retracted redirect is
never reactivated (`redirect_already_retracted`); merging `S` into anything again is a new row.

**6.5 Effects the merge must name.** The application performs, in the same database transaction as
the insert or retraction, the projection refresh of §9.2 for the affected transactions and the
`period_aggregates` recompute of §5.5, and the client's confirmation states the consequences before
the user confirms: (a) **sharing** — a grant on `S` admits nothing after the merge (no posted transaction
projects to `S`) and is shown to the grantor as inert rather than silently redirected; the grantor may
create a grant on `T`; a grant on `T` will admit the transactions that now resolve to `T`, which is
the grantor's own act and is named as "N people with access to T will see these" (`PenniLogic/api#34`,
`PenniLogic/contracts#8`); (b) **budgets** — a budget on `S` measures `resolve(S)` from now on and, when
a budget on `T` exists, both now track `T`, which the budget surface presents as a duplicate to resolve
(`PenniLogic/api#41`); (c) **rules** — a merchant rule whose category is `S` keeps its row and resolves at
application time (§4.1); (d) **AI** — the model receives the current category list, in which `S` no
longer appears (§10.5). None of these is a data migration.

### 7. System, user and uncategorised

**7.1 System and user categories.** A `SYSTEM` row is the owner's copy of a reference key: its identity
(`system_key`), parent, nature and default label are the seed file's, it is created for every owner
(§2.2), it cannot be re-parented, and it may be renamed (a personal label), re-coloured, archived or
merged **by its owner** like any other of the owner's categories, because every redirect and every
allocation is owner-scoped. A `USER` row is the owner's own: encrypted name, chosen icon and colour
from the registries, optional parent. Neither kind is shared with or visible to another owner:
"team-wide custom categories" are a non-goal of `PenniLogic/api#9` and of this record, and a grantee
learns a category only through the grant that names it (§9.3). Rows of both kinds are identified by
their `id`; the wire also carries `system_key` so clients can localise and so an AI result or an
import can name a system category portably.

**7.2 One level of hierarchy, fixed at creation.** `parent_id` is set when a category is created and
never changes (a request to change it is `category_parent_immutable`); a parent has no parent and no
active redirect when the child is created (C11); a child's nature is its parent's. Re-parenting is a merge:
create the category under the new parent and redirect the old one into it, so the old grouping stays
true in `AS_RECORDED` views and the new one applies in `CURRENT`. Roll-up is `group` of §5.1, computed
over resolved categories, and §6.2 rule 6 keeps it one level deep after any merge. A grant is on one
exact category (ADR-018 §4.3's token names one `category_id`); a grant "on a parent" is materialised by
the sharing service as one grant row for the parent and one per **current** child, and a child created
later is not granted until the grantor grants it — default deny (ADR-005; `PenniLogic/contracts#8`).

**7.3 Uncategorised is an absence, not a row.** There is no "Uncategorised" category: the uncategorised
state is a line with `category_id IS NULL` (§3.4) or, for a transaction that has not yet posted, no
assignment at all. Consequences: it has no identifier to grant, so ADR-018 §4.3's predicate — whose
concatenation with a `NULL` `category_id` is `NULL` — admits it to nobody but its owner in detail and in
aggregate (`period_aggregates.category_id IS NULL` rows exist for the owner's own report and are never
admitted); it never appears in the AI's or the import's category list; the clarification inbox lists
transactions whose head has an uncategorised line (`PenniLogic/android#12`: "low-confidence and
uncategorised"); and the report bucket is keyed by `null` (§5.4). Making it a row would make it
grantable and would give every owner a category that means "I do not know", which is not a
classification.

**7.4 Archive versus merge versus delete.** *Archive* sets `is_archived` (mutable metadata): the category
disappears from pickers and the posting default (a merchant hint or rule that resolves to an archived
category falls through to the uncategorised line), accepts no new `USER`, `RULE`, `IMPORT` or `AI`
allocation (C4: `category_archived`), keeps every existing line, and appears in reports exactly as
before; un-archiving is the same column written back. *Merge* is §6. *Delete* is refused
(`category_delete_unsupported`), whether or not the category has lines — there is no `DELETE` grant
(§2.7) — and the refusal names merge and archive, which satisfies `PenniLogic/api#9`'s outcome and its
archive-in-use test.

### 8. Instalments, transfers, refunds and system-posted kinds

**8.1 An instalment.** A debt is a `LIABILITY` account whose balance is derived (ADR-017 §3.2). An
equated monthly instalment of ₹10,000.00 with a stated split of ₹8,000.00 principal and ₹2,000.00
interest, paid from a bank account, is one transaction:

```text
Asset (bank)        −1000000
Liability (loan)     +800000     -- principal: reduces owed; LIABILITY, not categorisable
EXPENSE (system)     +200000     -- interest: the only categorisable entry → debt.interest
```

Its head has one line: `debt.interest`, `+200000`. Spending in the period rises by ₹2,000.00 under
`debt.interest`; `owed` falls by ₹8,000.00 in ADR-017 §3.1's derivation; **no report anywhere shows
₹8,000.00 or ₹10,000.00 as spending**, because a `LIABILITY` entry is never categorisable (§1.2). A
late fee or a processing charge stated by the lender is a further `EXPENSE` entry under `debt.fees`.
The same holds for a credit card: a purchase on the card credits the card's `LIABILITY` account and
debits `EXPENSE` (categorised under the purchase's category); the bill payment is a transfer; the
card's interest and fees are `EXPENSE` entries under `debt.interest` and `debt.fees`
(`PenniLogic/api#69`).

**8.2 When the split is not stated.** A captured "EMI ₹10,000 debited" without a principal/interest split
posts as a **transfer**: `Asset −1000000`, `Liability +1000000`, no categorisable entry, nothing in any
category report, and no `needs_review` flag for that reason alone. The interest is a separate fact
recorded when a source states it — the lender's SMS or statement import (T-CON-10, `PenniLogic/api#10`)
as `Liability −200000`, `EXPENSE +200000` under `debt.interest`; or, after ADR-017 §3.4's reconciliation
surfaces a discrepancy and the user identifies its cause, the further adjustment ADR-017 §3.4 permits,
whose `EXPENSE` entry is categorised the same way. The two shapes yield identical balances and identical
category totals. The debt engine's amortisation schedule and projections (`PenniLogic/api#12`,
`PenniLogic/api#13`) **never post the interest entry and never create an assignment** (ADR-017 §6.2:
projections never write to the ledger); they may show the expected split and prompt the user to record
the fact. A statement or capture that states the split is a ledger fact fixed by the entries; there is
no `interest_minor` column and no percentage split stored anywhere (`InstalmentClassificationTest`).

**8.3 Refunds.** A consumer refund is a new `STANDARD` fact, never a reversal (ADR-017 §2.6;
`PenniLogic/api#66`, T-CON-13). Its categorisable entry is a **credit to the `EXPENSE` account**
(`Asset +50000`, `EXPENSE −50000`), so it is a negative line in an `EXPENSE`-nature category and reduces
that category's spending; C3 makes it impossible to place under an `INCOME` category, which is
`PenniLogic/api#66`'s "without becoming income" at the dimension level. At posting, a refund linked to
a purchase receives `SYSTEM`/`REFUND_LINK` lines carrying the purchase head's categories in position
order with amounts `allocate(shares = the purchase's absolute line amounts, ordering = position)` over
the refund entry (a partial refund is proportional and exact); an unlinked refund receives the posting
default; a later or corrected link posts a new `REFUND_LINK` assignment, which supersedes a default
and never a `USER` head (C7).

**8.4 System-posted kinds.** `OPENING_BALANCE` and `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT` post to `EQUITY` and an
`ASSET` or `LIABILITY` account (ADR-017 §3.2, §3.4) and have no categorisable entry. `REVERSAL` and
`REPOST` follow §4.4. An `EXCHANGE_LEG` (E30, `PenniLogic/api#46`) is categorisable only through its
`FX_FEE` entry, whose posting default is `fees.foreign_exchange` (§2.1); its clearing and asset entries
are outside the dimension, as ADR-017 §4.1 already excludes clearing accounts from category reports.

**8.5 Transfers are not categorisable.** A transfer has entries on `ASSET` and `LIABILITY` accounts only
(`PenniLogic/api#8`); an assignment request for it is refused as `no_categorisable_entry`, which is
the "re-categorisation guard" `PenniLogic/api#8` asks for, enforced structurally rather than by an
edit-endpoint rule. An ATM withdrawal reconciled into a cash account (`PenniLogic/api#44`) is such a
transfer; the later cash spending is categorised when it is recorded (`TransferNotCategorisableTest`).

### 9. The projection column and its sharing consequences

**9.1 `projection(t)`, stated once.** For a posted transaction `t` with head `H = head(t, ∞)`:

```text
D(t)          = { line_category(l, ∞) : l ∈ lines(H) }                 -- the set of resolved categories, NULL kept
movement(t)   = Σ |e.amount_minor| over every entry e of t  /  2       -- exact: debits equal credits (ADR-017 Z1)
explained(t)  = Σ |e.amount_minor| over e ∈ E_c(t)  =  movement(t)     -- the categorisable entries account for all money moved
projection(t) = c   when D(t) = {c}, c IS NOT NULL and explained(t)
              = NULL otherwise
```

`transactions.category_id` **is** `projection(t)` for every posted row and `NULL` for every non-posted
row (C9, `category_projection_equivalence`). The application writes the column through — at posting,
on every new assignment, and for the affected transactions of every redirect insert or retraction
(§9.2) — and the deferred trigger refuses a `COMMIT` in which any touched row disagrees, so the column
can never be edited into a value the history does not derive: a bare `UPDATE transactions SET
category_id` by the runtime role is admitted by ADR-018 §4.1's grant and rejected at `COMMIT` unless it
happens to equal the projection. This is the shape ADR-017 §2.1 gives `transactions.status = 'reversed'`
(a projection of the `reverses` link, guarded by `status_reversed_equivalence`), and it is how ADR-018's
"ADR-016 must expose the effective category of a transaction as the column `transactions.category_id`"
is satisfied while the truth stays append-only. ADR-017 §2.4 and ADR-018 §4.1 list the column as
mutable descriptive metadata; this record tightens that to "mutable only to its derivation" (§15).

**9.2 The affected set.** After a new assignment: its transaction. After a redirect insert `S → T` or a
retraction: every posted transaction of the owner whose head has a line naming a category that
resolved to `S` before the change or resolves to `S` after it — found through the owner's head lines,
never by scanning the ledger; the trigger evaluates C9 over exactly that set plus every transaction
row touched in the database transaction. The rows refreshed per change are observed (§12).

**9.3 What a grantee sees, and what a merge changes.** ADR-018 §4.3's `grant_mediated_read` admits a
transaction whose `(owner_id, category_id, 'detail')` token is in the grantee's grant set and whose
`needs_review = false`; `grant_mediated_aggregate_read` admits `period_aggregates` rows by
`(owner_id, category_id, 'aggregate')`. Under `projection(t)`:

- a **single-category, fully explained** transaction — the ordinary purchase, income or refund — is
  admitted to a detail grantee on that category, together with its entries (ADR-018 §4.3: amounts and
  opaque account identifiers, no names); its reversal projects the same category and is admitted too,
  so the grantee's view nets to zero as the owner's does;
- a **split** transaction, an **uncategorised** one, one whose categorisable entries **do not explain
  its movement** (the three-entry instalment of §8.1: a `debt.interest` grantee would otherwise see
  the principal and the payment; a salary with a tax entry; an exchange leg) and every **candidate**
  project `NULL` and are admitted to no grantee in detail — a tightening ADR-018 permits ("they may
  tighten one"); their lines still count in each category's `period_aggregates` row, so an aggregate
  grantee on `debt.interest` sees the interest total and nothing else. Aggregate and detail are
  separately granted permissions (`architecture/01-domain-model.md` §3 rule 4), and a grantee is not
  promised that the detail rows sum to the aggregate;
- the four dimension tables have **no grant policy**: a grantee never reads a `categories`,
  `category_assignments`, `category_allocations` or `category_redirects` row, learns a category only
  as the opaque `category_id` and `system_key` the grant itself carries (T-CON-04's grant shape;
  how a user category's label reaches a grantee is `PenniLogic/contracts#8`'s and ADR-021's), and never
  sees an allocation line or an assignment's source or history.

A merge `S → T` therefore changes sharing only through `projection`: transactions that projected `S`
now project `T` and become visible to `T`'s detail grantees and, through the recomputed aggregates, to
`T`'s aggregate grantees — the grantor's own act, named in the confirmation (§6.5) — while grants on
`S` admit nothing and are shown as inert; **no grant row is created, widened or re-pointed by a merge
or a retraction**, and ADR-018 §4.3's predicates are untouched (`CategoryGrantProjectionTest`, which
extends ADR-018 §12's `rls_needs_review_and_null_category_never_shared`).

### 11. Security and privacy consequences

- **Chosen.** Category identifiers, kinds, keys, natures, parents, allocation amounts and every timestamp
  are *constrained* columns under `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` and the low-privilege role (ADR-018 §1.1,
  §1.3): the database must sum lines, compare natures, follow redirects and evaluate ADR-018 §4.3's
  predicate, and a constraint the database cannot execute is not a constraint. User category names —
  which can reveal a health condition, a relationship or a debt — are *encrypted* under the owner's
  DEK (ADR-018 §1.2's row for this record) and never blind-indexed; a `SYSTEM` row's identity is a
  reference key that reveals only which seed entry it copies. Append-only assignments and redirects
  with withheld `UPDATE`/`DELETE` bound a compromised API process to *adding* dated, attributable rows
  (`assigned_at` and `created_at` within `PT5M` of the database clock, `source`, `reason`,
  `provenance_ref`, ADR-015 idempotency records), never to rewriting what a report showed; the
  projection column cannot be forged past `COMMIT` (C9). Sharing is default-deny at every step: the
  dimension tables have no grant policy; an uncategorised, split, unexplained or unposted transaction
  projects `NULL` and is never admitted; a merge never creates, widens or re-points a grant, and the
  widening a merge causes through the target's existing grants is the grantor's own act, named before
  confirmation (§6.5, §9.3). The AI path receives only the owner's own category list and may write
  only an `AI`-ranked assignment naming one of those identifiers (§10.5); no model output string is
  stored on a dimension row. Signals (§12) carry counts, sources, reasons, constraint identifiers and
  depths — never an amount, a name, a key or an identifier of a row. Erasure follows the owner: names
  are unreadable once the DEK is destroyed (ADR-006, ADR-018 §6.7), and the constrained residue —
  opaque category identifiers, amounts and instants under an unlinkable `owner_id` — is the same
  class of residue ADR-018 §9 accepts for the ledger; the interplay with shared data is ADR-021's.
  **Accepted residual:** `assigned_at` and `created_at` reveal the rhythm of a user's categorising and
  reorganising per opaque owner, as `booked_at` reveals activity (ADR-017 §7); an aggregate grantee on
  a category learns that category's total including split contributions it cannot see in detail.
- **Rejected.** A category-as-account model needs the runtime role to create and archive rows of a
  ledger table on every category edit and lets an account grant disclose a balance. Mutable category
  rows and an in-place `category_id` rewrite require the standing `UPDATE` the runtime role already
  holds on that column to be trusted without a derivation to check it against, so a compromise could
  re-categorise history — including what a grantee sees — without leaving a fact. A destructive merge
  (`UPDATE ... SET category_id = T; DELETE FROM categories WHERE id = S`) makes every idempotent
  replay, export and shared aggregate that named `S` irreproducible and is irreversible. Auto-expanding
  a grant on a merged-away category to its target silently widens what a third party can read. An
  "Uncategorised" row would be grantable.

### 15. Migration note and consistency with the records this one cites

No ledger or category table exists yet in `PenniLogic/api` (ADR-017 §11), so there is no data
migration: `PenniLogic/api#2`'s migration creates the two `system_role` values and the nullable
`transactions.category_id`, and `PenniLogic/api#9`'s migration creates the five tables, the composite
foreign key on `transactions.category_id` and the triggers of §2.6 — one migration at a time.
`PenniLogic/api#9`'s "introduce category splits as an additive schema change alongside the existing
single-category assignment" is moot: there is one storage shape from the first migration. What changes
relative to the research and ticket prose: `Expense:Groceries` in `architecture/01-domain-model.md` §2.1
reads as "the owner's `EXPENSE` account, categorised Groceries"; the domain model's §7 "last-writer-wins
on user-editable metadata (category, notes)" is realised as appended assignments whose last committed
row is the head; `PenniLogic/api#7`'s "expense category debited", `PenniLogic/api#8`'s
re-categorisation guard, `PenniLogic/api#9`'s two schemas, amount-bearing split error and "no sensitive
data" sentence, and `PenniLogic/api#66`'s category treatment are amended as §10 states.

**Consistency with the records this one cites.** This record was written against ADR-015 as merged
(`b91b901`, `PenniLogic/docs#150`), ADR-017 as merged (`55bd5cc`, `PenniLogic/docs#154`), ADR-018 as
merged (`08c2c14`, `PenniLogic/docs#147`) and ADR-020 as merged (`8d15a4c`, `PenniLogic/docs#151`); every
section number cited here was verified at those commits, and the pins are repeated in §14. It
**extends** two of them additively, each a coordination item for that record's next revision under its
own procedure, not a contradiction:

- **ADR-017** — (1) §1.6 gains the `system_role` values `INCOME` and `EXPENSE`, which §12 admits
  additively; (2) §2.4's `transactions.category_id` stays column-level updatable and is bound to
  `projection(t)` by `category_projection_equivalence` (§9.1), the shape §2.1 gives `status`; (3) §6.1's
  posting and correction helper (`PenniLogic/api#7`) write the initial, mirror and carry assignments in
  the posting database transaction (§4.1, §4.4); (4) the accounts API accepts `ASSET` and `LIABILITY`

  category semantics ADR-016 fixes" is satisfied by §1.2–§1.3 and §5.1; §13's deferral of "category
  semantics and how a category total is defined over entries" to this record is discharged here. This
  record introduces **no stored balance, no cached total that is not an ADR-017 §3.5 read model, and
  no edit of any amount**: every category figure is derived at read time from allocation lines that
  never change, and the only mutable column it touches (`transactions.category_id`) is bound to a

### 16. Rollout and rollback

Adopted on merge; no runtime rollout. §1–§9 bind `PenniLogic/api#2`, `PenniLogic/api#6`,
`PenniLogic/api#7`, `PenniLogic/api#8`, `PenniLogic/api#9` and every ticket of §10 immediately; the
contract components of §14 are published by `PenniLogic/contracts#1`, `#2`, `#12` and `#21` and the
three generated clients are regenerated from them. Until `PenniLogic/api#9` lands, no category can be
created, no assignment posted and no report rendered; nothing exists to reverse.

**Rollback position.** Once adopted, **additive** evolution needs no new record: a new system key, seed
version, label locale, icon or colour; a new `reason` with its `source` compatibility and
`provenance_ref` rule; a new refusal reason; a new mutable descriptive column on `categories`; a
report offered in `AS_RECORDED` that was `CURRENT`-only; a **larger** `redirect_max_depth`, provided
every resolver reads it from §14. Changing the category-is-not-an-account rule, the two system
accounts, the append-only rule, the exact-sum or remainder rule, the default view or the meaning of
either view, the terminal-target or cycle rule, a **smaller** depth bound, the precedence ranks, the
projection rule or what it admits to a grantee, the one-level hierarchy, the uncategorised
representation, or the instalment classification requires a **superseding record that states the
migration cost**: every assignment and redirect row's interpretation, both views of every historical
report and export, the projection column and its predicate consequences, every published component of
§14, three generated clients, the `period_aggregates` read model and the conformance fixtures of §13.
Reversing this record after categorisation data exists is a preservation migration, not a revert
(`adr/LAYOUT.md`).

### 17. Non-goals

Implementation, which belongs to §10's tickets; the seed list's content, labels and the icon and colour
registries (`PenniLogic/api#9`, reviewed for the Indian market); the merchant-normalisation and
rule-matching engine and recurring detection (`PenniLogic/api#19`); the AI model, its confidence
threshold, routing and evaluation gate (`PenniLogic/ai-service#5`, `PenniLogic/ai-service#7`) and its
egress (ADR-022); the clarification-item, confidence-band and rollback request shapes (T-CON-02,
T-CON-10); the sharing grant shapes, bounds and how a user-category label reaches a grantee (T-CON-04,
ADR-021); erasure of dimension and shared data (ADR-021); budget arithmetic, rollover and safe-to-spend
(`PenniLogic/api#41`, `PenniLogic/api#42`, T-CON-08); the debt engine's statement-line kinds and
amortisation (`PenniLogic/api#10`–`#13`, T-CON-01, T-CON-10); the money wire form, `allocate`'s
implementation, instants and idempotency (ADR-015); the ledger's entries, corrections and derivations
(ADR-017); column classes, keys and policies (ADR-018); free-form tags, merchants and notes, which are
descriptive metadata and not this dimension; team-wide or shared custom categories; a hierarchy deeper
than one level; category-level permissions other than ADR-005's grants.

**Revisit if:** a real user cohort exhausts the eight-hop bound in ordinary use (`redirect_depth_exceeded`
above a rounding-error rate), in which case the bound is raised additively under §16 or, if chains
themselves are the problem, a superseding statement introduces dated path compression without losing
the as-recorded truth; the product needs a category that spans
income and expense (a "reimbursements" bucket), which would need a nature-neutral kind and a new sign
rule; a sharing model wants split transactions admitted in detail, which is a tightening of the
projection to "every line's category granted" that a later record may adopt; or the product decides to
share categories across owners, which would make `categories` a grant-mediated table and needs a new
record.
