## ADR-017 — Ledger currency, foreign-exchange reservation and derived debt balances
**Status:** ACCEPTED · 2026-09-30

**Decision.** Six rules bind the ledger schema, the debt engine, the split ledger, every aggregate the
API returns and the later multi-currency work:

1. **Every ledger transaction is single-currency and balances to zero in that currency.** An entry is
   `(transaction_id, account_id, currency, amount_minor)` with `amount_minor` a signed `BIGINT` count
   of ISO 4217 minor units (ADR-001, ADR-015 §1.3), positive for a debit and negative for a credit,
   never zero. For every `(transaction_id, currency)` the sum of `amount_minor` is exactly zero,
   enforced by a PostgreSQL **deferred constraint trigger** evaluated at `COMMIT`; the single currency
   of a transaction, and the pairing of every entry with its account's currency, are enforced by
   composite foreign keys. The MVP ledger admits one currency, `INR`; admission is data
   (`ledger_currencies`) and widens additively under E30.
2. **Nothing in the ledger is ever edited in place.** A correction is a *reversal* transaction that
   negates every entry of the original plus, when the fact must be restated, a *re-post* transaction,
   the pair committed together with a recorded reason and links (`reverses`, `replaces`). The runtime
   database role holds no `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on `entries`, on the ledger columns of `transactions`,
   on `statement_snapshots` or on `exchange_groups`.
3. **No stored balance is authoritative.** A debt's balance is the derived sum of its liability
   account's entries at a stated position; there is no balance column anywhere. A lender statement is
   recorded as an immutable **statement snapshot** (statement date, statement balance, source,
   evidence reference); a difference between it and the derived balance is recorded as an explicit
   **reconciliation adjustment** transaction that references the snapshot, never by altering an entry.
4. **Foreign exchange is reserved, not built.** The schema reserves a `CLEARING` account type with one
   system clearing account per (owner, currency), an `exchange_groups` table and the
   `transactions.exchange_group_id` link. A cross-currency movement is a **group of exactly two
   single-currency transactions**, one per currency, each balancing in its own currency through its
   clearing account. The group stores the quoted rate (exact, ten fraction digits), its source, its
   instant and an evidence reference; every fee is an explicit entry to a fee expense account; the
   group is atomic under a deferred constraint trigger and is reversed only as a whole, by a paired
   reversal group.
5. **No scalar total ever mixes currencies.** Every aggregate across accounts is a per-currency
   breakdown with a stated derivation; a converted figure carries the quote that produced it and never
   replaces a stored amount. A historical figure is never recomputed from a later rate.
6. **Only the reservations land now.** The ledger migration (`PenniLogic/api#2`) creates the reserved
   structures empty and constrained; the exchange write path, wider currency admission and reporting
   currencies are E30 (`PenniLogic/docs#33`), phase P6 Moat. Until then the one-row admitted set makes
   an exchange group structurally impossible to insert.

**Why.** ADR-002 requires `SUM(entries.amount) = 0` per transaction in the database, and ADR-001
requires every amount to carry a currency. Those two rules collide the moment one transaction holds
two currencies: a sum across `INR` and `USD` minor units is not a number, so the invariant would have
to be dropped or faked. Making the constraint *per currency* and making every transaction
single-currency keeps the invariant exact, keeps it executable by the database (ADR-018 §1.3: ledger
money stays a constrained `BIGINT` precisely so this can run), and leaves a place for exchange later
without touching any stored amount. The debt engine has the mirror-image problem: a stored
`current_balance` is a second source of truth that drifts from the entries (ADR-010: "never sync a
cached balance — always recompute from entries"), and a lender statement is a third. Deriving the
balance and recording the statement as evidence with an explicit adjustment leaves exactly one source
of truth and a reconstructible history. In-place edits are rejected for the same reason ADR-002 made
the ledger append-only and ADR-015 made replays receipts of the original effect: a figure reported,
replayed or exported yesterday must still be derivable today.

### 1. The entry shape and the zero-sum constraint

**1.1 Entry shape and sign convention.** An entry is one row of `entries`:

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `UUID` | Primary key (UUIDv7, ADR-018). |
| `transaction_id` | `UUID` | The transaction this entry belongs to. |
| `owner_id` | `UUID` | Denormalised owner for the RLS predicate (ADR-018 §1.2); composite foreign key to `transactions (id, owner_id)`. |
| `account_id` | `UUID` | The account posted to; composite foreign keys to `accounts (id, owner_id)` and `accounts (id, currency)`. |
| `currency` | `CHAR(3)` | ISO 4217 alphabetic code; composite foreign key to `transactions (id, currency)`. |
| `amount_minor` | `BIGINT` | Minor units; `amount_minor <> 0`; `amount_minor >= -9223372036854775807` (ADR-015 §1.3 excludes `-2^63` so negation never overflows). |

**Positive is a debit, negative is a credit.** Asset and expense accounts carry a natural debit
(positive) balance; liability, income and equity accounts carry a natural credit (negative) balance.
The examples in `architecture/01-domain-model.md` §2.1 already use this convention
(`Expense:Groceries +1200`, `Liability:HDFC Card -1200`). The amount a debtor owes on a liability
account is therefore `-balance`, and because `-2^63` is excluded that negation is always
representable. A zero entry is forbidden: an absent fee is no entry, not a zero entry. Every
`amount_minor` crosses the wire as ADR-015's `Money` object; the sign is semantic (ADR-015 §1.6).

**1.2 The constraint, stated precisely.** For the tables `accounts`, `transactions` and `entries`:

```text
Z1  ∀ (transaction_id, currency):  SUM(entries.amount_minor) = 0
      over the entries of that transaction in that currency, evaluated at COMMIT.
Z2  ∀ entry: entries.currency = transactions.currency of its transaction        (one currency per transaction)
Z3  ∀ entry: entries.currency = accounts.currency  of its account                (account currency pairing)
Z4  ∀ entry: entries.owner_id = transactions.owner_id = accounts.owner_id        (own accounts only)
Z5  ∀ entry: amount_minor <> 0  ∧  amount_minor >= -(2^63 - 1)
Z6  ∀ (transaction_id, currency) that has at least one entry: COUNT(entries) >= 2
      (follows from Z1 and Z5 but is asserted so the failure is named).
Z7  ∀ transaction: COUNT(entries) = transactions.entry_count, where entry_count is written once at
      posting and never changes — a posted transaction's composition is sealed (§1.5).
```

Z2–Z5 are declarative and immediate. Z1, Z6 and Z7 are deferred constraints; Z1 and Z6 share one
trigger:

```sql
-- entries: the composite keys make Z2, Z3 and Z4 declarative; the CHECK is Z5.
CREATE TABLE entries (
  id             UUID    PRIMARY KEY,
  transaction_id UUID    NOT NULL,
  owner_id       UUID    NOT NULL,
  account_id     UUID    NOT NULL,
  currency       CHAR(3) NOT NULL,
  amount_minor   BIGINT  NOT NULL
                 CHECK (amount_minor <> 0 AND amount_minor >= -9223372036854775807),
  FOREIGN KEY (transaction_id, owner_id) REFERENCES transactions (id, owner_id),  -- Z4 (ADR-018 §1.2)
  FOREIGN KEY (transaction_id, currency) REFERENCES transactions (id, currency),  -- Z2
  FOREIGN KEY (account_id, owner_id)     REFERENCES accounts (id, owner_id),      -- Z4
  FOREIGN KEY (account_id, currency)     REFERENCES accounts (id, currency)       -- Z3
);
-- transactions and accounts each carry UNIQUE (id, owner_id) and UNIQUE (id, currency) so the
-- composite keys above are legal; accounts.currency and transactions.currency reference
-- ledger_currencies (code), the admitted set of §1.4.

CREATE FUNCTION ledger.assert_transaction_balanced() RETURNS trigger
LANGUAGE plpgsql AS $$
DECLARE
  tx UUID := COALESCE(NEW.transaction_id, OLD.transaction_id);
BEGIN
  -- SUM(BIGINT) is NUMERIC in PostgreSQL, so the aggregate itself cannot overflow.
  PERFORM 1
     FROM entries
    WHERE transaction_id = tx
    GROUP BY currency
   HAVING SUM(amount_minor) <> 0 OR COUNT(*) < 2;
  IF FOUND THEN
    RAISE EXCEPTION USING ERRCODE = '23514',            -- check_violation
                          MESSAGE = 'ledger.transaction_zero_sum',
                          DETAIL  = format('transaction %s', tx);   -- an opaque id, never an amount
  END IF;
  RETURN NULL;
END $$;

CREATE CONSTRAINT TRIGGER transaction_zero_sum
  AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE OR DELETE ON entries
  DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED
  FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION ledger.assert_transaction_balanced();
```

A transaction with no entries has no ledger effect and passes Z1 (the aggregate has no group); the
ingestion lifecycle's `candidate` and `suppressed_by_link` rows (ADR-018 §7.3) are exactly such
transactions and acquire entries only when posted, in the same database transaction that sets
`status = 'posted'` and `entry_count`. **Z7 closes the one gap append-only leaves open**: without
it, a balanced pair of entries could be appended to a year-old posted transaction in a later
database transaction and Z1 would still hold, rewriting history under an old `effective_date`. A
second deferred constraint trigger, `transaction_sealed`, fires for every transaction whose entries
or `entry_count` were touched and requires `COUNT(entries) = entry_count` at `COMMIT`; because
`entry_count` can be written exactly once (§1.5), nothing can be added to a posted transaction
afterwards, and nothing can be removed because `DELETE` is withheld. The same trigger requires a
`REVERSAL`'s entries to be the exact negation of its target's — same account multiset, negated
amounts (`reversal_exact_negation`) — so "reversed" always means "restored".

**1.3 Why a `CHECK` cannot do it, and why this mechanism can.** A `CHECK` constraint is evaluated
per row against that row's own columns; PostgreSQL forbids subqueries in it, so it cannot see the
other entries of the transaction, and it is evaluated at the moment each row is written, when the
transaction is necessarily unbalanced between its first and last entry. `UNIQUE`, `FOREIGN KEY` and
`EXCLUDE` cannot express a sum, and SQL's `CREATE ASSERTION` is not implemented in PostgreSQL. A
**constraint trigger declared `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`** is the one mechanism that (a) sees
every row of the transaction, (b) runs at `COMMIT` after all entries exist, (c) makes the `COMMIT`
itself fail — the writer receives the error and nothing of the database transaction persists — and
(d) cannot be skipped by the writer: `SET CONSTRAINTS ... IMMEDIATE` only moves the check earlier,
`ALTER TABLE ... DISABLE TRIGGER` requires table ownership the runtime role does not have, and
`session_replication_role = replica` requires superuser, which no service credential holds
(ADR-018 §4.1, `T-PLT-05`). The research sketch in `architecture/03-stack-and-monetization.md` §12
already used this shape; this record fixes it, adds the per-currency grouping, the entry-count and
sign rules, and the composite keys. The same mechanism carries the split-share sum (§6.3) and the
exchange-group shape (§4.5). Implementation: `PenniLogic/api#2`.

**1.4 Admitted currencies.** ADR-015 §1.4 leaves "which currencies a ledger accepts" to this record.
The ledger admits exactly the codes present in `ledger_currencies`, a projection of the published
`currency-registry.v1.json` (`code`, `exponent`, `admitted_at`); `accounts.currency`,
`transactions.currency`, `exchange_groups.base_currency` and `exchange_groups.quote_currency`
reference it. **The MVP set is `{INR}`.** Accepting `JPY` or `KWD` at the codec for the conformance
fixtures (ADR-015 §7) is not admission: an account or transaction in a non-admitted registry currency
is refused by the API as `validation_rejected` with reason `currency_not_admitted` (`T-CON-12`) and,
should the application fail to refuse, by the foreign key. A captured candidate whose parsed
currency is not admitted is refused the same way by the ingestion endpoint (`T-CON-02`) and stays a
device-side raw event awaiting review; it never becomes a ledger transaction. Admission widens only
by inserting rows,
an additive migration owned by E30 (§4.9); a code is never removed while an account references it,
and the foreign key makes removal fail otherwise. Test environments insert further rows to exercise
the multi-currency conformance tests of §9.

**1.5 Transactions.** The columns this record forces on `transactions` (the rest — `status`, `source`,
`source_event_id`, `dedupe_key`, descriptive metadata — are ADR-018 §1.2's and the ingestion
contract's):

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `currency` | `CHAR(3)` | The one currency of every entry (Z2); references `ledger_currencies`. |
| `kind` | `TEXT` | Closed set `STANDARD`, `OPENING_BALANCE`, `REVERSAL`, `REPOST`, `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT`, `EXCHANGE_LEG` (§10). |
| `occurred_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | Effective instant from the source or the user (ADR-015 §3.1). |
| `booked_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | Server-assigned posting instant from the application clock; the as-recorded axis. |
| `value_date` | `DATE`, nullable | Date-only effective fact (ADR-015 §3.1): required for `OPENING_BALANCE` and `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT`, optional otherwise. |
| `reverses` | `UUID`, nullable, `UNIQUE` | Set on and only on a `REVERSAL`; the transaction whose entries this one negates. Composite foreign key `(reverses, owner_id, currency) → transactions (id, owner_id, currency)`: only the owner's own transaction, in the same currency. |
| `replaces` | `UUID`, nullable | Set on and only on a `REPOST`; the reversed transaction whose fact this one restates. Composite foreign key `(replaces, owner_id) → transactions (id, owner_id)`; not `UNIQUE`, because one wrong transaction may be restated as several (§2.2). |
| `entry_count` | `SMALLINT` | The sealed number of entries (Z7): `0` for a `candidate` or `suppressed_by_link` row, otherwise `>= 2`; written at posting and, for a candidate, changed exactly once from `0` when `status` moves to `posted` (a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger permits no other change and no return to `candidate`). |
| `reason_code` | `TEXT`, nullable | Required on `REVERSAL`, `REPOST` and `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT`: `USER_CORRECTION`, `DUPLICATE_LINK`, `RECONCILIATION`, `EXCHANGE_REVERSAL`, `ADMINISTRATIVE`. |
| `reason_note` | text, nullable | Free text; encrypted descriptive class (ADR-018 §1.2). |
| `reconciles_snapshot_id` | `UUID`, nullable, `UNIQUE` | Set on and only on a `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT` (§3.4). |
| `exchange_group_id` | `UUID`, nullable | Reserved; set on an `EXCHANGE_LEG` or on a `REVERSAL` of one (§4); `NULL` on every MVP row. |

`CHECK ((kind = 'REVERSAL') = (reverses IS NOT NULL))`, `CHECK ((kind = 'REPOST') = (replaces IS NOT
NULL))`, `CHECK ((kind = 'RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT') = (reconciles_snapshot_id IS NOT NULL))`,
`CHECK (kind <> 'EXCHANGE_LEG' OR exchange_group_id IS NOT NULL)`, `CHECK (exchange_group_id IS NULL
OR kind IN ('EXCHANGE_LEG', 'REVERSAL'))`, `CHECK (kind NOT IN ('OPENING_BALANCE',
'RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT') OR value_date IS NOT NULL)`, `CHECK (reverses IS DISTINCT FROM id AND
replaces IS DISTINCT FROM id)`. Every one of these columns is a **ledger fact**: the runtime role has
no `UPDATE` on any of them (§2.4), with the single, trigger-guarded exception of `entry_count`'s
one-time write at posting. The `effective_date` of a transaction, used by every as-of-date
derivation and period report, is `value_date` when present, otherwise the local calendar date of
`occurred_at` in the profile time zone under ADR-015 §3.3; ordinary transactions carry no
`value_date`, so nothing changes for them.

**1.6 Accounts.** `accounts.type` is the closed set `ASSET`, `LIABILITY`, `INCOME`, `EXPENSE`,
`EQUITY`, **`CLEARING`** (the sixth value is reserved by this record; `PenniLogic/api#2` adopts the
set from §10 rather than the five-value list in its original text). `accounts.currency` is
immutable after creation (ADR-018 §4.1 grants `UPDATE` only on `name` and `is_archived`). A nullable
`system_role` marks the system accounts the server creates lazily, one per (owner, role, currency),
enforced by a partial unique index:

| `system_role` | `type` | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `OPENING_BALANCE` | `EQUITY` | Counter-account of every opening balance (§3.2). |
| `RECONCILIATION` | `EQUITY` | Counter-account of every reconciliation adjustment (§3.4). |
| `FX_CLEARING` | `CLEARING` | The per-currency clearing account of an exchange (§4.1); the only accounts of type `CLEARING`. |
| `FX_FEE` | `EXPENSE` | The explicit exchange-fee account (§4.4). |

There is **no balance column** of any kind on `accounts` — the research sketch's
`initial_balance BIGINT` is rejected (§3.2, §11) — and a migration guard fails any deploy that adds a
writable column named like a balance to a ledger table (`PenniLogic/api#11`).

### 2. Corrections: reversal and re-post, never mutation

**2.1 Reversal.** A reversal of transaction `T` is a new transaction `R` with `kind = 'REVERSAL'`,
`reverses = T.id`, the same `owner_id` and `currency`, one entry per entry of `T` on the same account
with the negated `amount_minor` (representable by Z5), `occurred_at` and `booked_at` assigned at
posting, `value_date = T.value_date`, `entry_count = T.entry_count`, and a `reason_code` (plus an
optional `reason_note`). `R` balances by construction, and the `transaction_sealed` trigger rejects
at `COMMIT` a `REVERSAL` whose entries are not the exact negation of `T`'s (§1.2). `reverses` is
`UNIQUE`, so a transaction is reversed at most once; a
reversal may itself be reversed, which reinstates the original's effect — no special case, and every
derived figure stays correct. The effective status of a transaction is *derived*: `ACTIVE` when no
row reverses it, `REVERSED` otherwise. The `status = 'reversed'` value of ADR-018's lifecycle enum is
a denormalised projection of that link, written in the same database transaction as `R` (and
returned to `posted` when `R` is itself reversed), and a conformance test asserts the two agree;
consumers derive truth from the link.

**2.2 Re-post.** When the fact must be restated — wrong amount, wrong account, wrong date — the
correction is `R` plus a new transaction `P` with `kind = 'REPOST'`, `replaces = T.id`, carrying the
corrected entries. `R` and `P` commit in one database transaction; a deferred constraint trigger
rejects a `REPOST` whose `replaces` target has no reversal at `COMMIT`, so a re-post without its
reversal cannot exist. Several re-posts may replace one reversed transaction (one wrong ₹1,000
transaction restated as ₹600 and ₹400); a re-post may be in another currency when the original was
posted to the wrong account (E30). A date correction is a re-post (`occurred_at` is a ledger fact),
never an update. Both links are provenance: the derivation of any balance needs neither, because
every transaction's entries simply count.

**2.3 Who posts corrections.** The application posts `R`/`P` through one server-side helper
(`PenniLogic/api#7` implements it; `PenniLogic/api#2` supplies the linkage schema) under ADR-015's
idempotency rules: the correction is its own state-changing operation with its own key; replaying
it replays the receipt of the same `R`/`P`, never a second pair — and because `reverses` is `UNIQUE`,
a second pair is impossible even under a lost key. Ingestion's duplicate merge posts a reversal for a
non-surviving posted candidate (ADR-018 §7.3, `reason_code = 'DUPLICATE_LINK'`); an administrative
correction is posted by ADR-020's `admin_api` in the same transaction as its audit event, which
references `R.id` and never an amount (ADR-020 §4.4); `reason_code = 'ADMINISTRATIVE'`.

**2.4 What may change in place.** Only the descriptive metadata ADR-018 §4.1 enumerates for the
ledger tables — `transactions.needs_review`, `.status`, `.category_id` (semantics: ADR-016), `.note`,
`.description`, the merchant display and blind-index columns, the external-reference columns,
`.source_confidence`, the dedupe key while a row is still a `candidate`, `accounts.name` and
`accounts.is_archived` — plus the one-time `entry_count` write of §1.5. The runtime role
`pennilogic_app` has `SELECT` and `INSERT` on `entries`, `statement_snapshots` and
`exchange_groups`, column-level `UPDATE` on ledger tables for nothing but that list, and no `DELETE`
anywhere (ADR-018 §1.3, §4.1; ADR-018 governs the non-ledger tables). A change to any other column
§1.5 names is therefore a privilege error, not a policy breach. Metadata changes are visible through
the sync change feed (`T-SYN-01`) and do not alter any derived amount.

**2.5 Rejected alternatives, with reasons.**

- **`UPDATE` of an entry or a transaction's ledger columns** — rejected. (a) *Audit*: a balance,
  report, export or lender reconciliation produced earlier could no longer be reproduced, and
  ADR-020's chained log would have to carry before/after amounts to compensate, duplicating the
  ledger inside the audit store, which ADR-020 §4.4 forbids. (b) *Idempotency*: ADR-015 §4.4 replays
  the stored response as a receipt of the original effect and screens duplicates against committed
  records; a mutated record makes replays contradict state and makes the duplicate screen compare
  against a fact that no longer exists. (c) *Replay and sync*: ADR-010 and `T-SYN-01` replay
  append-only changes to offline clients and recompute balances from entries; an amount update
  requires a conflict rule for money that ADR-010 deliberately rules out ("you never edit a past
  debit, you post a correction"). (d) *Privilege*: append-only is the only shape the database can
  enforce by withholding `UPDATE`/`DELETE`; a "controlled edit" needs a standing `UPDATE` grant on the
  most sensitive table, which is indistinguishable from an attacker's rewrite (`PenniLogic/api#2`:
  a legitimate runtime need for `UPDATE` "would itself signal a design defect").
- **A soft-delete or `is_void` flag on entries** — rejected: it is an `UPDATE`, it makes every sum a
  filtered sum whose filter can be forgotten, and it breaks Z1 unless every entry of the transaction
  is voided together, which is a reversal expressed badly.
- **`DELETE` and re-insert** — rejected: `DELETE` is withheld, and history is lost.
- **An "edit window" (mutable for N minutes after posting)** — rejected: the window is a client-side
  convenience that the server cannot distinguish from a late rewrite; a client may present a quick
  "undo" that posts `R` (and `P`) underneath.

**2.6 Refunds and returns are not reversals.** A refund is a new economic fact — money came back —
recorded as a `STANDARD` transaction whose lifecycle extension (`T-CON-13`,
`PenniLogic/contracts#17`) may reference the original purchase; it never sets `reverses`. A reversal
exists only to correct the record. The two are distinguishable by `kind` in every response.

### 3. Debt balances are derived, statements are evidence

**3.1 The derivation.** For an account `A` and a set of entries `E(A)`:

```text
balance(A)            = Σ amount_minor over E(A)                                   -- current, all posted entries
balance_asof(A, D)    = Σ amount_minor over e ∈ E(A) with effective_date(tx(e)) <= D   -- D a LocalDate, §1.5 rule
balance_recorded(A,r) = Σ amount_minor over e ∈ E(A) with tx(e).booked_at <= r         -- as the ledger stood at instant r
owed(A)               = -balance(A)   for a LIABILITY account;  owed_asof(A, D) = -balance_asof(A, D)
```

All three sums are exact `BIGINT` arithmetic (overflow-checked; `SUM` is `NUMERIC` in SQL) over the
same rows the zero-sum trigger constrains. `effective_date` follows §1.5, computed on the server in
the profile time zone at read time (ADR-015 §3.3) so a changed time zone re-derives every as-of
figure consistently and rewrites nothing. `balance_recorded` exists because `booked_at` is stored on
every transaction: the ledger is bitemporal by construction, and "what did the app show on 12
September" is answerable without a snapshot. A derivation returned to a client carries its
**ledger position** — the as-of instant or date, the maximum `booked_at` included and the count of
entries summed — so the figure is reproducible and a client renders it without recomputing
(ADR-010).

**3.2 The debt entity and its account.** A debt (`PenniLogic/api#11`) links to **exactly one**
`LIABILITY` account (`debts.account_id UNIQUE`), whose `currency` is the debt's (ADR-018 §1.2 pairs
them). Its balance is `owed(A)`; its "current balance" in any response is a derived field marked as
derived (`T-CON-01`: computed balances distinguishable from snapshots). Declared terms — principal,
APR in basis points, minimum payment, term, fees (`PenniLogic/api#11`, ADR-018 classifies them
encrypted) — are inputs to the engine, not balances. The balance a user enters when creating a debt
is posted as an `OPENING_BALANCE` transaction with `value_date` = the opening date: `LIABILITY
-X` (credit) and the owner's `OPENING_BALANCE` equity account `+X` (debit). Likewise for every
asset account. There is no `initial_balance` column, no `current_balance` column and no
`principal_outstanding` column; a migration guard (`PenniLogic/api#11`) fails a deploy that introduces
a directly writable balance on any ledger table, and the privilege test (§9) proves the runtime role
could not write one anyway. Payments, interest actually charged and fees actually levied enter the
liability account only as entries — from SMS/notification capture, statement import or manual entry
— never from the engine's projection (§6.2).

**3.3 Statement snapshots.** A lender's (or any institution's) statement is recorded as one immutable
row of `statement_snapshots`:

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `UUID` | Primary key. |
| `owner_id`, `account_id`, `currency` | `UUID`, `UUID`, `CHAR(3)` | Composite foreign keys to `accounts (id, owner_id)` and `accounts (id, currency)`. |
| `statement_date` | `DATE` | The statement's own date; a date-only fact, no time invented (ADR-015 §3.1). |
| `statement_balance_minor` | `BIGINT` | The closing balance the statement shows, **in ledger sign** (§1.1): a card statement showing ₹10,512.40 owed is stored as `-1051240`; a credit balance on a card is positive. Range as Z5 without the non-zero rule. |
| `source` | `TEXT` | `MANUAL` or `IMPORT`. |
| `evidence_ref` | `UUID`, nullable | Opaque identifier of the import artefact or attachment (`T-CON-10`); never statement text, never a lender credential (ADR-004, ADR-013). |
| `captured_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | When the user or importer captured it. |
| `booked_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | Server-assigned. |

The runtime role has `SELECT` and `INSERT` only. A snapshot never changes a balance: recording it is a
pure evidence write. The debt resource exposes the statement figure in owed form (`-statement_balance`
for a liability), with the mapping stated once in `T-CON-01`; the storage sign matches the ledger so
the discrepancy arithmetic below has no sign conversion in it.

**3.4 Recording a discrepancy.** Reconciling a snapshot `S` for account `A`:

```text
derived = balance_asof(A, S.statement_date)
delta   = S.statement_balance_minor - derived
```

If `delta = 0` the snapshot stands as evidence of an exact reconciliation and nothing is posted. If
`delta <> 0` the difference is recorded — as a distinct, idempotent, user-confirmed operation
(`PenniLogic/api#11` decides how the client offers it) — as one **reconciliation adjustment**
transaction `J` with `kind = 'RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT'`, `reason_code = 'RECONCILIATION'`,
`reconciles_snapshot_id = S.id`, `value_date = S.statement_date`, `occurred_at` and `booked_at` at
posting, and exactly two entries:

```text
entry 1:  account A                           amount_minor = +delta
entry 2:  owner's RECONCILIATION equity account (same currency)  amount_minor = -delta
```

After `J` commits, `balance_asof(A, S.statement_date) = S.statement_balance_minor` exactly. Worked
example in INR: derived owed ₹10,000.00 (`balance = -1000000`), statement owed ₹10,512.40
(`-1051240`); `delta = -51240`; entry 1 posts `-51240` to the liability (a credit — more is owed),
entry 2 posts `+51240` to `RECONCILIATION`; the two sum to zero and the derived owed becomes
₹10,512.40. `reconciles_snapshot_id` is `UNIQUE`, so one snapshot produces at most one adjustment; a
disputed adjustment is reversed (§2.1, `reason_code = 'RECONCILIATION'`) and the statement, if it is
to be applied again, is captured as a new snapshot. Capturing the same statement twice is harmless:
the second reconciliation derives `delta = 0` and posts nothing. The counter-account is **equity**, not an
expense or income: the cause of a discrepancy — unrecorded interest, an unrecorded payment, a
lender's own correction — is unknown at reconciliation time, so the adjustment states a change in
net position and nothing about spending; once the user identifies the cause they may post a further
adjustment moving the amount to the right account. Because `value_date` is the statement date,
period reports attribute the adjustment to the statement's period while the transaction list shows
it at the instant it was made. The reconciliation state of a snapshot is derived at read time:
`EXACT` (`delta = 0`), `ADJUSTED` (an active `J` exists), `UNRECONCILED` (`delta <> 0` and no active
`J`); `T-CON-01` publishes the shape. Because `effective_date` follows the *current* profile time
zone (ADR-015 §3.3), a later zone change can move a near-midnight transaction across the statement
date and re-open a reconciliation as `UNRECONCILED`; that is the derived truth, the user reconciles
again, and nothing stored changes. `PenniLogic/api#11` emits the daily count of adjustments split
by direction (§8).

**3.5 Read models are permitted only as non-authoritative caches.** `period_aggregates` (ADR-018
§1.2, `T-FAM-05`), a materialised balance table or an in-memory cache may exist when, and only when:
(a) it is written solely by recomputing the derivation of §3.1 from entries, never by a client and
never by any other write path; (b) it records the ledger position it was computed at; (c) a
scheduled reconciliation query compares it with the derivation and any non-zero difference alerts
(`PenniLogic/api#2` observability; §8); (d) it is never synced to a client as a balance (ADR-010);
and (e) any figure that becomes a ledger entry — a reconciliation `delta`, a percentage-of-balance
minimum payment that is posted, an allocation — is computed from the derivation itself, never from
the cache.

**3.6 Rejected alternatives.**

- **A stored `current_balance` on the debt, written by the client or by the engine** — rejected: a
  second source of truth that drifts, cannot be reconciled without the entries anyway, and is the
  usual origin of "the app shows the wrong amount" (`PenniLogic/api#2` notes; ADR-010). It also
  contradicts `PenniLogic/api#11`'s outcome as published.
- **A trigger-maintained running balance as the source of truth** — rejected: it is a cache with the
  cache's failure modes plus a write on every entry; it is allowed only under §3.5 as a read model
  the reconciliation query polices.
- **Treating the lender statement as the truth and overwriting the ledger to match** — rejected: it
  would rewrite history (§2.5) and would hide the very discrepancies — interest, fees, a missed
  payment — the debt engine exists to surface. The statement is evidence; the adjustment is the
  reconciled fact; both remain.
- **Storing the derived balance on the snapshot** — rejected: it is recoverable as
  `statement_balance_minor - delta` and from `balance_recorded`; storing it invites reading it as a
  balance.

### 4. Foreign exchange: reserved now, implemented in E30

Two situations look like "foreign currency" and are handled differently. **A cross-currency movement
between the owner's own accounts** (a bank converting USD to INR between two of the owner's
accounts, a foreign-currency card bill paid from a domestic bank account) is an **exchange group**
(§4.2). **A foreign-denominated purchase on a domestic account** (a USD 20 subscription billed as
₹1,682.42 on an INR card) is a **single-currency transaction in the account's currency** — what the
statement debits — with the merchant-currency figure, when known, reserved as descriptive provenance
on the transaction (`merchant_amount_minor`, encrypted, paired with a constrained
`merchant_currency` validated against the currency registry rather than the admitted set, since the
merchant's currency is never a ledger currency of the owner; ADR-018 §1.2 declared-figure pattern)
and any separately stated markup posted as an explicit fee entry (§4.4). The merchant figure is
never an entry, so ADR-001's "store the original amount, currency, rate and converted value" is
satisfied without a mixed-currency transaction.

**4.1 Per-currency clearing accounts.** For each (owner, currency) in which the owner exchanges,
the server creates lazily one account with `type = 'CLEARING'`, `system_role = 'FX_CLEARING'`,
`currency` = that currency, named `FX clearing <CCY>`; the partial unique index of §1.6 makes it one
per owner and currency. Clearing accounts are not user-creatable, not user-postable and accept
entries only from exchange legs (a deferred constraint trigger rejects a clearing entry whose
transaction is not an `EXCHANGE_LEG` or the `REVERSAL` of one). They are excluded from net worth,
income, expense and category reports and appear only in exchange derivations: the clearing balance
in a currency is the cumulative amount exchanged out of (debit) or into (credit) that currency, and
the clearing balances of two currencies are never netted into one number (§5). Reserved in the MVP
migration; the MVP admitted set of one currency (§1.4) means none can be created.

**4.2 The exchange group.** A cross-currency movement is one row of `exchange_groups` plus exactly
two transactions of `kind = 'EXCHANGE_LEG'` that reference it — one in the **base** currency (the
currency given up) and one in the **quote** currency (the currency received). Each leg is an
ordinary single-currency transaction that balances under Z1 in its own currency; the two are joined
only by the group row and its clearing entries.

| `exchange_groups` column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id`, `owner_id` | `UUID` | Primary key; owner for RLS. |
| `base_currency`, `quote_currency` | `CHAR(3)` | Each references `ledger_currencies`; `CHECK (base_currency <> quote_currency)`. |
| `quoted_rate_e10` | `BIGINT` | The quoted rate as **quote-currency major units per one base-currency major unit, times 10^10**; `CHECK (quoted_rate_e10 > 0)` (§4.3). |
| `rate_source` | `TEXT` | `PROVIDER_STATEMENT`, `USER_ENTERED` or `REFERENCE_FEED`. |
| `rate_feed` | `TEXT`, nullable | Identifier of the reference feed from the closed list `T-CON-05` publishes; required if and only if `rate_source = 'REFERENCE_FEED'`. |
| `rate_instant` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | The instant the quote applied — the provider's quote time or the feed's publication instant (ADR-015 §3.1). |
| `occurred_at`, `booked_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ(3)` | The exchange's effective instant (shared by both legs) and its posting instant. |
| `evidence_ref` | `UUID`, nullable | Opaque import artefact or attachment identifier; never text, never a credential. |
| `reverses_group_id` | `UUID`, nullable, `UNIQUE` | Set on and only on a reversal group (§4.6). |

Structure of the two legs, with `b` the base minor units exchanged and `q` the gross quote minor units
received before any quote-side fee (`fee_base` and `fee_quote` appear here when the provider takes
the fee out of the exchanged amount; a fee the provider posts as a separate statement line is its
own `STANDARD` transaction to `FX_FEE`, §4.4):

```text
base leg  (currency = base):   source account            -b - fee_base        (credit: money leaves)
                               FX_CLEARING <base>        +b                   (debit)
                               FX_FEE <base>             +fee_base            (debit; present only if fee_base > 0)
quote leg (currency = quote):  FX_CLEARING <quote>       -q                   (credit)
                               destination account       +q - fee_quote       (debit: money arrives)
                               FX_FEE <quote>            +fee_quote           (debit; present only if fee_quote > 0)
```

Each leg sums to zero in its currency; nothing sums across the two. The clearing entry of the base
leg is the one positive clearing entry, the clearing entry of the quote leg the one negative
clearing entry; `b` and `q` are read from those entries and are never stored a second time. A
three-currency movement is two groups. Amounts in a group are wire `Money` objects per currency
(ADR-015 §1.1).

**4.3 Quoted rate, source and instant; never recomputed.** The rate is an exact decimal with
**exactly ten fraction digits**, stored as the integer `quoted_rate_e10` and carried on the wire as a
canonical decimal string with exactly ten fraction digits (`"83.2543000000"`), strictly positive, in
ADR-015 §1.2's grammar with exponent 10 (ADR-015 §1.6 delegates FX-quote scale to this record); it is
never a JSON number and never a `Money`. Ten digits preserve every bank, card-network and reference
quote exactly (they carry at most six) and span `0.0000000001` to `922,337,203.6854775807`, which
covers every ISO 4217 pair. A string with another scale is `scale_mismatch`; a non-positive rate is
rejected. The rate is **provenance of what the provider applied**: it, `rate_source`, `rate_feed`,
`rate_instant` and both legs are stored once and are immutable (the runtime role cannot update the
row). **No stored leg, no stored rate and no figure ever presented from them is recomputed from a
later rate**: the value of a historical exchange in either currency is its stored leg; a report that
converts historical activity into another currency uses, for each transaction, the reference quote
in force at that transaction's effective instant from the named feed (§5.3), and a valuation of a
current balance at today's quote is labelled with that quote's instant and is not a historical
figure. ADR-001's note and `architecture/01-domain-model.md` §1 ("never convert on read using
today's rate") are thereby made precise; the research sketch's `journal_entries.fx_rate
NUMERIC(18,8)` per entry is replaced by the group row.

**4.4 Every fee is an explicit entry.** A fee the provider states, or the user knows, is an entry to
the owner's `FX_FEE` expense account **in the currency in which it was charged** — inside that
currency's leg when the provider deducts it from the exchanged amount, or as its own `STANDARD`
transaction when the provider posts it as a separate line; in both cases it is an entry, never a
rate adjustment. A markup the provider states as a percentage is computed by the named server
function `ExchangeArithmetic.feeMinor(gross_minor, markup_bps)` with `HALF_EVEN` (ADR-015 §2
rounding rule) and posted as that entry. **The rate is never adjusted to absorb a fee**: no API
accepts an "effective rate", and the write path verifies the **rate identity** between the two
clearing entries before posting:

```text
expected_q = HALF_EVEN( b × quoted_rate_e10 × 10^e_quote  /  10^(e_base + 10) )     -- exact rational, one rounding
residual   = q - expected_q                                                          -- in quote minor units
```

`e_base` and `e_quote` are the registry exponents; the product `b × quoted_rate_e10 × 10^e_quote`
exceeds 64 bits, so the identity is evaluated in arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic
(`BigInteger`, or `NUMERIC` in SQL), never in `BIGINT`. The write path accepts the group only when
`abs(residual) <= 1` — one minor unit tolerates a provider that rounded half-up or truncated where this
record rounds half-even — and otherwise refuses it as `validation_rejected` with reason
`exchange_rate_inconsistent` (an inverted rate, `base` per `quote`, fails the identity by orders of
magnitude and is caught here), returning the implied difference to the user so it can be entered as
the fee it is; the API response may carry the amounts (they are the user's), logs and signals never
do. The residual is derived at read time and shown in the derivation; it is never stored and never
posted as an entry, because money in neither currency was created or destroyed — each leg balances
exactly — and the residual is a property of the provider's rounding, not of the ledger. If a
provider states only an all-in rate and no fee, the quoted rate is that all-in rate with
`rate_source = 'PROVIDER_STATEMENT'` and no fee entry: the record does not invent a fee, it forbids
hiding one.

**4.5 Atomicity, enforced in the database.** The group row and both legs (with all their entries)
are written in one database transaction, and a **deferred constraint trigger** on `exchange_groups`
(with companions on `transactions` and `entries`) verifies at `COMMIT`, for every group touched:

1. exactly two transactions reference the group; both have the group's `owner_id` and `occurred_at`;
   their currencies are exactly `{base_currency, quote_currency}`; each is an `EXCHANGE_LEG`, or,
   for a reversal group, a `REVERSAL` (§4.6);
2. the base leg has exactly one entry on the owner's `FX_CLEARING` account in the base currency and
   the quote leg exactly one on the owner's `FX_CLEARING` account in the quote currency; in an
   original group the base clearing entry is positive and the quote clearing entry negative, and in
   a reversal group both signs are inverted, because each leg negates its original (§4.6); each leg
   also has at least one entry on an account with `system_role IS NULL`, so a leg cannot consist of
   system accounts alone (a "negative fee" cannot conjure a source of funds);
3. no entry on any `FX_CLEARING` account belongs to a transaction outside an exchange group, and no
   `EXCHANGE_LEG` exists without its group;
4. each leg satisfies Z1–Z7 (the triggers of §1.2 run regardless).

A group with one leg, two legs in one currency, a wrong clearing sign, a leg of another owner, a
missing rate, source or instant, or a clearing entry from an ordinary transaction therefore makes
the `COMMIT` fail and leaves nothing behind: **the whole group commits or none of it does**, and
that is decided by PostgreSQL, not by application code, for the reasons of §1.3. A process that
dies after writing one leg has written nothing.

**4.6 Reversal of a group is paired and whole.** An exchange is corrected only by a **reversal
group** `G'` with `reverses_group_id = G.id`, whose two `REVERSAL` legs (`reason_code =
'EXCHANGE_REVERSAL'`) each reverse one leg of `G` under §2.1, copying `G`'s `base_currency`,
`quote_currency`, `quoted_rate_e10`, `rate_source`, `rate_feed` and `rate_instant` unchanged — a
reversal restates the quote, it does not re-quote. The trigger of §4.5 requires the reversal group
to cover **both** legs of `G` at `COMMIT`; a `REVERSAL` whose target is an exchange leg and which
does not belong to a reversal group of that leg's group is rejected; `reverses_group_id` is
`UNIQUE`, so a group is reversed at most once; and each leg's `reverses` is `UNIQUE`, so a leg cannot
be reversed both inside and outside a group. **A partial reversal of one leg is impossible to
commit.** A corrected exchange is `G'` plus a new group; both derivations remain inspectable.

**4.7 Worked example** (E30). USD 100.00 exchanged for INR at the bank's quoted rate 83.2543 with a
₹250.00 fee charged in INR, from the owner's USD account to the owner's INR account:

```text
exchange_groups:  base USD, quote INR, quoted_rate_e10 = 832543000000, rate_source PROVIDER_STATEMENT,
                  rate_instant 2026-09-30T04:52:08.439Z
USD leg:          Asset USD account      -10000       FX_CLEARING USD    +10000
INR leg:          FX_CLEARING INR       -832543       Asset INR account  +807543      FX_FEE INR  +25000
identity:         expected_q = HALF_EVEN(10000 × 832543000000 × 10^2 / 10^12) = 832543;  residual = 0
reversal group:   USD leg  Asset +10000, FX_CLEARING -10000;  INR leg  FX_CLEARING +832543, Asset -807543, FX_FEE -25000
```

Each leg sums to zero; the USD bucket of the owner's net worth falls by USD 100.00 and the INR bucket
rises by ₹8,075.43, with ₹250.00 of expense; no figure adds USD to INR.

**4.8 No existing stored amount changes when exchange arrives.** Every MVP entry already carries
`(amount_minor, currency)` and every MVP transaction already carries `currency` and a `NULL`
`exchange_group_id`; the `CLEARING` type value, the `system_role` values, `exchange_groups`, the
group triggers and `ledger_currencies` exist from the ledger migration. E30's migration therefore
**inserts** admitted currencies, **creates** clearing and fee accounts lazily, and **enables** the
write path behind its flag; it alters no existing column, rewrites no row and changes no derived
balance. `MigrationEquivalenceTest` (§9) proves it with a checksum over `(id, amount_minor,
currency)` of every entry and every derived balance before and after.

**4.9 Phase.** Multi-currency implementation — the exchange write path, wider admission, reporting
currencies, reference feeds and every client surface — stays in **P6 Moat** under E30
(`PenniLogic/docs#33`: `PenniLogic/api#46`, `PenniLogic/contracts#9`, `PenniLogic/web#11`,
`PenniLogic/android#45`). What lands earlier is exactly the reservation: the enum values, the empty
constrained tables and columns, the triggers, the one-row admitted set and the per-currency response
shapes of §5, all in P0 Foundations (`PenniLogic/api#2`). Nothing in the MVP can create an exchange
group, and a negative test in `PenniLogic/api#2`'s suite proves it.

### 5. Totals: a per-currency breakdown, never a mixed scalar

**5.1 The rule.** Any figure that aggregates entries or balances across more than one account —
a total balance, net worth, spending in a period or category, a debt portfolio total, a household
aggregate, a split group's position — is a **per-currency breakdown**: one `Money` per currency
that contributed, currencies unique and sorted by code, **with no total member**. A scalar `Money`
may appear only where the enclosing resource fixes the currency: one account's balance, one
transaction, one debt, one exchange leg. A response that adds minor units of two currencies, or
that presents a converted sum as if it were a balance, is a defect; the contract lint of §9 rejects
a schema that permits it. With the MVP admitted set of one currency every breakdown has one bucket;
the shape is still the breakdown, so E30 is additive for every client (`T-CON-05`, `T-CON-08`).

**5.2 Shape and derivation.** `T-CON-05` and `T-CON-08` publish the components; this record fixes
their meaning:

```yaml
MoneyByCurrency:            # every cross-account aggregate
  type: array
  items: { $ref: '#/components/schemas/Money' }
  # invariant: currencies unique, ascending by code; an empty array is "nothing to sum", not zero
Derivation:                 # attached to every aggregate and every derived balance
  as_of:            Instant or LocalDate (the position summed to; §3.1)
  ledger_position:  { max_booked_at: Instant, entry_count: integer }
  contributors:     [ { account_id, currency, amount: Money } ]    # every account and figure that entered a bucket
  incomplete:       boolean          # a contributor is stale or unavailable (T-NWT-01's explicit state)
ConvertedMoney:             # E30 only; a presentation of a bucket in a reporting currency
  amount:           Money            # in the reporting currency
  from:             Money            # the bucket it was derived from
  quote:            { rate: string(10 fraction digits), base, quote, source, feed, instant }
  rounding:         "HALF_EVEN"
```

**5.3 Reporting-currency conversion (E30).** A converted figure is a **presentation** of a bucket,
computed on the server by the named function `ExchangeArithmetic.convert(bucket_minor, quote,
e_from, e_to)` = `HALF_EVEN(bucket_minor × rate_e10 × 10^e_to / 10^(e_from + 10))` — exact
arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic and one rounding per bucket — and shown only inside
`ConvertedMoney` next to its bucket.
A converted total is the sum of the rounded per-bucket figures, in that order, so the displayed
buckets add up to the displayed total exactly. The quote for a **current valuation** is the latest
reference quote at or before the as-of instant from the named feed; the quote for a **historical
transaction** is the latest reference quote at or before that transaction's effective instant; both
name the quote in the derivation, and the reference-quote store is append-only so the same query
returns the same figure later (§4.3). A stored leg, a stored rate and a stored statement figure are
never converted in place. Reporting currency is a request parameter (`T-CON-05`), never a profile
setting that silently reshapes stored data, and formatting is a render-time concern
(`PenniLogic/android#34`, `T-GLO-02`).

### 6. Consequences, by ticket

**6.1 Ledger schema and posting** — `PenniLogic/api#2` creates `ledger_currencies` (`INR`),
`accounts` with the six-value type set and `system_role`, `transactions` with the §1.5 columns and
checks, `entries` with the composite keys and Z5, the `transaction_zero_sum` trigger, the reserved
`exchange_groups`, `statement_snapshots`, the group and clearing triggers, the `reverses`/`replaces`
linkage, the `entry_count` seal and the role grants of §2.4, and adopts the account-type set and
enums from §10 in place of
its original five-value list; it does not populate any reserved structure. `PenniLogic/api#6`
(accounts) creates the `OPENING_BALANCE` equity account lazily and posts opening balances as
transactions. `PenniLogic/api#7` (transaction creation) posts every transaction single-currency in
the account's currency and implements the reversal/re-post helper. Every `Money`-bearing write is
idempotent under ADR-015 §4.

**6.2 Debt engine** — `PenniLogic/docs#6` (E05). `PenniLogic/api#11`: one liability account per debt,
`owed` derived, declared terms as inputs, snapshots and adjustments exactly as §3.3–§3.4, the
migration guard of §3.2. `PenniLogic/api#12` (amortisation), `PenniLogic/api#13` (projections),
`PenniLogic/api#14` (strategies), `PenniLogic/api#15` (simulator) and `PenniLogic/api#50`
(`T-DEBT-01`): every schedule and projection starts from `owed_asof` at the projection's stated
start and carries that `Derivation` in its response (the `T-CON-01` computation identifier names
it); projections **never write to the ledger** — actual interest and fees enter only as captured or
imported entries or as reconciliation adjustments, so a projected accrual can never be mistaken for
a posted one; a percentage-of-balance minimum uses `owed_asof` at the due date; "debt-free" is
`owed = 0`. `PenniLogic/api#16` (independent verification, `debt_maths_independent_model`) includes
derived-balance equivalence over generated entry sequences and the discrepancy arithmetic of §3.4.
`PenniLogic/contracts#5` (`T-CON-01`) publishes the snapshot shape, the owed-sign mapping, the
reconciliation state and the derived-balance marker. `PenniLogic/android#3`, `PenniLogic/android#4`,
`PenniLogic/web#10`, `PenniLogic/web#23` render derivations and never compute a balance.

**6.3 The split constraint** — `PenniLogic/docs#16` (E15). `PenniLogic/api#33` (`T-SPL-01`) enforces
`SUM(split_shares.share_minor) = split_expenses.amount_minor` by the **same mechanism** as §1.2 — a
deferred constraint trigger over the expense's shares, since a `CHECK` cannot sum rows — and
single-currency groups by composite keys (`split_expenses (group_id, currency) → split_groups (id,
currency)` and `settlements (group_id, currency) → split_groups (id, currency)`; a share has no
currency column of its own and inherits its expense's through `expense_id`, ADR-018 §1.2). Shares are
stored facts allocated by ADR-015's server-only `allocate` (largest remainder,
stable member ordering from `T-CON-04`); an expense, a share set or a settlement is corrected by
reversal and re-post, never by `UPDATE` (the runtime role has none on `split_shares` or
`settlements`, ADR-018 §1.3). Simplification computes per currency and, with single-currency groups,
never meets a second one; a member who paid in another currency records an exchange group in their
own ledger (E30) and the expense in the group's currency. `PenniLogic/api#49` (`T-SPL-03`) records
settlements as immutable facts; `PenniLogic/contracts#8` (`T-CON-04`) carries the sum constraint and
the member ordering.

**6.4 The multi-currency epic** — `PenniLogic/docs#33` (E30). `PenniLogic/api#46` (`T-GLO-01`)
implements §4 as written — clearing accounts, groups, rate provenance, fee entries, atomicity,
paired reversal, the rate identity, admission widening, `MigrationEquivalenceTest` — and adds no
second representation. `PenniLogic/contracts#9` (`T-CON-05`) publishes `QuotedRate` (ten fraction
digits), `ExchangeGroup`, `MoneyByCurrency`, `ConvertedMoney` and the closed `rate_feed` list, and
its lint asserts no bare amount and no scalar cross-currency total. `PenniLogic/web#11`
(`T-GLO-03`) and `PenniLogic/android#45` (`T-GLO-04`) render breakdowns, exchanges and derivations
and never convert. The E30 flag gates group creation and admission; with it off, behaviour is the
MVP's.

**6.5 Reporting and aggregates** — `PenniLogic/api#54` (`T-NWT-01`): net worth as `MoneyByCurrency`
of assets minus liabilities per currency, clearing accounts excluded, with `Derivation` and the
incomplete-data state. `PenniLogic/api#72` (`T-FAM-05`): `period_aggregates` are §3.5 read models,
one row per currency, never a mixed sum. `PenniLogic/contracts#12` (`T-CON-08`) publishes the
breakdown and derivation components for income, budgets, safe-to-spend and goals; a budget or goal
compares against the bucket in its own currency. Category totals are per-currency sums over entries
under whatever category semantics ADR-016 fixes; this record adds no category rule.

**6.6 Offline and sync** — ADR-010, `PenniLogic/contracts#4` (`T-SYN-01`), `PenniLogic/android#6`,
`PenniLogic/android#50` (`T-SYN-04`): the client's local schema mirrors `(amount_minor INTEGER,
currency TEXT)` per entry (ADR-015 §2) and recomputes balances from local entries by §3.1; no
balance is synced; a correction reaches the server as a new reversal/re-post change, never as an
amount update; the server's derivation wins on disagreement.

**6.7 Administration** — ADR-020: an administrative money-bearing effect on the ledger is an
adjusting or reversing transaction created by `admin_api` in the same transaction as its audit
event, which references the transaction identifier and never an amount; no administrative path
updates an entry.

### 7. Security and privacy consequences

- **Chosen.** Amounts and currencies stay constrained plaintext `BIGINT`/`CHAR(3)` columns under
  `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` and the low-privilege role (ADR-018 §1.3, §4) — the price of a
  constraint the database can execute. Append-only with withheld `UPDATE`/`DELETE` bounds the blast
  radius of a compromised API process to *adding* rows, which are visible, attributable
  (`booked_at`, `reason_code`, ADR-015 idempotency records) and reversible; silent rewriting of
  history is a privilege error. Constraint triggers cannot be disabled by any service credential.
  `reason_note`, `merchant_amount_minor`, account names and statement text-like fields follow
  ADR-018's encrypted classes; `evidence_ref`, `rate_feed`, `rate_source` and every identifier are
  opaque or reference values; `statement_snapshots` and `exchange_groups` hold no lender or
  provider credential and no raw statement or message text (ADR-004, ADR-013,
  `PenniLogic/api#11`). Snapshots, adjustments and exchange rows are owner-scoped under `owner_rows`;
  a grant admits entries only through ADR-018's `grant_mediated_read`, and clearing, opening-balance
  and reconciliation accounts are never part of a split-relevant projection. Signals (§8) carry
  counts, kinds, reason codes and residual buckets, never an amount, balance, rate or identifier.
  Erasure leaves constrained ledger rows under an unlinkable owner (ADR-018 §6.7, ADR-021); a
  reversal is never used as erasure and erasure never posts entries. Accepted residual: `booked_at`,
  `occurred_at` and `rate_instant` reveal the rhythm of activity per opaque owner (ADR-018 §9).
- **Rejected.** In-place edits require a standing `UPDATE` grant on the most sensitive table and
  turn every compromise into undetectable history rewriting. A stored balance is a second copy of the
  single most sensitive figure per account that can be tampered without leaving an entry, and must
  be separately protected, reconciled and erased. Application-encrypted amounts (rejected by
  ADR-018) would make Z1 unexecutable. A fee absorbed into the rate hides the cost of an exchange
  from the person paying it, contrary to the product's provable-figure positioning (ADR-012). A
  scalar mixed-currency total discloses nothing extra but states a number that is not true.

### 8. Observability

Signals that prove the decision is honoured, none carrying an amount, balance, rate, account or
transaction identifier:

- `ledger.constraint.violation{constraint}` — database rejections by named constraint
  (`transaction_zero_sum`, `transaction_sealed`, `reversal_exact_negation`,
  `transaction_single_currency`, `entry_account_currency`, `entry_nonzero_or_range`,
  `repost_without_reversal`, `exchange_group_shape`, `exchange_group_reversal`,
  `clearing_entry_membership`, `split_shares_sum`); expected zero from the application, so any
  count names a defect or a probe.
- `ledger.transaction.posted{kind}`, `ledger.transaction.reversed{reason_code}` and
  `ledger.reversal.age_seconds` (histogram from the original's `booked_at` to the reversal's) — the
  correction path in use and how late corrections arrive.
- `ledger.balance.reconciliation_run` and `ledger.balance.reconciliation_mismatch{read_model}` — the
  §3.5 scheduled comparison of every read model with the derivation; a non-zero mismatch alerts
  (`PenniLogic/api#2`).
- `ledger.statement_snapshot.recorded{source}`, `ledger.reconciliation.adjustment{direction}`
  (daily, split debit/credit, `PenniLogic/api#11`), `ledger.reconciliation.state{state}` and
  `ledger.reconciliation.unreconciled_age_days` (histogram) — statements arriving, discrepancies
  found, and how long they stay unreconciled.
- `ledger.exchange.group{outcome=committed|rejected}`, `ledger.exchange.rate_inconsistent`,
  `ledger.exchange.rate_residual{value=-1|0|1}` and `ledger.exchange.reversal` (E30) — group
  atomicity and the rate identity in production.
- `ledger.currency.not_admitted` — refusals of non-admitted registry currencies; a rise before E30
  measures demand, and any value after admission names a client bug.
- `ledger.total.mixed_currency_assertion` — a runtime assertion in the aggregate serialiser that
  every breakdown has unique currencies and no total member; must be zero.
- Build-time and migration-time checks, trended: the constraint triggers exist and are
  `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`; the runtime role's privilege matrix on ledger tables; no column
  named like a balance is writable on a ledger table; no `DISABLE TRIGGER`, `NO FORCE ROW LEVEL
  SECURITY` or `session_replication_role` appears in any migration (ADR-018 §4.2 lint); the contract
  lint's count of `MoneyByCurrency` usages and zero scalar cross-account `Money` totals.

### 11. Migration note

No ledger table exists yet in `PenniLogic/api`; the first ledger migration (`PenniLogic/api#2`)
conforms to this record, so there is no data migration for the MVP. What changes relative to the
research and ticket prose: `accounts.initial_balance` (research §12) is removed in favour of
`OPENING_BALANCE` transactions; `journal_entries.fx_rate` per entry is removed in favour of
`exchange_groups.quoted_rate_e10`; the account-type set gains `CLEARING`; `PenniLogic/api#2`'s
`SUM(entries.amount_minor) = 0` is grouped per currency and joined by Z2–Z6 and the reversal
linkage; `PenniLogic/api#11`'s "current balance" field is a derived response field, its
`StatementSnapshot` resource carries the adjustment reference by join (the database link runs from
the adjustment to the snapshot so the snapshot row never changes), and its statement balance is
stored in ledger sign. The domain model's `accounts.currency` and `entries.currency` are kept; its
"store the FX rate and converted amount" sentence is realised by the exchange group and the
descriptive merchant amount, not by a per-entry rate. ADR-002's "SUM(entries.amount) = 0" is
unchanged in intent and made per-currency in statement. The E30 migration is described in §4.8 and
proven by `MigrationEquivalenceTest`.

### 12. Rollout and rollback

Adopted on merge; `PenniLogic/api#2`, `PenniLogic/api#7`, `PenniLogic/api#11`,
`PenniLogic/contracts#5`, `PenniLogic/contracts#9`, `PenniLogic/api#33`, `PenniLogic/api#46` and
the tickets of §6 consume it directly and must not restate or contradict it. Additive evolution
without a new record: a new admitted currency, a new `rate_feed` identifier, a new `reason_code`
value, a new `system_role` whose `type` is one of the six, a tighter residual bound. Changing the
sign convention, the constraint mechanism, the single-currency-per-transaction rule, the derived
balance rule, the append-only rule, the rate scale or direction, the two-leg group shape, the
whole-group reversal rule or the per-currency total rule requires a **superseding record that
states the migration cost**: every ledger row's interpretation, the reversal and adjustment
history, every published contract component of §5.2 and `T-CON-01`/`T-CON-05`, three generated
clients, the read models, and the conformance fixtures of §9. Reversing this record after ledger
data exists is a preservation migration, not a revert (`adr/LAYOUT.md`).

### 13. Non-goals

Implementing multi-currency, which is E30's (`PenniLogic/docs#33`); implementation of anything here,
which belongs to the tickets of §6; category semantics and how a category total is defined over
entries (ADR-016); the money wire form, rounding-mode default, instant form and idempotency
behaviour (ADR-015); column classification, key management, row-level-security policies and the
dedupe key (ADR-018); the audit and transparency stores (ADR-020); erasure of shared and ledger
data (ADR-021); the debt engine's own rounding declarations, amortisation and strategy arithmetic
(`T-CON-01`, `PenniLogic/api#12`–`#16`); the reference-feed choice, provider and update cadence
(E30, an owner decision); statement import parsing (`T-CON-10`, `PenniLogic/api#10`); the split
allocation algorithm and member ordering (ADR-015 §2, `T-CON-04`); executing a currency conversion
or holding a balance on the user's behalf, permanently (`T-GLO-01` non-goal).

**Revisit if:** a real provider's rounding produces a residual beyond one minor unit on legitimate
exchanges (widen the bound by a superseding statement, never by absorbing it into the rate); a
currency pair emerges outside the ten-digit range; or the product decides to hold or exchange
currency itself, which would make the clearing accounts real balances and needs a new record.
