## ADR-021 — Shared-data erasure and cross-user key access
**Status:** ACCEPTED · 2026-09-30

**Decision.** One record settles how a grantee reads data encrypted under another user's key, what a
revoked grantee can still decrypt (nothing), and what happens to a record with more than one data
subject when one of them is erased, so that family sharing and split groups neither open a second key
path nor silently corrupt other people's balances:

1. **Cross-user reads are server-mediated decryptions under the ADR-018 grant predicate, with zero
   grantee-bound key material.** A grantee's request never receives, caches or is addressed by a key: the
   API, inside the grantee's request transaction, reads the grantor's `user_keys` row only through
   ADR-018's `grant_mediated_key_read`, unwraps it in process memory, decrypts the rows
   `grant_mediated_read` admitted, and returns plaintext for the admitted fields. No wrapped copy of a
   DEK exists for any grantee, no group key exists for family sharing, and no client ever decrypts (§1).
2. **Split-group records are encrypted under one group-scoped data encryption key (`key_scope = group`).**
   `split_groups.name`, `split_group_members.placeholder_name` / `display_alias`, `split_expenses.description`
   and `settlements.note` — the five columns in the four tables ADR-018 §1.2 left `key_scope_pending` — use a group DEK held
   in `group_keys`, admitted to a member's request by `group_member_key_read` and to the key-operations
   worker by `begin_group_keyops`. The author-DEK-plus-rewrap alternative is rejected because every
   rewrapped copy of a personal DEK is a copy that erasure must find and destroy (§1.4, §10).
3. **Revocation removes the ability to decrypt because nothing decryptable was ever held.** Revoking a
   grant or ending a membership sets `revoked_at` / `left_at` (ADR-018 §5.4), the next `begin_request`
   denies (`grant_not_active`), the row and key policies admit nothing, the event is journaled by
   identifier and replayed on restore so a backup cannot resurrect a grant, and the owner's DEK is **not**
   rotated because no key material left the server. A grantee can decrypt **nothing** after revocation,
   at any level, for any date (§2).
4. **A multi-subject record has exactly one owner scope; erasure shreds the subject's scope and severs or
   pseudonymises the subject's references in every other scope, and destroys nothing that belongs to a
   counterparty.** In a split group the erased member becomes an unclaimable placeholder (`user_id = NULL`,
   alias cleared, `erased_at` set); the group-local `member_id` survives, so every share, expense and
   settlement still reconciles and every other member's net balance is re-derived to the same value. Free
   text the erased member authored is redacted; money, dates and opaque member references survive (§3).
5. **What survives for a counterparty is proportional to the counterparty's legal position.** A claim
   (split expense, share, settlement) survives at row level as ADR-018 constrained money under a stable
   pseudonymous member reference; a shared target (joint goal) survives only as the counterparty's own
   contributions and a live total marked partial; a mere view (family grant) survives only as the
   counterparty's transparency record with the viewer pseudonymised (§4).
6. **The retention position is reduced to sourced obligations.** DPDP Act 2023 §8(7), §12(3) and §17(1)(a),
   DPDP Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)) Rule 6(1)(e) and — as a counsel-gated structural hook, not a legal
   conclusion — Rule 8(3) with its Seventh Schedule, and the CERT-In 180-day log direction apply, with every
   numeric value read from the T-CMP-04 profile; a profile-driven statutory pre-shred hold gates the
   irreversible phase. The inherited "user account lifetime + 7 years (RBI financial record requirement)" and
   "confirmation within 72 hours" rules have no applicable source and are removed from PenniLogic's retention
   position (§5).
7. **The erasure-completeness harness** (`erasure_completeness_harness`, owned by T-SEC-02 with T-SEC-03,
   T-SEC-07, T-SPL-03, T-FAM-02 and T-FAM-05) proves, on synthetic subjects, that no DEK-decryptable byte of the
   subject remains readable by any role after erasure, that a pre-erasure backup is re-erased before it can
   serve traffic, that every counterparty's balances are unchanged, that the row-level policies admit exactly
   the post-erasure rows, that the job resumes from any partial failure to the same end state, and that a
   revoked grantee's decrypt attempt fails at three independent layers (§7).
8. **The machine-readable artefact** `adr-021-erasure-parameters` v2 (§6.3) is the only form in which
   dependent contract and implementation tickets consume these decisions; the schema, policy, setter,
   journal and contract consequences are enumerated in §6 and are additive to ADR-018's inventory, with the
   one named extension of ADR-018 §4.1's key-operations grant enumeration.

**Relationship to other records.** ADR-005 (default-deny ReBAC grants, unilateral revocation, logged
detail access) and ADR-006 (crypto-shredding; its open split-group question) are made concrete here;
neither is loosened. ADR-018 is the premise: its column classes, forced row-level security, the exact

### 1. Cross-user key access

#### 1.1 The question and the three candidate mechanisms

ADR-006 gave every user a DEK and no other user a way to read under it. Two product features need a
cross-user read: family sharing (a grantee reads a grantor's own rows at aggregate or detail level,
ADR-005) and split groups (every member reads the group's shared rows). Three mechanisms were considered
for each:

| Mechanism | Shape | Who holds what |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared group key** | One DEK per sharing relationship or group; every reader's request unwraps it | The server holds one wrapped key per scope; no user-bound copies |
| **Re-encryption / re-wrap** | The owner's DEK (or the record) is re-wrapped or re-encrypted to a key addressed to the grantee | The server holds one wrapped copy per (key, grantee) |
| **Proxy (server-mediated) decryption** | The server unwraps the owner's own key inside the grantee's admitted request and returns plaintext | The server holds exactly one wrapped copy per key; the grantee holds nothing |

The choice differs by feature because the two features have different records: a family grant shares
*the grantor's own record*, a split group shares *a record that belongs to the group*.

#### 1.2 Family sharing: server-mediated decryption under the grant predicate, no grantee-bound material

**Chosen.** The read path ADR-018 already fixed is the mechanism, and this record closes it against any
second path:

1. The grantee's request calls `app_ctx.begin_request(principal = grantee, targets = [grantor], level)`.
   The setter resolves the active `sharing_grants` rows on the primary into `app.grant_set`, raises
   `grant_not_active` (`42501`) when any named target has no active grant at the requested level, and
   binds the context to this transaction by MAC (ADR-018 §4.5, §5.2).
2. Rows are admitted only by `grant_mediated_read` (detail: `transactions`, `entries`) or
   `grant_mediated_aggregate_read` (`period_aggregates`). An aggregate read touches no ciphertext and
   **unwraps no key**; `period_aggregates` is constrained.
3. For a detail read the API reads the grantor's `user_keys` row admitted by `grant_mediated_key_read` —
   `purpose = 'DEK'`, `state IN ('ACTIVE','DECRYPT_ONLY')`, a `detail` token for that grantor in the verified
   grant set — asks the custodian to unwrap it, decrypts the admitted rows' `encrypted` columns in process
   memory using the key the ciphertext header names, and zeroises the plaintext key on eviction (ADR-018
   §5.4). The grantor's IK is never admitted (no cross-user search shape exists, ADR-018 §3.3), so a grantee
   cannot search a grantor's data, only read what the predicate admits.
4. The response carries plaintext for the admitted fields of the admitted rows and the grant reference
   (the policy identifier, T-CON-04). It never carries a key, a wrapped key, a ciphertext, a nonce, a key
   identifier or a blind-index value; the T-CON-04 schema has no member that could, and the T-CON-02 /
   T-CON-09 negative fixtures and the T-QA-09 traffic capture assert it (`no_grantee_bound_key_material_exists`,
   §7).
5. The same transaction inserts the `sharing.read.*` `audit.event` under ADR-018 §1.2's integrity check
   (scoped in the merged text to the cross-user read actions, so that a grantor's own `sharing.grant.*`
   lifecycle events name only the acting principal); ADR-020's relay projects it into the grantor's
   `access_events` (T-TRU-04), applying this record's erasure register as it does so (§3.5). The custodian
   records the unwrap with the request identifier.

**Key-cache admission rule (a tightening of ADR-018 §5.4).** ADR-018 lets the API cache an unwrapped DEK for
at most five minutes keyed by `(user_id, key_id)`. This record fixes that **the cache saves the unwrap,
never the admission**: a key whose `user_id` differs from the verified principal — or a group key — may be
used in a transaction only after that transaction has itself read the corresponding wrapped-key row under
`grant_mediated_key_read` or `group_member_key_read`; the row read is the admission, the cache only avoids
a custodian round trip. A cached key presented to the decrypt routine without an admission in the current
transaction is refused and counted (`crypto.key_admission_refused_total`, §8). Grant revocation and
membership end additionally evict every cache entry the grant or membership admitted; eviction is a
courtesy, the admission rule is the guarantee (`key_cache_never_admits`, §7).

**Why this and not a re-wrap.** Under ADR-018 every DEK is wrapped under one versioned platform KEK held
by one custodian. A "grantee-addressed" re-wrap of the grantor's DEK — `KEK.wrap(DEK_grantor, aad =
grant_id || grantee_id …)` stored per grant — is wrapped under the *same* KEK the server already uses to
unwrap the grantor's own row. It therefore protects against no adversary the existing path does not: a
party with custodian unwrap rights can unwrap either copy, a party without them can unwrap neither. What it
adds is cost and hazard: every DEK rotation must rewrap every grant copy; every revocation must delete a
copy and — to make the deletion mean anything against backups — rotate the grantor's whole DEK, re-encrypting
the grantor's entire history per revocation while the old key stays `DECRYPT_ONLY` for the backup window
anyway; and, decisively, **every copy is a place erasure must find**: a missed grant copy keeps a destroyed
DEK alive and breaks ADR-006's promise. With exactly one wrapped copy per key, E26-F17's inverse attack —
"a removed or revoked grantee retains a decryptable copy" — is impossible by construction, because nothing
addressed to the grantee exists to retain. A re-wrap to a key the grantee alone controls would give a
genuinely different guarantee, but that is end-to-end encryption, which ADR-018 §13 and
`architecture/02-security-architecture.md` §4.3 rejected for a product with web access, server-side matching
and grant-mediated reads; this record does not reopen it.

**Why not a shared key for family sharing.** A per-relationship key would require re-encrypting the
grantor's rows under it (a second ciphertext of the same data, doubling erasure surface) or encrypting the
grantor's own data under a key the grantor does not solely own; either contradicts ADR-018's rule that a
user's own rows are under the user's own `ACTIVE` DEK.

#### 1.3 Aggregate grants

An aggregate grant admits `period_aggregates` rows only (ADR-018 §4.3). They are sums the database holds
in the clear under forced RLS; no key is read (`grant_mediated_key_read` requires a `detail` token), so
revocation of an aggregate grant has no key consequence at all, and the closed-period snapshot,
completeness and suppression rules of T-FAM-05 and T-CON-04 govern what the number reveals.

#### 1.4 Split groups: one group-scoped DEK

**Chosen: `key_scope = group`.** Each split group has exactly one `ACTIVE` group DEK and zero or more
`DECRYPT_ONLY` ones, in a new table `group_keys` (§6.1). The five encrypted split-group columns are
AES-256-GCM ciphertext under that key with ADR-018 §2's header (`key_scope` byte = `group`, `key_id` = the
`group_keys.key_id`) and AAD `header || table_name || column_name || row_id || group_id`. Writers always use the
group's `ACTIVE` key; readers select the key the header names; a group without an `ACTIVE` key cannot receive
an encrypted write (fail closed, `crypto.group_key_missing`).

*Read path.* A member's request already carries `app.group_set` (ADR-018 §5.2). Rows are admitted by
`group_member_rows`; the key row is admitted by the new policy `group_member_key_read` — `FOR SELECT TO
pennilogic_app USING (state IN ('ACTIVE','DECRYPT_ONLY') AND group_id::text = ANY ((SELECT
app_ctx.verified_group_set())))`. The API unwraps the group key in process (cache rule of §1.2 applies) and
decrypts the admitted rows. A placeholder member has no `user_id`, resolves to no principal and reads nothing
(T-SPL-01).

*Write path and lifecycle.* The first `group_keys` row is inserted in the transaction that creates the group,
under the creation policy the split-ledger ticket defines for `split_groups` and the creator's membership row
(T-SPL-01 with T-SEC-01); the group key follows ADR-018's DEK class unchanged — 32 CSPRNG bytes wrapped as
`KEK.wrap(gdek, aad = group_id || key_id || 'GDEK')`, a `canary`, `encrypt_count` with rotation due at 2^27 and
fail-closed at `2^28 − 2^24`, a 24-month cryptoperiod, the resumable rotation state machine, the retirement proof,
emergency rotation and the restore rule of ADR-018 §6. Rotation and erasure steps on group rows run as
`pennilogic_keyops` under a sixth setter of ADR-018 §5.2's form, `app_ctx.begin_group_keyops(p_group uuid,
p_job_id uuid)` — principal `''`, `acr` `system`, empty grant set, `app.group_set = p_group`, actor
`keyops:<job_id>`, `EXECUTE` only for `pennilogic_keyops` — admitted by the policy `group_keyops_rows` (§6.1)
with column-level grants on the five ciphertext columns, their header bytes and the redaction and placeholder
columns of §3.3, and by `group_key_ops` on `group_keys`.

*Destruction.* A group DEK is destroyed (state `DESTROYED_BY_ERASURE`, wrapped material overwritten,
journaled) when the group becomes **orphaned**: no membership row with a non-null `user_id` and `left_at IS
NULL` remains, whether because the last identified member was erased or left. The constrained ledger rows of
the group survive under an unlinkable `group_id`, exactly as a user's ledger survives under an unlinkable
`owner_id` (ADR-018 §6.7); the names of never-claimed placeholder seats — personal data of people who never
installed the app — become unreadable with the key, which is the desired outcome. An orphaned group is
**closed**: it accepts no join, no claim and no write, its pending invitations are void, and it is never given a
new key; a product-level "delete this group" is the ending of every membership and therefore the same event. A
group that is merely inactive is not orphaned and keeps its key.

**Why a group key and not the author's DEK with rewrap.** ADR-018 supports both through the header byte
and left the choice here. Under author scope, member B's request would unwrap author A's *personal* DEK — the
key that also protects A's private transactions, notes and merchant names — relying on row admission alone to
keep A's private rows out of B's response; the blast radius of a row-admission bug or a process-memory
disclosure would be A's whole account instead of one group. A's DEK would need one rewrapped copy per group
(the hazard of §1.2 multiplied by group count), every rotation of A's DEK would have to re-key A's rows in every
group, and A's erasure would have to destroy every group copy or leave A's entire account recoverable. Under
group scope the request unwraps a key that protects only the group's five descriptive columns, the group's
rows are re-keyed in one job per group, and a member's erasure never touches the key other members depend
on. The cost is that the erased member's identifiers inside group rows are not crypto-shredded by the
member's own key destruction and must be physically overwritten (§3.3); that set is small, enumerable and
covered by the harness, and its completeness bound equals the crypto-shredding bound (§4.2).

**Why not one ciphertext copy per member.** Encrypting every group row once per member under each member's
own DEK would let erasure fall out of key destruction, but it multiplies storage and writes by group size,
requires a fan-out that can leave copies inconsistent, and moves the share-sum invariant from one row to N;
ADR-002's single append-only fact per event is worth more than the symmetry.

#### 1.5 Joint goals and household aggregates: owner scope plus grants

A joint goal (product spec §3.4–§3.5, T-FAM-05) is **owned by one household member** — its `goals` row, name and
target are encrypted under the goal owner's DEK, `key_scope = user` — and is shared with other household
members through ordinary ADR-005 grants at aggregate or detail level. **A contribution is a ledger fact of the
contributor and nothing else**: money the contributor moved into a savings account, recorded as the
contributor's own `entries` under ADR-002 and ADR-017. `goal_contributions` is defined **once**, here: a
**non-authoritative read model in the sense of ADR-017 §3.5** — `(owner_id = contributor, goal_id, currency,
amount_minor, period)` rows recomputed from the contributor's own entries, reconciled on schedule, never synced
and never a write input — owned by the contributor under `owner_rows`, readable by the goal owner and other
members only through a grant, exactly like the `period_aggregates` ADR-018 §1.2 defines. **The attribution
source is a constrained goal→account link**: `goal_accounts (goal_id, owner_id, account_id, linked_from,
linked_to, goal_erased_at)`, an additive owner-scoped table under ADR-018 §1.1 that a contributor writes to declare "entries on
this savings account of mine count toward goal G from this date"; the read model is the deterministic sum of the
contributor's entries on the linked accounts within the link's dates, per currency and period. The rule is
T-FAM-05's to refine under two constraints: derived from constrained columns only, and never a tag on a ledger
row and never the read-model row itself. No `transactions` or `entries` row carries a goal identifier, so no goal
operation and no erasure step ever touches a ledger row (ADR-017 §2; ADR-018 §4.1); on erasure `goal_accounts`
rows of the subject are the subject's own rows and are deleted (Phase 3), and a `goal_accounts` row of a
contributor that names an erased owner's goal is nulled with the contributor's read-model rows (§3.4). The goal
total is computed per request and per currency over the `goal_contributions` rows the grant predicate admits and
therefore "reconciles exactly to authorized member contributions per currency" (T-FAM-05). No shared key, no copy
of one member's contribution inside another member's scope, and no household-scoped key exist; if a later record
introduces a household entity with its own key scope, joint goals may move to it only through a superseding
record (§11).

#### 1.6 The single-owner-scope rule

The three designs above follow one rule that every later multi-subject record must also follow: **a record
has exactly one owner scope (a user's DEK or a group's DEK) and every other participant is an opaque
reference inside it or holds their own record in their own scope.** No record is ever encrypted under two
users' keys, no user's DEK is ever wrapped for another user, and no participant's contact or payment handle
is ever copied into a shared record — settlement deep links resolve the live member's own channels at request
time (ADR-019, T-SPL-03), and an invitation to a placeholder seat keeps the invitee's contact in the invitation
record under the platform channel key, deleted on claim, expiry, the inviter's erasure or the group's orphaning
(§6.1) — so a shared row never carries anything that erasure would have to chase. Whatever channel delivers
shared or group data to a client — the on-demand operations of T-CON-04 or a change feed under T-SYN-01 — it
delivers plaintext of admitted fields under the same predicate, never ciphertext or key material, and the
client's copy is subject to the offline lease bound and to eviction on revocation or membership end (T-FAM-03,
T-FAM-04, the split client tickets).

### 2. Revocation: what a grantee can decrypt afterwards

**Answer: nothing.** Not the rows shared yesterday, not an aggregate, not a row whose ciphertext the grantee
somehow obtained, not after a restore. The statement holds for three independent reasons, each of which the
harness asserts separately (§7):

1. **No material.** At no point before, during or after a grant does any key, wrapped key or ciphertext
   addressed to or held by the grantee exist — not in `user_keys`, not in any other table, not in a response,
   not on the grantee's device. There is nothing to revoke cryptographically because nothing was granted
   cryptographically; the grantee's ability to *see* plaintext was a property of the server's per-transaction
   admission, never of the grantee.
2. **No admission.** After revocation the setter refuses any request naming the grantor (`grant_not_active`),
   the row policies admit no row of the grantor at any level, and the key policy admits no key row of the
   grantor. A request would have to pass all three to obtain one decrypted byte; each is evaluated in the
   database from the current `sharing_grants` state, none from a cache (ADR-018 §5.4), and the key-cache
   rule of §1.2 makes a stale in-memory key unusable without a fresh admission.
3. **No resurrection.** The revocation is a **journaled key event** (below), so a restore from a backup taken
   before the revocation re-applies it before the environment may serve traffic (T-SEC-07). A grant cannot
   come back from a backup any more than a destroyed key can (ADR-018 §6.6).

#### 2.1 Family grant revocation

- **Operation.** T-CON-04's revoke sets `sharing_grants.revoked_at` and removes the ReBAC tuple in one unit of
  work (ADR-018 §5.4). Either party may do it unilaterally (`grant_revoke`, ADR-005); no reason, no counterparty
  action, no notification content beyond a pointer (T-FAM-02, T-NOT-01).
- **Effect.** The next `begin_request` on any connection sees the revocation. The online bound is the one
  T-CON-04 publishes (no smaller than ADR-018's `transaction_timeout` plus request handling); the offline lease
  bound is T-CON-04's second number, enforced by the client evicting shared data (T-FAM-03, T-FAM-04). Neither
  is "immediate" (`architecture/01-domain-model.md` §3 rule 3); no user-facing string and no test says so.
- **Key consequence.** None. The grantor's DEK is not rotated, no copy is deleted, no re-encryption runs. A
  rotation would re-encrypt the grantor's whole history on every revocation for no confidentiality gain (the
  grantee never held material) and would leave the old key `DECRYPT_ONLY` for the backup window regardless
  (ADR-018 §6.4), so the bound would not even improve. Cache entries the grant admitted are evicted (§1.2).
- **Journal.** `grant.revoked {grant_id, revoked_at, reason ∈ {party, safe_exit, erasure, expiry}}` is appended to
  the key journal (ADR-018 §6.6; T-SEC-07 control store, identifiers only). Restore replay re-sets `revoked_at`
  on any grant row the journal names whose restored copy is still active (`restore_does_not_resurrect_revoked_grant`).
  T-FAM-02's "revocation is recorded as a key event" is satisfied by this entry; it carries no key material. The
  trail's own `sharing.grant.revoked` event is the acting party's own action and names only that principal as
  its subject (ADR-020 §4.1's `WITH CHECK`).
- **Historical access.** Because admission is evaluated per row against the current grant set, a revoked
  grant admits no row of any date: "historical access cut on revocation" (T-FAM-02) is the same denial as
  future access, not a second mechanism.
- **What the grantee keeps.** Memory, screenshots and any client cache until the offline lease elapses and the
  client evicts it. No cryptography reaches those; the coercive-control review of T-FAM-03 and the lease bound
  own that residue, and this record states it rather than claiming otherwise.

#### 2.2 Group membership end (leave or removal)

- **Operation.** `split_group_members.left_at` is set (by the member, or by an authorised member per T-SPL-03's
  authorization matrix). The setter's `verified_group_set()` filters on `left_at IS NULL`, so the next request
  carries no token for the group.
- **Effect.** `group_member_rows` admits no row of the group and `group_member_key_read` admits no key row, for
  rows of every date: leaving "removes access within the published online bound and historically" (T-SPL-03,
  restated with the two-bound rule). The group key is **not** rotated on membership end for the same reason the
  grantor's DEK is not: the member never held it. It continues to rotate on ADR-018's schedule and on compromise.
- **Journal and replay.** `membership.ended {group_id, member_id, left_at}`; replay re-applies it.
- **Placeholder afterwards.** A member who left is not a placeholder: `user_id` and the alias remain until the
  member's own erasure (§3.3), so remaining members still see who the seat was, as the group's record; only
  erasure removes the identity. Leaving is quiet: no notification describes it (T-FAM-02).

#### 2.3 Safe exit

Safe exit (T-FAM-02) is the revocation of every grant in both directions plus every membership end the
person chooses, performed in one request under step-up (ADR-019), quiet by design. It leaves no server-side
cache of shared data because this record permits no server-side copy of shared data to exist (§1.6); the
harness asserts the absence rather than a purge.

#### 2.4 Erasure as the strongest revocation

An erasure request performs safe exit for the subject in its first, reversible phase (§3.7) and then destroys
the subject's keys. Every counterparty's ability to decrypt the subject's data ends with the freeze; the
subject's ability to decrypt anyone else's data ends at the same instant, because the subject can no longer
obtain a context (`begin_request` refuses an account in `erasing` or `erased` state, a defence-in-depth check
T-SEC-01 adds to ADR-018's setter; sessions and refresh families are already revoked by ADR-019).

### 3. Erasure of records with more than one data subject

#### 3.1 Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | The account being erased (`users.id`, ADR-018's `owner_id`). |
| **Counterparty** | Any other account that holds a record referencing the subject: a split-group member, a joint-goal owner or contributor, a grantor or grantee. |
| **Owner scope** | The key that encrypts a record's descriptive columns: a user's DEK (`key_scope = user`) or a group's DEK (`key_scope = group`). Constrained columns have no key but belong to the same scope for erasure purposes. |
| **Participant reference** | A constrained, opaque column that names a person inside a record of another scope: `split_group_members.user_id`, `access_events.viewer_id`, `sharing_grants.grantor_id` / `grantee_id`. `member_id` is *not* a participant reference to a person: it is a group-local identifier that names a seat in the group and survives erasure. |
| **Placeholder** | A `split_group_members` row with `user_id IS NULL`. The product already has them for people who never installed the app (T-SPL-01); an erased member becomes one that can never be claimed. |
| **Erasure pseudonym** | The value that replaces a participant reference on a counterparty's row (§3.5). |
| **Erasure register** | The journaled `erasure.*` events of one erasure in ADR-018 §6.6's key journal (T-SEC-07 control store), including the per-context pseudonym entries (§3.5). |
| **Redaction** | Replacing an encrypted free-text value the subject authored with `NULL` plus a constrained `*_redacted_at` timestamp on the same row. |
| **Settlement direction** | `settlements.from_member` is the **paying** seat and `to_member` the **receiving** seat (`architecture/01-domain-model.md` §4.2: `X→Y ₹500` means X pays Y). A settlement therefore raises the payer's net position and lowers the receiver's. |

#### 3.2 The rule

1. **One owner scope per record** (§1.6). Erasure destroys the subject's own scope by key destruction
   (ADR-006, ADR-018 §6.7) and never destroys another scope's key.
2. **Every participant reference to the subject outside the subject's own scope is severed or
   pseudonymised**, so that after erasure no live row outside ADR-020's trail and chain joins `users.id` of the
   subject to any counterparty (`erasure_placeholder_unlinkable`, §7) — with **one named exception**: the
   `erasure.pseudonymise` entries of the erasure register, which join the subject to each counterparty context
   for exactly as long as the trail events they exist to re-project, are forward-lookup-only for their two
   readers, and are bounded and published in §3.5 and §4.2. Constrained ledger facts on those rows — amounts,
   currencies, instants, opaque seat and group identifiers — are never changed.
3. **Free text the subject authored inside another scope is redacted; free text a counterparty authored is
   the counterparty's record and is retained**, even where it may mention the subject. A group's own collective
   fields (`split_groups.name`) have no author and are retained.
4. **Balances are derived, never stored, and therefore never rewritten.** Every net balance, settlement
   proposal and goal total is recomputed from surviving constrained rows (ADR-017 §3, §6.3); the harness asserts
   that every surviving member's balance is identical before and after (§7). Erasure never posts an entry, a
   share, a settlement or a reversal, and a reversal is never used as erasure (ADR-017 §7).
5. **Every step is idempotent, journaled by identifier and count, and replayed on restore** before the
   restored environment may serve traffic (T-SEC-07). Re-running erasure for an erased subject is a no-op
   (T-SEC-02).
6. **Nothing is notified to counterparties about the erasure.** No outbox row, no push, no message. The erased
   state is nevertheless **visible on the counterparty's next read**, explicitly: the wire carries
   `member_state = erased`, `description_state` / `note_state = redacted_by_erasure`, `viewer: {erased: true}`
   and the completeness reason `member_erased` (§6.2), because a redacted description or a partial total that
   pretended to be something else would mislead the counterparty about their own record. What this rule forbids
   is a *push*: a "this person deleted their account" message would be a coercion signal (T-FAM-02, T-FAM-03).
   That a grantor can infer from `viewer: {erased: true}` that a former viewer erased rather than left is an
   inherent residue and is published in §4.2.

#### 3.3 A split-group member is erased

Given member `M` of group `G` with seat `member_id = m`:

| Row set | Effect | Unchanged |
|---|---|---|
| `split_group_members` row of `m` | `left_at` set (ADR-018 §6.7, under `begin_keyops(M)`); then under `begin_group_keyops(G)`: `user_id → NULL`, `display_alias → NULL`, `placeholder_name → NULL`, `erased_at` set, `role` kept. The row becomes an **unclaimable placeholder**: the atomic identity claim of T-SPL-01 refuses a row with `erased_at` set, and no merge of an erased placeholder into any member is ever offered (`erasure_placeholder_unclaimable`). | `member_id`, `group_id`, `joined_at` |
| `split_expenses` with `payer_member_id = m` | Nothing to the money. `description` is redacted **only where `description_author_member_id = m`** (`description → NULL`, `description_redacted_at` set). The author seat is fixed at `INSERT`; a later change of description is a **reversal and re-post** (ADR-017 §6.3, never an in-place `UPDATE`), whose new row carries its own author and `description_redacted_at = NULL`, while the redacted row remains as the reversed history. | `amount_minor`, `currency`, `occurred_at`, `payer_member_id`, `group_id` |
| `split_expenses` whose description `m` authored, for another payer | `description` redacted as above. | everything else |
| `split_shares` with `member_id = m` | Nothing. The share is the counterparties' evidence of the debt or credit and the operand of `SUM(share_minor) = amount_minor`. | all columns |
| `settlements` with `from_member = m` or `to_member = m` | `note` redacted where `note_author_member_id = m`. | `amount_minor`, `currency`, `settled_at`, `method`, both seats |
| Split disputes (T-CON-04) naming `m` | Nothing: they carry seats, states and an evidence-free reason code, no free text. | all |
| `split_groups.name` | Retained: a collective field with no author. | — |
| Group DEK | Unchanged unless `G` is now orphaned (§1.4), in which case it is destroyed in the same job. | — |
| Every other group `M` belonged to | The same steps, one `begin_group_keyops` transaction per group, including groups `M` had already left. | — |

**Balance invariant.** With `from_member` the paying seat (§3.1), for every currency and every surviving seat
`s ≠ m` in `G`,

`net(s) = Σ paid by s − Σ shares of s + Σ settled from s − Σ settled to s`

is computed from the same rows before and after erasure and is therefore identical; the placeholder's own net
balance is also unchanged and remains visible to the counterparties as owed to or by "a former member". A later
settlement recorded by a counterparty naming the placeholder seat `m` is an ordinary settlement row, **accepted
under T-SPL-03's authorization matrix and dispute path exactly as a settlement against a never-claimed
placeholder is today** — never unconditionally, since no principal can confirm or dispute on the seat's behalf;
no deep link can be produced for it because the subject's channels are gone (ADR-018 §6.7) and shared rows never
held a payment handle (§1.6). Debt simplification (T-SPL-01) runs over the same seats and produces the same
proposals.

**Worked example** (INR, minor units are paise). `A` pays a team dinner of 240000 for seats `A`, `B`, `C`, equal
shares of 80000 each, description "Team dinner" entered by `A`. Before any settlement: `net(a) = 240000 − 80000
= 160000`, `net(b) = net(c) = −80000`. `B` then pays `A` 80000 (`from_member = b`, `to_member = a`): `net(b) = 0`,
`net(a) = 80000`, `net(c) = −80000` — `C` still owes. `A` is erased. Afterwards: the expense row still says payer
`a`, 240000, the three share rows still say 80000 each, the settlement still says `b → a` 80000; every `net` above
is unchanged; the simplification proposal is unchanged; the description reads as redacted because `A` authored
it; the seat `a` shows as a former member with no name; if `B` had entered the same expense the description
"Team dinner" would survive as `B`'s record. This example — one expense, three shares, one settlement `b → a` —
is the normative test vector for `erasure_counterparty_balances_unchanged` and for the sign convention (§7).
**A second, separate fixture** for the redaction rule: a settlement `A → C` of 50000 recorded with a note by `A`
keeps its amount, seats and instant and loses the note (in a group where that row exists, the nets are of course
different — `net(a) = 130000`, `net(c) = −130000` with the vector's other rows — which is why it is not part of
the vector).

#### 3.4 A joint-goal participant is erased

- **A contributor is erased.** The contributor's `goal_contributions` rows are the contributor's own §1.5 read
  model — a non-authoritative cache recomputed from the contributor's entries (ADR-017 §3.5) — and are deleted
  by the erasure job exactly as ADR-018 §6.7 deletes the subject's `period_aggregates`; the underlying entries
  survive under the unlinkable `owner_id` like every ledger fact, no ledger row changes and no ledger sum
  changes, and no row now joins those entries to the goal owner's goal. The goal owner's `goals` row is
  untouched. The live goal total — a display total over a read model, never a ledger quantity — recomputes over
  the surviving admitted contributions and is reported **partial** with the non-identifying completeness reason
  `member_erased` (T-FAM-05, T-CON-04), which the viewer's own household roster row supplies (§3.5). **No copy or
  snapshot of the erased member's contributions is retained in any counterparty's scope**: a contribution to a
  shared target creates no claim by the goal owner over the contributor's savings, so there is no legal position
  that would justify keeping the erased person's amounts against their erasure (§5.2), and under the
  coercive-control threat the household dashboard must not become a record of a partner who left. The household
  aggregate of T-FAM-05 behaves identically (`member_erased` completeness); its differencing-resistance rules,
  not this record, govern what a before/after comparison reveals.
- **The goal owner is erased.** The goal's name, target and every other descriptive field are under the owner's
  DEK and become unreadable to everyone; the constrained `goals` row survives as the subject's own row under
  the unlinkable `owner_id`. Because a contributor's `goal_contributions.goal_id` and `goal_accounts.goal_id` would
  otherwise be live joins from the contributor to the erased owner, the erasure job sets `goal_id → NULL` and
  `goal_erased_at` on every contributor's read-model and link rows that named the goal (`erasure_contribution_rows`,
  §6.1) — writes to a read model and a link table, never to a ledger row; the contributor's entries are untouched
  and their surfaces render the rows against a goal that no longer resolves (the copy is the taxonomy's, not this
  record's, and must not imply the person's identity). Nothing of a contributor's is destroyed.

#### 3.5 Family sharing counterparties

There is no shared record, so erasure changes only references:

- **The subject was a grantor.** Every grant the subject issued is revoked (§2.1, reason `erasure`) and then
  deleted (§3.6). The grantee's `access_events` rows about the subject's data are *the subject's own rows*
  (`owner_id = subject`) and are deleted under ADR-020's `transparency_erasure`. The grantee keeps nothing of the
  subject's data because the grantee never held any.
- **The subject was a grantee.** Every grant the subject received is revoked and deleted. On each grantor's
  `access_events` rows where `viewer_id = subject`, the erasure job — as `pennilogic_keyops` under
  `begin_keyops(subject, erasure job)`, admitted only by ADR-020's `transparency_erasure` — replaces `viewer_id`
  with the **erasure pseudonym** and sets `viewer_erased = true`; the grantor keeps "an erased account viewed
  this category at this level on this date under this policy" and nothing more (ADR-018 §6.7, ADR-020
  §8.6–§8.7). **The job deletes only rows with `owner_id = subject`**; a counterparty's row on which the subject
  was the viewer is the counterparty's record and is only ever pseudonymised, never deleted
  (`erasure_deletes_only_own_transparency_rows`, §7). The subject's own `transparency.entry` rows are deleted
  under the same policy (ADR-020 §8.7).

**The erasure pseudonym and the erasure register.** A random UUIDv4 minted by the erasure job **once per
(subject, counterparty context)** — one per grantor for `access_events`, and one per roster owner where a
T-FAM-05 household roster or snapshot-metadata row names the subject (such rows hold the subject only as an
opaque reference and never a value of the subject's, §1.6) — and written only into the rows it replaces. It
is **not** derived from any key or from the subject's identifier, so it cannot be recomputed from a `users`
tombstone by anyone. It **is recorded in the erasure register**: the journaled `erasure.pseudonymise` event
carries `(subject, context_kind, context_id, pseudonym)` for every context (ADR-018 §6.6's key journal in the
T-SEC-07 control store; identifiers only, no key material), because ADR-020 §8.4 item 5 and §13 item 12
require a full re-projection of the transparency tables after an erasure to reproduce **exactly** the
post-erasure state, row for row, and a value that cannot be looked up cannot be reproduced. The rules:

- **Context enumeration.** The contexts are enumerated from durable relationship rows, never from the projection:
  for `access_events`, every grantor in the subject's grantee-side `sharing_grants` rows (including revoked and
  expired ones), read in Phase 4 **before** those rows are deleted (§3.6); for rosters, every T-FAM-05 roster owner
  whose row names the subject. A grantor whose only read events are still in the relay's backlog therefore has a
  register entry before the relay reaches them.
- **Write-once, read-before-mint.** The register key is `(subject, context_kind, context_id)`. The job reads the
  register for the key before minting; an existing entry is reused, never replaced; a second append for the same
  key is refused by the control-store writer and ignored by every reader (first entry wins). A crash between the
  register append and the row `UPDATE`, or a resumed or re-run job, therefore mints nothing for a registered context
  (`erasure_resume_reuses_registered_pseudonym`, §7). The job keeps no working map of its own (§6.1).
- **Ordering against the relay (completeness), fail-closed projection (guarantee).** Phase 1 records in
  `erasure.started` the **`freeze_position`: the trail head — the highest committed `audit.event` position —
  observed once the T-CON-04 online revocation bound has elapsed after the freeze** (ADR-018 §5.4: no smaller than
  `transaction_timeout` plus request handling). The bound matters because a context admitted *before* the freeze is
  MAC-bound to its transaction and is not re-evaluated, so such a transaction can still commit one `sharing.read.*`
  event naming the subject up to the online bound after the freeze (§2.1: neither bound is "immediate"); after the
  bound no context admits either read, so every event naming the subject as owner or viewer is at or below
  `freeze_position`. **Phase 3's deletion of the subject's own projection rows and Phase 4's pseudonymisation run
  only after the projector's gap-safe cursor (ADR-020 §8.4 item 2) has passed `freeze_position`**, so the job's
  `UPDATE … WHERE viewer_id = subject` sees every row it must rewrite — this wait is the **completeness** mechanism.
  A projector that has not passed the position within the profile's rights clock pages
  (`erasure.waiting_for_projector_seconds`, §8); the job waits, it never proceeds around the relay. The
  **no-raw-identifier guarantee** does not rest on the wait: it rests on the fail-closed projector rule below,
  which holds under every interleaving of a late event with Phase 4.
- **The projector applies the register on every insert, and fails closed.** For every projection insert (backlog
  or rebuild) the relay consults the register keyed on **`erasure.started` without a later `erasure.cancelled`**
  for the same request (§3.7), not on completion: a row whose owner is such a subject is **not projected**; a row
  whose viewer is such a subject is projected with the registered pseudonym and `viewer_erased = true`; a row whose
  viewer is such a subject **and for whose context no register entry exists is not inserted** — it is
  dead-lettered and paged, never inserted raw and never minted by the relay, which is `INSERT`-only on the
  projections and no register writer (`access_events_projection_fails_closed_without_register_entry`, §7).
  **Re-drive rule:** a "no register entry" dead letter is retried once the entry exists and, at the latest, at
  `erasure.completed` (by then every context is registered), so a grantor's legitimate record of the erased
  viewer's access is delayed, never lost (ADR-005); a dead letter for a subject whose request is later
  `cancelled` is re-driven as a normal projection. Keying on the freeze also covers a backlog self-actor event of
  the subject between Phase 3 and Phase 6, which would otherwise re-insert an owner row the job already deleted.
  This is a tightening of ADR-020 §8.4 item 5 this record forces on the relay (T-SEC-03).
- **Bounded retention, forward-only readers, restorable key.** A register entry joins the erased `users.id` to a
  live counterparty outside the trail and chain, so it is bounded to the purpose that justifies it — but the
  bound must not outrun the backups that can bring a served partition back. The rule: the `subject` column of each
  `erasure.pseudonymise` entry is replaced by a **keyed digest `HMAC(K_register, subject)`** — `K_register` a
  platform key of ADR-018 §6.1's class held by the custodian, never in the database backup set — **no earlier
  than the T-PLT-02 backup retention after the served trail partition was archived** under ADR-020 §8.7 (b), and
  never later than the profile's `erasure_register_retention_days` (§5.4). Every restorable backup therefore
  either predates the drop (the raw `subject` is present) or postdates the archival by the whole retention
  window (no restorable backup holds that partition live); and the T-SEC-07 restore path and the relay, which hold
  the trail's raw subject UUID, can recompute the digest and look the entry up **after** the drop as well. The
  pseudonym value stays so `viewer_erased` rows remain stable and rebuildable; the mapping back to the person
  survives only as the digest, reversible by nobody without `K_register` and the UUID. The relay and the restore
  path receive a **forward lookup only**, `(subject-or-digest, context_kind, context_id) → pseudonym`, never a bulk
  read and never a reverse lookup; a reverse lookup (pseudonym → subject) is an ADR-020 audit-role capability,
  admitted only as a recorded `admin_recorded_access` read with a purpose code, exactly like a read of the trail
  row that already names the subject (`erasure_register_bounded_and_forward_only`, §7).
- **Stability.** The pseudonym is stable within a run, across a T-SEC-07 restore replay and across an ADR-020
  projection rebuild — the job, the replay and the projector all read the same register entry
  (`erasure_pseudonym_properties`, `transparency_rebuild_reproduces_post_erasure_state`, §7).
- **Who can link.** Within the register's bound, who can link a pseudonym back to the erased subject is exactly
  who can already read the trail: the audit roles ADR-020 names, since `access_events.id` is the projected
  `audit_event_id` whose trail row still names the subject (ADR-018 §9). The subject-facing surface, the
  counterparty, `pennilogic_app`, the relay (forward lookup only) and a holder of the projection alone cannot
  link it, and no party can link the same subject's pseudonyms across counterparties without the register or
  the trail. The register is published as residue in §4.2 beside the trail rows.

`access_events.viewer_id` and every roster member reference carry **no foreign key to `users`** so that a
pseudonym is a valid value; `viewer_erased` / `member_erased` tell the reading surface not to resolve the
reference through any grant.

#### 3.6 Grant rows

ADR-018 §6.7 revokes and "tombstones" the subject's grants in both directions without fixing the tombstone's
shape — its `erasure_grant_rows` is `FOR UPDATE` on `revoked_at`, so under ADR-018 alone the row survives — and
delegates to this record the shape of the placeholder that preserves other members' balances. This record fixes
the tombstone's shape, and does so as a **deliberate, flagged extension** of ADR-018's step (§6.1, §9, §10). A live
`sharing_grants` row that keeps the subject's UUID next to a counterparty's is exactly the join
that §3.2 rule 2 forbids, and it serves no purpose after erasure: the relationship is over and the
counterparty's record of what was viewed lives in the pseudonymised projection. The tombstone is therefore
the journal entry `grant.revoked {…, reason: erasure}` followed by **deletion of the row** by the erasure job
(`erasure_grant_rows` extended to `DELETE`, §6.1). This is the one place this record removes rather than marks
a grant; the grant model stays append-only for every actor and every purpose other than the journaled
erasure of a party.

#### 3.7 Order of operations

The erasure job runs as `pennilogic_keyops`, is resumable from its own progress rows, and is the only
writer of the steps below. Phases 2 to 6 are idempotent and are what T-SEC-07 replays after a restore.

**Who may start what.** A request is created (Phase 0) by the subject as a `user` actor under step-up
(ADR-019), or by an operator **fulfilling** a rights request whose identity verification T-ADM-13 recorded — an
operator never originates one — recorded as ADR-020's `account_action`; a `delegate:<client_id>` context (a tool
call, ADR-019/ADR-022) cannot. Phase 1 (freeze) may follow a single-operator start: it is reversible and
protective. **Phase 2 is irreversible and is the one action in the system that cannot be undone, so for an
operator-started request it is gated as ADR-020 gates its capability-gated actions** (§5 item 4, §8.4 item 3): a
**distinct approver** eligible under T-ADM-06 who is neither the requester nor the originator records an
approval bound to the rights-request identifier as a chain-scoped `admin.*` event, and Phase 2 runs only after
the chain verifier has confirmed that event. A subject-started request needs no approver — the subject's
step-up is the authority — but is gated by the same statutory hold below. A single-operator start of Phase 2 is
refused (`erasure_not_startable_by_delegate`, `erasure_operator_phase2_requires_confirmed_approval`, §7).

**Two gates between Phase 1 and Phase 2.** (a) The product cancellation window, `erasure_cancellation_window_hours`
(may be zero), during which the subject can cancel (below). (b) The **statutory pre-shred hold**,
`statutory_pre_shred_retention_days` (§5.4; `0` until counsel records that DPDP Rules 2025 Rule 8(3) or another
retention obligation applies, R-9): while it is non-zero the request stays `frozen` for that period after the
freeze — no login, no cross-user read in either direction, safe exit already done — and the subject's data is
readable in that period **only** through ADR-020's lawful-request path as `admin_recorded_access` with a
Seventh-Schedule purpose code, never through a customer context; the T-CON-09 state carries the profile period so
the subject is told (`erasure_honours_statutory_hold`, §7). The hold is a profile value, so counsel's decision is
a hand-off, not a superseding record.

**Cancellation.** For the **whole pre-shred period** — the product window (a) **and** any statutory hold (b),
i.e. until Phase 2 starts — the subject may cancel. Phase 1 has revoked every session and the setter refuses a
customer context for an `erasing` account, so cancellation is a **T-CMP-01 rights-request state transition
`frozen → cancelled` authenticated by the same proof as intake** — the step-up or channel-verified path of
ADR-019's recovery-style flows, carried as a `preauth` / restricted-`acr` context of ADR-018 §5.2 and never a
customer context (`erasure_cancellation_reachable_during_freeze`, §7). It is refused once Phase 2 has started.
**The same transition writes `erasure.cancelled {request_id, subject, cancelled_at}` to the key journal** (§6.1)
in the same unit of work as the request-state flip, so a cancellation lives outside the backup set exactly as the
freeze does: on restore, a `frozen` request whose journal carries `erasure.cancelled` is re-cancelled before
readiness, and a request with an outstanding `erasure.started` but neither `erasure.cancelled` nor `erasure.shred`
is **held, never resumed**, until an operator re-verifies with the person that the request still stands
(`restore_does_not_resurrect_cancelled_erasure`, §7); T-SEC-07's "zero outstanding entries" counts such
started-without-completed requests as outstanding until they are re-verified or re-cancelled. The projector and
the rebuild key on `erasure.started` **without a later `erasure.cancelled`** (§3.5), so a person who cancels keeps
their access transparency: their events committed during the freeze were projected normally (Phase 3 never ran),
their new events project normally as owner and as viewer, and any dead letter raised for them during the freeze is
re-driven. The person is told that grants stay revoked and memberships stay ended and must be re-created, and that
their transparency log is intact (T-TRU-02 owns the copy). `cancelled` is a T-CON-09 state (§6.2).

**The job never unwraps a key**: key destruction, placeholder conversion, redaction, pseudonymisation and
deletion are all writes of `NULL`, constrained values or state, so no phase places the subject's or any group's
plaintext key in the job's memory (`erasure_job_unwraps_no_key`, §7).

**The job writes the key journal, never the trail.** ADR-020 §4.1 binds each `audit.event` namespace to one
role by a `FOR INSERT` policy and grants `pennilogic_keyops` none, so the erasure's two trail records are
written as follows and by nobody else: **intake** — the T-CMP-01 rights path as `pennilogic_app` in the
`consent.*` namespace (ADR-020 §4.1 binds `consent.*` to `pennilogic_app`, and §8.7 (b) treats `consent.*` rows as customer-actor trail events; the rights-request lifecycle is placed in that namespace by this record and T-CMP-01), `actor_class = self`,
`actor_principal_id = subject_id = subject`, action `consent.rights.erasure.requested`, projected to the
subject's own `transparency.entry` as `self_action`; **completion** — the same T-CMP-01 path as `pennilogic_app`
in the `consent.*` namespace, `actor_class = system`, `actor_principal_id` null (ADR-020 §10 item 1), action
`consent.rights.erasure.completed`, carrying the receipt identifier and the two policy versions of Phase 6 and
no personal-data field; not projected (the subject's transparency rows are gone). For an operator fulfilment
the ADR-020 `account_action` and its approval event are additional chain-scoped `admin.*` events by
`admin_api_owner`. If ADR-020's `consent.*` policy does not admit a null actor for `system`, T-SEC-03
(`PenniLogic/api#39`) adds that admission for exactly this action under ADR-018 §1.1's additive procedure; the
one fixed constraint is that the inserter is never `pennilogic_keyops` (`erasure_trail_events_exact`, §7).

#### 4.2 What still leaks, and its bound

Stated so that no ticket claims more than the design delivers:

- **Amount and time correlation.** A split expense is a copy of the payer's transaction (amount, instant);
  the payer's own ledger row survives under the unlinkable `owner_id` with the same amount and instant. A
  reader with a database dump can therefore link seat `m` to owner `M` probabilistically by matching
  constrained values. The identifier-level join is removed; the statistical one is inherent to ADR-018 §1.3's
  plaintext money and is accepted and published as residue exactly as ADR-018 §9 publishes the underlying
  leak.
- **Free text authored by counterparties** may name the subject; it is the counterparty's record (§3.2 rule 3)
  and is not searched or rewritten.
- **The trail and chain** keep the subject's opaque UUID as `subject_id` (and as `viewer_id` on the counterparty's
  `sharing.read.*` events) in `audit.event` rows for the profile's security-log floor — cross-user reads by
  identified counterparties and administrative accesses — retained unrewritten for ADR-020's retention (§8.7
  periods (a)–(b)), **readable only by the audit roles ADR-020 names, never by the counterparty**, pseudonymised
  on detached partitions where the profile requires (ADR-020 §8.7 (d)), and published to the T-CMP-04 profile
  beside the backup bound exactly as ADR-018 §9 publishes them. `access_events.id` equals the projected
  `audit_event_id`, so a party holding the trail can reverse the projection's pseudonym for as long as the trail
  row exists; the same holds for a reader of the erasure register (§3.5). The projection's pseudonymisation
  protects the subject-facing surface, the counterparty and the application role, not an adversary who already
  holds the trail or the register.
- **Backups.** Until T-PLT-02 retention elapses, a pre-erasure backup contains the subject's wrapped keys and
  ciphertext (ADR-018 §6.7), the pre-redaction group ciphertext together with the still-live group key, the
  pre-pseudonymisation `viewer_id` and `user_id` values in the clear and, for a backup taken during the run, the
  job's progress rows. A party holding such a backup **and** custodian unwrap rights on that KEK version can
  recover the first two; the rest need the backup alone. The key journal's `grant.revoked` identifiers and the
  register's pseudonym entries likewise resolve to identified parties only against such a backup or the trail.
  The live system is clean from Phase 4; a restored backup is re-cleaned by replay before readiness (with `K_ctx`
  rotated first, ADR-018 §6.6); the backup itself is unreadable without the backup key and expires with T-PLT-02.
  **The erasure-completeness bound of this record is therefore the same as ADR-018 §6.7's: the T-PLT-02 backup
  retention window**, published to T-CMP-04 and T-SEC-02 as one number for both the crypto-shredded and the
  physically overwritten residue.
- **Counterparties' memory, screenshots, rendered notifications and offline caches** until the lease bound or
  their next sync; see §2.1. A counterparty's device shows the subject's alias until the placeholder reaches it.
- **The erasure register** (§3.5): each `erasure.pseudonymise` entry joins the erased `users.id` to one live
  counterparty context and one pseudonym, in the T-SEC-07 control store outside the trail and chain. It exists
  so ADR-020's rebuild can reproduce the projection; it is forward-lookup-only for the relay and the restore
  path; a reverse lookup is a recorded ADR-020 audit-role read; its raw subject is replaced by a keyed digest no
  earlier than T-PLT-02 retention after the served trail partition is archived and no later than
  `erasure_register_retention_days`, after which only a holder of `K_register` and the UUID can match an entry. A
  control-store reader or a holder of its backup within that bound can link every pseudonym of the subject to the
  subject and, through `owner_id`, to the subject's constrained ledger residue — the same capability the trail
  already gives ADR-020's audit roles, now stated for the register as well.
- **A grantor's own knowledge.** Pseudonymisation removes the identifier, not the grantor's memory: a grantor with
  few grantees can tell from their own grant history who the pseudonymised former viewer was. Inherent to any
  sharing model; relevant to the coercive-control review T-FAM-03 owns.
- **The `erased` states are themselves disclosures.** `viewer: {erased: true}`, `member_state = erased` and
  `redacted_by_erasure` tell the counterparty that the former viewer or member *erased* rather than left (ADR-020
  §8.6 changes a row only on erasure). Accepted because the alternative — misrepresenting the counterparty's own
  record — is worse (§3.2 rule 6); the copy T-TRU-04 and the split clients render must not embellish it.
- **Authentication artefacts** of the subject are purged at Phase 3 (§3.7); nothing of them survives beyond the
  `auth.*` trail events, which carry no identifying value (ADR-019 §16).

#### 5.2 The position

1. **A user's own data** is erased on request (R-2) or when the purpose ends (R-1) by key destruction and the
   physical clearing of platform-scoped values (ADR-018 §6.7), with the completeness bound of §4.2, after the
   product cancellation window and any statutory pre-shred hold the profile records (R-9). No inactivity timer
   applies (R-6).
2. **A counterparty's evidence of a claim** survives the other party's erasure as constrained money plus a
   stable pseudonymous seat (R-2 specified purpose; R-3), for as long as the counterparty's own account and
   purpose exist; it is erased with the counterparty in turn. Descriptive content authored by the erased party
   is not part of the claim and is redacted.
3. **A counterparty's evidence of a shared target** does not include the erased party's contributions (§3.4):
   no claim, no basis, no retention.
4. **A counterparty's transparency record** of who viewed *their* data survives with the viewer pseudonymised,
   because it is the counterparty's own record about access to the counterparty's data (ADR-005; ADR-020 §8.7)
   and holds no value of the erased party.
5. **The trail and chain** survive unchanged for the profile's security-log floor (R-4, R-5) under ADR-020's
   access controls and post-retention pseudonymisation.
6. **PenniLogic's own statutory business records** — invoices and tax records of a paying account (GST,
   `compliance` O-19, T-BIL-04) — are a class the profile must enumerate as a narrowly retained statutory
   record; they are not shared data and this record fixes nothing about them beyond requiring that they hold
   no ledger content of the subject.

### 6. Contracts, schema and the machine-readable artefact

This record is the contract input. Every consequence below is **additive** to ADR-018's `crypto-inventory`
(extended under its §1.1 procedure with a version bump by the owning ticket) and to the published contracts;
none loosens a class, predicate or lifecycle rule. **One enumeration is extended, and is named as such:** ADR-018
§4.1's list of `pennilogic_keyops` erasure grants ("never a `DELETE` outside that list") grows by the erasure
writes below. Every addition is scoped to the subject's own rows by a policy of the form `TO pennilogic_keyops
USING (<owner or subject column> = (SELECT app_ctx.verified_customer_principal()))` under `begin_keyops(subject,
job)` — column-level for `UPDATE`, with an explicit post-state `WITH CHECK` wherever the admitted write changes
the compared column — is journaled, and is carried by the `crypto-inventory` version bump so that ADR-018 §4.2's
CI lint has one source of truth for the enumeration. **Every erasure policy whose admitted `UPDATE` or `DELETE`
locates rows by a subject-referencing `WHERE` also admits `SELECT` for `pennilogic_keyops` under the same
`USING`**: PostgreSQL applies the `SELECT` (or `ALL`) policies to the rows an `UPDATE`/`DELETE` reads, and a row
that fails them is *silently suppressed* (row count `0`, no error), so a `FOR UPDATE`-only policy on a row the
subject does not own would locate nothing. The shape is the one `group_keyops_rows` already uses. Where this
record writes `FOR SELECT, UPDATE[, DELETE]` it means one `CREATE POLICY` per command (PostgreSQL takes one command
per `FOR`, or `FOR ALL` restricted by table grants) sharing the stated `USING`, with the `WITH CHECK` on the
`UPDATE` policy only and the narrower clause on the `DELETE` policy where stated. `app_ctx.verified_group_set()`
returns `begin_group_keyops`'s group under the `keyops:` actor (ADR-018 §5.2 lists `keyops:%` among the customer
actors), so `group_key_ops` and `group_keyops_rows` admit exactly that group's rows.

#### 6.2 Contract consequences

- **T-CON-04 (sharing) and T-CON-09 (transparency payload).** Membership shape carries `member_state ∈ {active,
  left, placeholder, erased}` and no contact or payment handle; expense and settlement shapes carry
  `description_state` / `note_state ∈ {present, absent, redacted_by_erasure}`; the transparency-log entry
  (ADR-020's one payload contract, T-CON-09) carries `viewer: {ref, erased}` where `ref` is opaque; the household
  and joint-goal completeness enumeration includes `member_erased`; the grant reference remains the ADR-018
  policy identifier; the revocation section states both bounds and, in prose, that revocation performs no key
  rotation because no key was ever issued. No shape may carry a key, wrapped key, ciphertext, nonce, key
  identifier or blind-index value (`no_grantee_bound_key_material_exists`).
- **T-CON-09 (rights).** The erasure operation reports states `queued`, `frozen` (cancellable while the product
  window runs; during a statutory hold it carries the profile period, §3.7), `cancelled`, `shredding`,
  `completing`, `complete` and carries the receipt identifier; the export produced
  before erasure contains the subject's own rows, and of a group's rows only those that name the subject's seat
  (expenses the subject paid or shares in, settlements to or from the subject) with other seats as opaque
  references and other members' authored text omitted — never a counterparty's own rows or another member's
  contributions.
- **T-CON-12 (errors).** `grant_not_active` is unchanged and is the only cause a revoked or erased counterparty
  ever produces for a viewer; no new problem code distinguishes "erased" from "revoked" (identical copy,
  `product/client-state-taxonomy.md` §3.6).
- **Client-state taxonomy (T-UX-01).** A placeholder, a redacted description, a pseudonymised viewer and a
  partial goal are rendered in the surface's ordinary state with canonical copy that names no person; they are
  not `permission_denied`. Any new copy string is added to the taxonomy data file first (taxonomy §13); this
  record forces no new state identifier.
- **Contracts repository.** T-SEC-01 copies the artefact of §6.3 beside ADR-018's `crypto-inventory`; dependent
  tickets consume it rather than restating it.

### 7. The verification harness and the conformance tests

**`erasure_completeness_harness`** is one named, versioned suite that T-SEC-02 (`PenniLogic/api#38`) owns and
runs against synthetic subjects in staging in full, and in production as a read-only residue scan after
every erasure. It exists because ADR-006 promised an outcome that no ticket had proven: the ticket's
acceptance criterion is a harness that shows erasure is *real* (nothing of the subject is decryptable by any
role) and *non-destructive to others* (every counterparty's balances are unchanged). Its assertions are
named below so that tickets can reference them; the owning ticket is in brackets.

**Erasure is real**

- `erasure_no_decryptable_byte_any_role` [T-SEC-02] — after Phase 6, for every table and every `encrypted`
  and `blind-indexed` column, every row whose owner scope is the subject and every group-row value the subject
  authored: the production decrypt routine fails under every context the harness can mint (each counterparty
  as `user`, `pennilogic_keyops` under `begin_keyops(subject)`, ADR-020's `admin_recorded_access` and redaction
  contexts), and a direct attempt with every `user_keys` row still present for the subject fails because the
  rows are `DESTROYED_BY_ERASURE` with no material. Redacted group values are `NULL`, not ciphertext.
- `erasure_no_key_material_remains` [T-SEC-02, T-SEC-06] — no wrapped copy of any destroyed key exists in any
  table (`user_keys`, `group_keys` for orphaned groups, nowhere else, because §1.6 permits nowhere else), and the
  custodian holds no data key for the subject.
- `erasure_backup_bound_published` [T-SEC-07, T-PLT-02] — a pre-erasure backup restored into an isolated
  environment is not admitted to readiness until the replay of phases 2–6 has run; before replay the subject's
  residue is exactly the published class of §4.2 and nothing else; after replay
  `erasure_no_decryptable_byte_any_role` passes there too (ADR-018's `restore_after_erasure_stays_erased`
  extended to the group and projection steps); no backup older than the T-PLT-02 retention exists.
- `erasure_placeholder_unlinkable` [T-SEC-02, T-SPL-03] — after erasure, a full-table scan outside
  `audit.event` / `chain.record` finds the subject's `users.id` in no `split_group_members.user_id`, no
  `sharing_grants` row, no `access_events.viewer_id`, no roster member reference and no `owner_id` of a row
  that is not the subject's own ledger, and finds no `goal_contributions.goal_id` of any other owner pointing at
  a goal the subject owned; the harness also asserts that every `viewer_erased` / `member_erased` pseudonym is a
  UUIDv4 that matches no `users.id`.
- `erasure_authored_text_redacted` and `erasure_counterparty_text_retained` [T-SPL-03] — descriptions and notes
  with `description_author_member_id` / `note_author_member_id` = the subject's seat are `NULL` with `*_redacted_at` set; those authored by
  counterparties are byte-identical ciphertext under the unchanged group key.
- `erasure_pseudonym_properties` [T-SEC-02, T-SEC-07] — within one run the same counterparty context receives
  one pseudonym on every row; two contexts of the same subject receive different pseudonyms; every value written
  to a row is present in the register's `erasure.pseudonymise` entry and no other pseudonym value exists anywhere;
  a T-SEC-07 restore replay reproduces exactly the same values from the register; the register carries no key
  material; a pseudonym cannot be recomputed from the `users` tombstone by any key; **a keyops `UPDATE` that
  writes a `viewer_id` or `member_ref` value absent from the register fails** (the job writes only registered
  values; the harness plants an unregistered value and asserts the suite fails).
- `erasure_rls_admits_exactly_the_post_erasure_row` [T-SEC-01, T-SEC-03, T-FAM-05] — under `begin_keyops(subject,
  job)` the production pseudonymisation `UPDATE` on a counterparty's `access_events` row commits under RLS
  (`transparency_erasure`'s `WITH CHECK (owner_id = principal OR viewer_erased = true)`), as do the roster
  (`WITH CHECK (member_erased = true)`) and contribution (`WITH CHECK (goal_id IS NULL AND goal_erased_at IS NOT
  NULL)`) updates — **the positive case asserts affected row count > 0 on planted fixtures**, which is what the
  `SELECT` admissions of §6 make possible, and the Phase 4 context enumeration `SELECT` on `sharing_grants` returns
  the subject's grants. Negative cases are asserted by their real PostgreSQL semantics: a planted `UPDATE` of a row
  whose viewer or member is not the subject is a **`USING` miss — row count `0` and the row unchanged, no error**;
  a planted `UPDATE` of any other column is refused by the column grant; a planted write that sets `viewer_erased =
  false`, `member_erased = false` or a non-null `goal_id` is a **`WITH CHECK` violation — "new row violates
  row-level security policy"**; a planted `DELETE` of a counterparty's row on which the subject was the viewer is a
  `USING` miss on the `DELETE` policy (row count `0`). No policy on any of the tables reads `WITH CHECK (true)`
  (catalogue lint over `pg_policy`), and every erasure `UPDATE`/`DELETE` policy has a same-`USING` `SELECT`
  companion for `pennilogic_keyops` (catalogue lint).
- `erasure_deletes_only_own_transparency_rows` [T-SEC-02, T-SEC-03] — after erasure, every `access_events` row
  whose `owner_id` was the subject is gone; every row on which the subject was the viewer and a counterparty the
  owner still exists, pseudonymised with `viewer_erased = true`; a planted job that attempts to delete a viewer row
  affects zero rows (the `DELETE` policy's `USING (owner_id = principal)`) and the harness fails on the attempt.
- `transparency_rebuild_reproduces_post_erasure_state` [T-SEC-03, T-SEC-02] — ADR-020 §13 item 12 run against
  this record's register: after an erasure, a full re-projection of `access_events` and `transparency.entry` from
  the trail yields tables row-for-row identical to the pre-rebuild tables — the erased subject's rows do not
  reappear and every pseudonymised `viewer_id` carries the registered value with `viewer_erased = true`.
- `access_events_projection_applies_register` [T-SEC-03] — a `sharing.read.*` event whose viewer or owner is
  erased *after* the event was committed but *before* the relay projected it is projected in its post-erasure form
  (skipped, or pseudonymised with `viewer_erased = true`); a planted raw identifier of an erased subject in either
  projection fails the suite; the projector keys on `erasure.started` without a later `erasure.cancelled`, so a
  backlog self-actor event of the subject between Phase 3 and Phase 6 is not projected, while a **cancelled**
  subject's backlog and new events, as owner and as viewer, project normally and any dead letter raised for them
  during the freeze is re-driven.
- `erasure_waits_for_projector_past_freeze_position` [T-SEC-02, T-SEC-03] — `freeze_position` recorded in
  `erasure.started` equals the trail head observed after the T-CON-04 online bound, not the relay cursor; with the
  relay artificially held behind it **and** a planted in-flight `sharing.read.*` event committed 15 s after the
  freeze under a pre-freeze context, the job does not enter Phase 3 or Phase 4, `erasure.waiting_for_projector_seconds`
  grows and pages at the profile bound; once the gap-safe cursor passes the position the job proceeds and the late
  event is already projected in post-erasure form; the interleaving "relay reads register → job appends entry and
  updates → relay inserts raw" is exercised with the relay held and asserted impossible — the projector either
  pseudonymises with the registered value or dead-letters, never inserts raw.
- `access_events_projection_fails_closed_without_register_entry` [T-SEC-03] — a planted `sharing.read.*` event
  whose viewer has an `erasure.started` entry but no `erasure.pseudonymise` entry for that grantor is dead-lettered
  and paged; no row is inserted, raw or otherwise; the relay mints nothing; **the dead letter is re-driven** once the
  entry exists and at the latest at `erasure.completed`, after which the grantor's log shows the pseudonymised
  access (`erasure_transparency_pseudonym_render` counts it), so the record is delayed, never lost.
- `restore_does_not_resurrect_cancelled_erasure` [T-SEC-07, T-SEC-02] — a subject freezes and then cancels; a
  backup taken between the two is restored after the cancellation window and any hold would have elapsed on the
  clock: replay re-applies `erasure.cancelled` before readiness, the request reads `cancelled`, the job does not run
  Phase 2 and every key of the subject remains `ACTIVE`; a second fixture restores a backup taken during a freeze
  that was **never** cancelled or shredded: the job is **held, not resumed**, readiness counts the request as
  outstanding until an operator re-verifies with the person, and Phase 2 runs only after that re-verification.
- `erasure_resume_reuses_registered_pseudonym` [T-SEC-02] — a context already in the register is never re-minted:
  a resumed or re-run job reads the entry and writes the same value; a second append for the same key is refused
  by the control-store writer and, if planted, ignored by every reader.
- `erasure_register_bounded_and_forward_only` [T-SEC-07, T-SEC-03, T-CMP-04] — the relay's and the restore path's
  register access is a forward lookup `(subject-or-digest, context_kind, context_id) → pseudonym` only (a bulk or
  reverse read by either identity is refused); a reverse lookup succeeds only for an ADR-020 audit role as a
  recorded `admin_recorded_access` read with a purpose code; the entry's raw `subject` is not replaced before
  T-PLT-02 retention has elapsed after the served partition's archival, and is replaced by `HMAC(K_register,
  subject)` no later than `erasure_register_retention_days`, after which the pseudonym value remains; **a
  pre-erasure backup taken while the served partition was live and restored after the entry's subject was
  replaced replays Phase 4 for that context by recomputing the digest from the trail's subject UUID and reproduces
  the pseudonymised rows; a raw `viewer_id` of the subject anywhere in the restored projection, or a dead-lettered
  event for that context, fails the suite**; a rebuild after the replacement still reproduces the pseudonymised
  rows.
- `erasure_resume_after_partial_failure` [T-SEC-02, T-SEC-07] — the job is killed at every phase boundary and at
  each of: (a) after the `erasure.pseudonymise` register write and before or during the `viewer_id` row `UPDATE`;
  (b) between groups in Phase 4; (c) after key destruction and before its journal write; (d) between the barrier
  write and `erasure.completed`; it is resumed from its progress rows and the end state is byte-identical to an
  uninterrupted run: exactly one `erasure.pseudonymise` entry per (subject, context) and no second pseudonym value
  anywhere; key-destruction and `grant.revoked` journal entries not duplicated;
  `erasure.counterparty_rows_total{table, action}` equal to the uninterrupted run; a resumed run mints nothing for
  a registered context; a restore taken inside any window replays to the same end state.
- `erasure_orphaned_group_key_destroyed` and `erasure_live_group_key_untouched` [T-SPL-03, T-SEC-06] — a group
  whose last identified member is erased has its `group_keys` rows destroyed and journaled; a group with any
  surviving identified member keeps its key state and every canary decrypts.
- `erasure_users_tombstone_minimal` [T-SEC-02] — the surviving `users` row has `status = 'erased'`, `NULL`
  descriptive columns and `NULL` time zone; `begin_request` for it raises `account_erasing`.
- `erasure_job_unwraps_no_key` [T-SEC-02] — the custodian log and the API's unwrap counter show zero unwraps
  attributed to an erasure job across a full run; a planted decrypt call inside the job fails the suite.
- `erasure_not_startable_by_delegate` [T-SEC-02, T-CMP-01] — a `delegate:<client_id>` context cannot create an
  erasure request; a `user` context without step-up cannot either; an operator path that does not fulfil a
  T-ADM-13-verified rights request is refused; **a single-operator start of Phase 2 is refused**.
- `erasure_operator_phase2_requires_confirmed_approval` [T-SEC-02, T-ADM-13, T-SEC-03] — for an operator-started
  request, Phase 2 does not run without a chain-scoped approval event by a distinct T-ADM-06-eligible approver
  bound to the rights-request identifier and confirmed by the chain verifier; the requester, the originator and
  an unconfirmed approval each fail; a subject-started request needs none.
- `erasure_cancellation_reachable_during_freeze` [T-CMP-01, T-TRU-02, T-AUTH-01] — with every session of the
  subject revoked and `begin_request` refusing the account, the subject completes `frozen → cancelled` through the
  step-up or channel-verified path as a `preauth` / restricted-`acr` context, at any point of the product window
  **and** of a non-zero statutory hold; the transition writes `erasure.cancelled` in the same unit of work (a planted
  journal failure leaves the request `frozen`); it is refused once Phase 2 has started; afterwards grants remain
  revoked and memberships ended and the copy says so; **the subject's new `sharing.read.*` events are projected
  normally as owner and as viewer, their events committed during the freeze are present, and any dead letter raised
  for them during the freeze is re-driven**; no customer context is minted for an `erasing` account at any point.
- `erasure_honours_statutory_hold` [T-CMP-04, T-SEC-02] — with `statutory_pre_shred_retention_days` set to a
  non-zero synthetic value, Phase 2 does not run before the hold elapses; during the hold the subject cannot log
  in, no cross-user read in either direction is admitted, the data is readable only through ADR-020's
  `admin_recorded_access` with a Seventh-Schedule purpose code, the T-CON-09 `frozen` state carries the period; with
  the field `0` the hold is absent; with the field missing the request queues (`erasure_fails_closed_without_profile`).
- `erasure_orphaned_group_closed` [T-SPL-03] — an orphaned group refuses a join, a claim and an encrypted write,
  voids its pending invitations and never receives a new `group_keys` row.
- `erasure_invitations_cleared` [T-SPL-03] — invitations the subject sent are deleted and invitations addressed
  to the subject's channels are void; no member row ever held a contact value (catalogue lint).
- `erasure_auth_artefacts_purged` [T-AUTH-01, T-CMP-01] — after Phase 3 no `sessions`, `refresh_tokens`, `devices`,
  `recovery_requests`, `recovery_code_sets` or `step_up_assertions` row of the subject exists; a presented refresh
  token of the subject fails as unknown; the purge is restore-safe (ADR-019's `AuthArtefactRetentionTest` extended
  with the erasure case).
- `erasure_mode_c_destination_treatment` [T-CMP-01 with ADR-022's destination-registry ticket] — after Phase 3 the
  subject's Mode C destination rows carry no readable host, path prefix, credential-header choice or
  `pinned_addresses`, keep destination id and event types, and the egress-audit rows are intact (ADR-022 §2.8
  step 10).
- `erasure_trail_events_exact` [T-SEC-02, T-SEC-03, T-CMP-01] — exactly two trail events exist for the erasure
  and each is asserted **conjunctively**: the intake event has namespace `consent.*`, action
  `consent.rights.erasure.requested`, `actor_class = self`, `actor_principal_id = subject_id = subject`, inserter
  role `pennilogic_app`; the completion event has namespace `consent.*`, action `consent.rights.erasure.completed`,
  `actor_class = system`, `actor_principal_id` null, `subject_id = subject`, inserter role `pennilogic_app`, payload
  carrying the receipt identifier and the two policy versions and passing the schema negative fixture for
  personal-data fields; an operator fulfilment additionally has the `admin.*` `account_action` and approval events
  by `admin_api_owner`; `pennilogic_keyops` holds no `INSERT` on `audit.event` (ADR-020 §4.1) and a planted insert
  attempt by the job fails; any other inserter, namespace, actor class or principal for either event fails.
- `erasure_grant_rows_deleted_after_journal` [T-SEC-02] — every grant naming the subject has a `grant.revoked`
  journal entry with reason `erasure` and no live row; deletion without the journal entry is impossible (the
  test injects a journal failure and asserts the rows remain, revoked).
- **T-SEC-02's listed tests, named and given assertions here** (`PenniLogic/api#38` lists them descriptively —
  "Idempotent re-erasure test", "In-flight job fail-closed test", "Residue scan across derived stores",
  "Barrier-write failure and signed-receipt verification tests" — and this record extends that ticket with the
  identifiers and assertions below) [T-SEC-02]:
  `erasure_idempotent` — a second request for a `status = 'erased'` subject performs zero row writes, appends
  zero journal entries, leaves `erasure.requests_total{state="complete"}` unchanged and returns the existing
  receipt identifier; `erasure_in_flight_job_fails_closed` — a queued job (export, notification, aggregate
  refresh, assistant turn) that references the subject and runs after Phase 2 terminates with a fail-closed
  outcome, writes no row naming the subject and re-creates no derived data, counted in
  `erasure.residue_scan_findings_total` if it wrote anything; `erasure_residue_scan_derived_stores` — the Phase 5
  scan over caches, indexes, queues, embeddings, conversation stores and both transparency projections finds zero
  rows or keys naming the subject, and a planted residue in each store is found and blocks completion;
  `erasure_barrier_write_failure_blocks_completion` — with the control-store write failing, `users.status` stays
  `erasing`, no `erasure.completed` entry exists, the completion trail event is not written, T-CON-09 reports
  `completing` and never `complete`, and the receipt verifies against the barrier's signature once the write
  succeeds.
- `erasure_processor_propagation_inventory` [T-CMP-01, T-CMP-03] — every processor in the inventory that could
  hold the subject's data (custodian logs, push, messaging, AI providers) either holds only identifiers or has a
  recorded erasure or retention-mode evidence; a shared row holds no processor copy.

**Erasure is non-destructive to others**

- `erasure_counterparty_balances_unchanged` [T-SPL-03, T-QA-01] — for every group the subject belonged to and
  every currency: each surviving seat's net balance under the §3.3 formula and sign convention, the placeholder's
  net balance, `SUM(share_minor) = amount_minor` for every expense, and the debt-simplification proposal with its
  derivation are computed before and after erasure and are equal; a planted change of one minor unit fails; the
  §3.3 worked example is a fixed vector (`net(a) = 80000`, `net(b) = 0`, `net(c) = −80000` before and after), and a
  planted sign inversion of the settlement terms (yielding `net(b) = −160000`) fails. Runs against the
  independent money model of the test strategy's `debt_maths_independent_model` category.
- `erasure_settlement_against_placeholder` [T-SPL-03] — a counterparty records a settlement naming the
  placeholder seat after erasure; it is accepted **only under T-SPL-03's authorization matrix and dispute path,
  exactly as a settlement against a never-claimed placeholder** (an unconditional accept fails the test);
  balances re-derive correctly, and no payment deep link or contact is produced.
- `erasure_placeholder_unclaimable` [T-SPL-01, T-SPL-03] — an identity claim or merge against a seat with
  `erased_at` is refused; a returning person joining the same group receives a new seat with no link to the old
  one.
- `erasure_joint_goal_reconciles_partial` [T-FAM-05] — after a contributor's erasure the goal total equals the sum
  of surviving admitted `goal_contributions` rows per currency, is reported partial with reason `member_erased`,
  carries no identity of the erased member, the erased contributor's read-model rows are gone, **no `transactions`
  or `entries` row changed** (byte-identical before and after) and no copy or snapshot of their amounts exists in
  any counterparty's scope; after the owner's erasure each contributor's read-model rows carry a null goal
  reference with `goal_erased_at` and their entries are byte-identical.
- `erasure_household_aggregate_partial` [T-FAM-05] — the household aggregate after a member's erasure behaves as
  after revocation (`member_erased` completeness) and reveals no identifier.
- `erasure_transparency_pseudonym_render` [T-TRU-04, T-FAM-02] — a grantor's access log shows the former viewer
  under the pseudonym as "an erased account" with the category, level, policy identifier and instant unchanged;
  the row is not resolvable through any grant; the grantor cannot delete it; the erased viewer's own log is gone.
- `erasure_no_counterparty_notification` [T-NOT-01, T-FAM-02] — no outbox row is created for any counterparty by
  the erasure job.

**Revocation removes the ability to decrypt**

- `revoked_grantee_cannot_decrypt` [T-FAM-02] — the cryptographic proof T-FAM-02 requires, in three parts:
  (1) *no material*: the key inventory has no row addressed to the grantee, the T-CON-04 schema has no member
  that can carry key material or ciphertext and the T-QA-09 capture of a full detail read contains none; (2) *no
  admission*: after revocation the pre-revocation request is re-issued and denied with `grant_not_active`; a
  transaction under the grantee's verified context reads zero rows from the grantor's `user_keys`, zero rows
  from the grantor's `transactions` and `entries` and zero rows from `period_aggregates`; the production decrypt
  routine, handed the grantor's pre-revocation ciphertext bytes inside the grantee's context, fails with
  `key_not_admitted`; (3) *no resurrection*: after a restore of a pre-revocation backup and journal replay, the
  grant row is revoked and (2) holds again.
- `key_cache_never_admits` [T-SEC-01, T-FAM-02] — with the grantor's unwrapped DEK planted in the process cache,
  a transaction under a revoked or never-granted context cannot use it; `crypto.key_admission_refused_total`
  increments and pages.
- `no_grantee_bound_key_material_exists` [T-CON-04, T-CON-02, T-CON-09, T-QA-09] — schema negative fixtures with
  planted `key`, `wrapped_key`, `dek`, `ciphertext`, `nonce`, `key_id` and `*_bidx` members fail contract
  verification; the export archive and the traffic capture contain none.
- `restore_does_not_resurrect_revoked_grant` and `restore_does_not_resurrect_membership` [T-SEC-07] — a backup
  older than a revocation or a membership end is restored; replay re-applies `grant.revoked` / `membership.ended`
  before readiness; the grantee and the former member are denied.
- `membership_end_cannot_decrypt` [T-SPL-03] — after `left_at` the former member's context reads zero group rows
  of any date and zero `group_keys` rows; the group key state is unchanged (no rotation was triggered).
- `aggregate_grant_reads_no_key` [T-SEC-01] — an aggregate-only grant never causes a `user_keys` read or a
  custodian unwrap (ADR-018's `rls_grant_key_read_is_dek_only`, asserted from the custodian log side too).
- `safe_exit_leaves_no_server_copy` [T-FAM-02] — after safe exit a scan finds no row in any table that holds
  another user's descriptive data under the exiting user's scope; the assertion is structural (no such table
  exists) and is re-run whenever the inventory version changes.
- `erasure_freeze_ends_all_cross_user_reads` [T-SEC-02, T-FAM-02] — from Phase 1, every counterparty's read of
  the subject and the subject's read of every counterparty is denied, before any key is destroyed.

**Group key**

- `group_key_member_only` [T-SEC-01, T-SPL-03] — a non-member, a placeholder, a left member and a `preauth` or
  administrative context read zero `group_keys` rows; a member reads the `ACTIVE` and `DECRYPT_ONLY` rows of
  their groups only.
- `group_rows_use_group_scope_header` [T-SEC-01] — a catalogue lint: every ciphertext in the five group columns
  carries `key_scope = group` and a `key_id` present in `group_keys` for that `group_id`; a ciphertext moved

- `group_key_missing_fails_closed` [T-SPL-01] — a group with no `ACTIVE` key refuses an encrypted write with
  `crypto.group_key_missing`; plaintext is never written.
- `group_key_rotation_resumable` and `group_key_retirement_rules` [T-SEC-06, T-SEC-07] — ADR-018 §12's

- `group_keyops_context_scoped` [T-SEC-01] — `begin_group_keyops` for group `G` admits no row of any other
  group and no owner-scoped row; a `user` actor cannot execute it.

**Retention and profile**

- `erasure_fails_closed_without_profile` [T-CMP-04, T-SEC-02] — with any field of §5.4 missing or expired,

- `no_unsourced_retention_rule_cited` [T-CMP-04] — the compliance reconciliation check of `compliance` §0 fails
  when any ticket, contract or profile cites a removed rule of §5.3 as a retention period.

### 8. Observability

Signals carry counts, durations, phase names, policy identifiers and key identifiers — never a subject or
counterparty identifier, a pseudonym value, an amount, a description or key material.

- `erasure.requests_total{state}` (`queued`, `frozen`, `cancelled`, `shredding`, `completing`, `complete`,
  `failed`), `erasure.phase_duration_seconds{phase}`, `erasure.queue_age_seconds` (a request older than the
  profile's response ceiling minus a safety margin pages), `erasure.hold_active_total` (requests inside a
  non-zero statutory hold), `erasure.waiting_for_projector_seconds` (Phase 3/4 waiting for the relay to pass
  `freeze_position`; pages at the profile bound), `erasure.operator_phase2_refused_total` (a Phase 2 attempt
  without a confirmed approval; must be zero and pages), `erasure.held_after_restore_total` (started-without-
  completed requests held for re-verification after a restore), `transparency.dead_letter_total{reason}` and
  `transparency.dead_letter_redriven_total` (a dead letter older than the profile's rights clock pages).
- `erasure.counterparty_rows_total{table, action}` with `action ∈ {placeholder, redacted, pseudonymised,
  goal_unlinked, deleted}` — volume only.
- `erasure.group_keys_destroyed_total`, `erasure.orphaned_groups_total`.
- `erasure.residue_scan_findings_total{store}` — **must be zero**; any non-zero value pages and blocks the
  request's completion.
- `erasure.barrier_write_failures_total`, `erasure.journal_write_failures_total` — a failure leaves the request
  incomplete and pages.
- `erasure.replay_outstanding{restore_id}` — outstanding erasure and revocation replays during a restore;
  readiness requires zero (T-SEC-07).
- `sharing.revocations_total{kind, reason}` (`grant` / `membership`; `party`, `safe_exit`, `erasure`, `expiry`),
  `sharing.revocation_to_first_denial_seconds` (measured against the T-CON-04 online bound; a breach pages).
- `sharing.decrypt_after_revocation_attempts_total` — a decrypt routine invoked inside a context whose grant
  set does not admit the key; **must be zero** and pages.
- `crypto.key_admission_refused_total` — a cached key presented without a same-transaction admission; must be
  zero and pages. `crypto.group_key_missing_total` — a write refused for want of an `ACTIVE` group key.
- `crypto.cross_user_key_use_total` and `crypto.dek_unwrap_total{result}` (ADR-018 §11) gain the label
  `scope ∈ {user, group}`.
- Release evidence: the harness report of §7 per release candidate, consumed by the private-beta gate
  (T-REL-02) under the test strategy's `disaster_recovery_restore` and `security_negative_tests` categories.

### 9. Security and privacy consequences

| Choice | Consequence of the chosen option | What the rejected option would have cost |
|---|---|---|
| Server-mediated decryption, no grantee-bound material | The grantee's exposure is bounded by the predicate and by what the grantee lawfully saw; revocation needs no key operation; erasure has exactly one wrapped copy per key to destroy. Residual trust: the API process, which holds unwrapped keys in memory for up to five minutes and must be compromised to obtain a key at all (ADR-018 §9). | Re-wrap per grant: N copies per DEK to rotate, revoke and — fatally — to find on erasure; whole-DEK rotation per revocation; no protection against any adversary the single-KEK design does not already stop. |
| Key-cache admission rule | A stale in-memory key cannot outlive a revocation inside a later transaction; a bug that skipped the setter still finds no key row. | Relying on the five-minute TTL alone: a revoked grantee's request served within the TTL by a buggy path would find a usable key. |
| Group-scoped DEK for split rows | A member's request unwraps a key that protects five columns of one group; a member's erasure never destroys what others need; the group's rows are re-keyed per group. Cost: the erased member's identifiers in group rows are overwritten, not shredded, with the same completeness bound (§4.2). | Author DEK with rewrap: every member's request holds every author's whole personal DEK; erasure must destroy one copy per group or leave the author's whole account recoverable. |
| Per-context random pseudonym, recorded in the erasure register | No key can recompute it from a tombstone; the counterparty, the surface and the application role cannot link contexts; the register makes it stable across restore replay and the erasure-aware rebuild ADR-020 requires. Cost: the register (T-SEC-07 control store) can link a pseudonym to the erased UUID — the same capability the trail already gives ADR-020's audit roles. | An un-journaled random value: cannot satisfy ADR-020 §8.4's row-for-row rebuild and changes across a restore. A keyed, deterministic pseudonym: recomputable by anyone holding the key and the subject's UUID, which the tombstone row still exposes, and re-keyed on rotation. |
| Redact the subject's authored text; keep counterparties' text | Data minimisation for the erased party without rewriting another person's record; enumerable by the author seat columns fixed at insert. Residue: counterparty text may name the subject. | Redacting all text in a group: destroys counterparties' own records; searching text for names: impossible on ciphertext and unreliable. |
| Delete grant rows on erasure | No live row joins the erased UUID to a counterparty; the journal keeps the identifier-only tombstone. Cost: one exception to the append-only grant model, confined to the erasure job. | Keeping revoked rows: a permanent join between the erased person's ledger identifier and an identified counterparty in every dump. |
| No snapshot of an erased contributor's joint-goal amounts | The household dashboard cannot become a record of a partner who left; consistent with the coercive-control review of T-FAM-03. Cost: the goal total visibly drops and must be labelled partial. | Retaining the amounts: no legal basis (§5.2) and a coercion surface. |
| Freeze before shred, with a cancellation path, a statutory hold and an approver gate | The first, reversible phase already ends every cross-user read in both directions, so a request made under duress or by mistake can be cancelled inside the profile window through the step-up/channel-verified path without any key loss; a profile that adopts Rule 8(3) is honoured by a value, not a superseding record; an operator cannot shred an account alone. | Shredding first: irreversible before the person has a chance to cancel; severing later leaves relationship residue while the job runs; a single operator or one admin-API bug able to perform the one irreversible action in the system. |
| No counterparty notification; erased states explicit on the wire | No push and no coercion signal; the counterparty's own record is not misrepresented — `member_state = erased`, `redacted_by_erasure`, `viewer: {erased: true}` and `member_erased` are visible on the next read. Cost: the counterparty can tell that the person erased rather than left (§4.2). | An "account deleted" message: reveals the act and its timing to a possibly abusive counterparty. Collapsing the erased states into `left` / `absent`: hides from the counterparty why their own description or total changed. |
| Pseudonym register bounded and forward-only, with a restorable digest key | The re-identification capability the register creates is limited to the trail's life plus the backup window and to ADR-020's audit roles through a recorded read; the relay and the restore path can only look forward, and can still do so after the raw subject is replaced by `HMAC(K_register, subject)` because they hold the trail's UUID. Cost: profile field, archival hook, one more custodian-held platform key. | An unbounded register readable in bulk: a permanent re-identification key outside ADR-020's controls. A drop at archival with no surviving key: a pre-archival backup restored inside T-PLT-02 retention could not be replayed for that context. |
| Cancellation journaled; held-not-resumed after restore | A person who cancels cannot have the cancellation undone by a restore, and a restored in-progress freeze never proceeds to the irreversible phase without a human re-verifying with the person; a cancelling subject keeps their access transparency because the projector keys on the freeze *without* a later cancellation. Cost: one journal event, one readiness rule, one re-verification step. | Cancellation only in database rows: a restore from a backup taken between freeze and cancellation resurrects the request and the resumable job shreds a live user; a projector keyed on the freeze alone silences a cancelling subject's transparency log forever. |

### 10. Options considered and rejected

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Grant-scoped re-wrap of the grantor's DEK to a grantee-addressed wrapping key, with revocation = delete the copy + rotate the grantor's DEK | Same KEK, same custodian, so no adversary is stopped that the predicate path does not stop; multiplies the copies erasure must destroy; forces a whole-history re-encryption per revocation whose old key stays `DECRYPT_ONLY` for the backup window anyway (§1.2). |
| A shared key per family-sharing relationship | Requires a second ciphertext of the grantor's own rows or a grantor key the grantor does not solely own; doubles erasure surface; contradicts ADR-018's one `ACTIVE` DEK per user. |
| Client-side (end-to-end) decryption with keys held by grantees | The only design in which revocation is a true key operation, and the design ADR-018 §13 rejected for web access, server-side matching and grant-mediated reads; not reopened here. |
| Proxy re-encryption schemes (re-encryption keys held by the server) | A third key class with its own lifecycle whose only benefit — the server never sees plaintext — the product does not have anyway, because the server matches, aggregates and renders. |
| Author's DEK with per-group rewrap for split rows | Exposes every author's whole personal DEK to every member's request; erasure must find one copy per group (§1.4). |
| One ciphertext copy per member for split rows | N-fold storage and writes, fan-out inconsistency, and N copies of the share-sum invariant (§1.4). |
| Rotate the group DEK on every membership end | The former member never held the key; rotation is cost without a confidentiality gain and would suggest to reviewers that the key, not the predicate, is the revocation mechanism. Rotation stays scheduled and on compromise. |
| Rotate the grantor's DEK on every grant revocation | As above for the personal DEK, at the cost of re-encrypting the grantor's whole history. |
| Destroy the group DEK when any member is erased | Destroys every other member's descriptive data and the collective record; contradicts ADR-006's stated resolution. |
| Keep `user_id` on the erased member's seat, or replace it with a deterministic pseudonym | A live join from the erased ledger identifier to identified counterparties, or a recomputable one (§3.5, §3.6). |
| An un-journaled random pseudonym | Cannot be reproduced by ADR-020 §8.4 item 5's erasure-aware rebuild or by a T-SEC-07 replay, so a rebuild would either resurrect the raw identifier or diverge from the pre-rebuild table, which ADR-020 §13 item 12 forbids (§3.5). |
| Keep a snapshot of an erased contributor's joint-goal contributions for the goal owner | No claim exists over a contributor's savings; a coercion surface (§3.4, §5.2). |
| Physically delete the subject's split shares and expenses | Corrupts every counterparty's balance and destroys their evidence of a claim (R-2, R-3); the exact failure E26-F17 names. |
| Inactivity-based automatic erasure of shared data (Rule 8 pattern) | PenniLogic is not in a Third Schedule class on the research reading; an automatic erasure of one party's seat would corrupt counterparties' balances (R-6). |
| A fixed retention period for user ledger data ("7 years", "5 years") | No applicable source (§5.3); a period the profile may later add with a citation is consumed at runtime, not fixed here. |
| Notify counterparties of an erasure | A coercion signal (T-FAM-02, T-FAM-03). |
